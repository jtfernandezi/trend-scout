# Watchlist

Where to look each run. The method is **proven pattern, narrowed buyer**, so
lead with *traction* sources — startups visibly winning right now — not trend
commentary. A company with no hard traction evidence is not a pattern.

## Traction signals (lead here)
- Funding announcements, last 1-2 weeks: TechCrunch, Axios Pro Rata, Term Sheet,
  Strictly VC. Look for unusually fast rounds, big seeds, and back-to-back raises
- Crunchbase / PitchBook summaries — recent rounds by stage and category
- **Y Combinator**: current batch directory, Demo Day coverage, and the current
  Requests for Startups. Batch breakouts are the highest-density source here
- a16z / Sequoia / Bessemer / Greylock portfolio announcements — what they just
  funded, not just what they say they want
- Hiring signals: startups posting many sales / forward-deployed / solutions
  roles at once — the tell that something is actually selling
- Disclosed milestones: ARR, customer counts, usage numbers in launch posts
- **Category clusters** — several startups funded for the same job in a short
  window. The strongest pattern evidence available; the market is confirming
  the job is real

## Where the mechanism comes from
- New model/lab releases and capability jumps (Anthropic, OpenAI, Google
  DeepMind, Meta, Mistral) — what became newly *possible* this month, and which
  breakouts are built on it
- Hacker News front page + Show HN (last 7 days) — what builders are shipping
- Product Hunt top launches (last 7 days)
- AI infra and agents: orchestration, evals, inference, memory, agent
  security/governance — the picks-and-shovels layer

## High-impact domains to mine (all in scope)
- **Applied AI in big verticals** — healthcare, legal, financial services,
  defense/gov, insurance, biotech, science. Richest source of segments a leader
  can't serve, because compliance and data models differ sharply by segment
- **AI infra & agents**
- **Frontier / deep tech** — robotics, bio×AI, energy, hardware, materials
- **Consumer & prosumer AI**

## Finding the segment the leader can't serve
- The leader's own pricing page and docs — what the floor price and minimum
  contract exclude, which integrations and regions are missing
- Their careers page — which markets they're staffing for (and which they aren't)
- Reviews and complaints: G2, Reddit, industry forums — who says "we tried it,
  it doesn't fit us," and why. The most direct evidence of a structural misfit
- Regulatory and certification regimes that would force a separate product
- Non-US markets with no localization, no local integrations, no local
  compliance work

## Verification sources (for the adversarial wedge check)
- YC company directory (ycombinator.com/companies) — search the segment, not
  just the category
- Crunchbase / "[segment] [workflow] startup" + recent funding searches
- Product Hunt / GitHub search for the specific capability or workflow
- The leader's changelog and release notes — if they're already shipping toward
  the segment, the config-change test has answered itself
