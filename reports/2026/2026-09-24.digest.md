*Trend Scout — 2026-09-24*

*0 survivors, 12 kills (plus 2 older ideas retired).*

No idea made it through this time, and that's an honest result, not a failed run. The companies
winning this week are clearly winning:
- *Angle Health* raised 600M dollars to sell health plans to tiny businesses.
- *Thatch* became a unicorn giving employees money to buy their own health cover.
- *Harvey*, the legal AI company, passed 400M dollars a year in revenue.
- *Comp AI* grew 15x helping startups pass security audits.
- *Buildots* and *Adaptive* raised for AI on construction sites.

But every gap I found next to them was already filled, frozen, or copyable.

*The main lesson.* The usual trick is to serve the customer a fast-growing company can't serve without
upsetting its own customers: the auditor for the compliance company, the lender for the builder's
progress tool, the subcontractor for the builder's accounting software. I tried that four times, and
each time someone was already there, usually founded years before the winner got big. The table has two
sides, and both sides get their startup early.

*Also retired.* The robot safety-certification idea we'd been watching since June is dead. A company
called AIUC just raised 40M dollars to certify AI systems and insure them through Lloyd's of London, and
extending that to robots is an easy step for them. The AI-failure catastrophe model for insurers is dead
too: the big reinsurance brokers are building it themselves.

*Next run* looks for government approvals that turn into assets someone keeps paying for, like the
aviation-certificate idea from the last run. Most approvals get used up once, like an audit report or a
permit sign-off.
