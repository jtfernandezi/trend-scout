# Trend Scout — Report 2026-09-08

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (thirty-seventh run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).
**Outcome:** **1 surviving idea (composite 4.2, one brief), 11 logged kills, 1 held-book entry updated
with new evidence.**

The 2026-09-05 report left three instructions. Two were followed and one was tested and found wanting,
which is the methodological result of this run.

The instruction that worked: *stop hunting for empty segments and start from mechanisms the ledger has
never touched.* The scan found one — **machine-conducted human inquiry** — and it is the strongest
category cluster available this week. Two companies raised for the identical job inside five weeks:
Simile at a $2B valuation with revenue up fivefold, and Conveo at a $50M Series A with 400+ enterprises
and 50+ Fortune 500 companies on the platform. Across 335 prior ledger entries the word "qualitative"
appears zero times and "interview" twice. That mechanism produced six of this run's twelve candidates
and the only survivor.

The instruction that did not work: *lead with statutes that took effect in the last twelve months and
have visibly failed to be implemented.* Four statutory triggers were worked properly — the FinCEN
residential real estate rule, the CMS RADV audit expansion, the Florida condominium milestone and
reserve-study deadline, and FDA's QMSR inspection programme. **None produced a survivor and two were
dead before the wedge check.** The FinCEN rule was vacated nationwide by a federal court on 2026-03-19,
five months before this run looked at it. The RADV extrapolation provisions, which supplied the entire
burning-platform economics, were vacated in September 2025 and are on appeal. The honest lesson is that
**a dated statutory trigger is not durable ground: rules with real money attached get litigated, and a
company whose why-now is a rule is a company whose why-now can be enjoined.** The two survivors that
came from statutory triggers on 2026-09-03 and 2026-09-05 (ts-0307, ts-0324) both sit behind
*professional standards* and *state licensure*, not behind a contested federal rulemaking — that is the
distinction the next run should carry, rather than "statutes" as a category.

**The kill distribution.** Ten of eleven kills came from the occupancy test, one from the venture-scale
gate; one of the ten was joined by the config-change test. In nine of the ten the occupant was funded
and shipping the identical product. That is the fourth consecutive run with this shape and it is no
longer news: **almost every re-point of a proven mechanism is already occupied.** What is new is
*where* the occupancy sat. Six candidates came off the same fresh interviewing mechanism, and five of
the six died — not because interviewing is a bad mechanism, but because the *commercially* valuable
narrowings (mass tort, worker voice, education, safety) were reached first by people who did not need a
regulated posture. The one that lived is the one where the regulated posture is the entire point.

## Surviving idea

### 1. Regulatory-grade patient interviewing — the AI-native clinical research organisation whose product is the interview

**In plain English.** When a drug company wants a regulator to accept a new way of measuring whether a
medicine helped — "patients could climb stairs again", "the itching stopped" — it has to prove that the
measurement means what it claims. The only way to prove that is to sit down with patients, one at a
time, for an hour, and ask careful open questions about their experience. Trained interviewers do this
today at a few hundred dollars an hour, which means a study talks to twenty people and takes three
months, and if the patients speak six languages or have a disease with four hundred sufferers worldwide
it often does not happen at all. This company does those interviews with a machine instead — properly,
under the same medical-research rules, with consent forms, audit trails and a real duty to report it
immediately if a patient mentions a side effect. Drug companies pay because the evidence is a
requirement they already budget for, and because for the first time they can talk to every patient in
the study instead of twenty of them.

**The proven pattern.** Simile (>$200M Series B at $2B, revenue up 5x, CVS/Deloitte/Gallup) and Conveo
($50M Series A on 2026-09-02, 400+ enterprises, 50+ Fortune 500) raised five weeks apart for the same
job — machine-conducted human inquiry at enterprise scale.

**Who buys the narrowed version.** The patient-centred outcomes or clinical outcome assessment lead at a
mid-cap biotech or specialty CRO, in rare disease or oncology, at the moment a regulator meeting or a
protocol going final requires content-validity evidence for the endpoint they intend to use — or an EU
Joint Clinical Assessment dossier requires patient input they have not collected.

**Why the leader can't follow.** Serving this segment means becoming a GCP-regulated clinical service
provider: IRB approval and informed consent, 21 CFR Part 11 validated records, pharmacovigilance intake
against a 24-hour adverse-event clock, participants recruited through investigator sites rather than
consumer panels, and standing behind the data in an FDA inspection. That is a second company with a
second regulatory identity. And the actual leaders in the narrowed market — IQVIA Patient Centered
Solutions, PPD, RTI-HS, Adelphi Values, Clinigma — sell the interviewer *hour*, so automating it deletes
their billable unit; they have had capable models for eighteen months and shipped nothing.

**Expansion path.** Rare-disease and oncology in-trial, exit and COA-development interviews (a few
hundred million dollars of services, and the place the method gets accepted by reviewers) → the
qualitative arm of record inside the trial, sitting beside eCOA rather than beneath it ($2.5B+, mid-teens
growth, held by Signant, Clario and Medidata) → post-market and real-world evidence → EU Joint Clinical
Assessment and payer dossiers, where patient input is now a mandatory input to a pricing decision across
27 countries and widens to all new medicines by 2030 → the compounding asset is a cross-indication corpus
of coded patient experience linked to outcomes: the concept libraries, saturation curves and
meaningful-change thresholds every future endpoint gets argued against. That is a standards position,
not a vendor position.

**Scores** — Wedge 4 · Pattern 5 · Market 4 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 4 →
composite **4.2**

**Biggest risk.** The field currently asserts that this cannot be done: 2026 write-ups of AI-moderated
interviewing state plainly that IRB-governed clinical studies and vulnerable populations *require human
moderation*. That belief is the wedge and it is also the objection you will hear in every first meeting,
and it will not be settled by a demo — it will be settled by the first regulator who accepts a submission
containing machine-conducted interviews. Until then the honest bear case is a services business waiting
on a precedent it does not control.

## Method note for the next run

**One. "Find a recent statute with an implementation gap" is too coarse an instruction, and this run is
the evidence.** Four statutory triggers were worked and none survived; two were already dead in court
before the run began — FinCEN's residential real estate rule vacated nationwide on 2026-03-19, and RADV
extrapolation vacated in September 2025 and on appeal. Refine the instruction: **prefer triggers that sit
behind a professional standard, a state licensure regime or a certification body over triggers that sit
behind a contested federal rulemaking.** Standards and licences are administered by bodies with no
opposing litigant; federal rules with billions attached have one, and they lose. Before developing any
statute-triggered candidate, spend one search on *whether the rule is still in force*.

**Two. Work the interviewing mechanism's remaining regulated corners, and only the regulated ones.** Six
narrowings were tested and the pattern in the results is sharp: every commercially obvious narrowing was
occupied (mass tort, worker voice, education, safety, complaint handling), and the only survivor was the
one where a regulated posture is the product rather than a tax. Remaining untested corners worth one
search each: independent medical examinations in workers' compensation, structured diagnostic
interviewing outside trials, life insurance tele-underwriting, and immigration or benefits determination
where the buyer is not a government. Do not test another consumer-facing narrowing; that half of the
market is finished.

**Three. Watch for the concentration risk that did not bite this time but nearly did.** Six of twelve
candidates came off one mechanism. That is efficient when the mechanism is fresh and dangerous when it
is not — the 2026-09-03 report flagged the same thing about the AI-native firm shape. Next run should
open on a mechanism from outside the human-inquiry family, and the two best-evidenced unmined candidates
in this scan are **self-pay diagnostics** (Scan.com at a $165M run rate doubling year on year, Function
Health at $450M) and **the deployment-labour constraint** (AWS and Microsoft committing $3.5B to embedded
engineers against a 95% pilot failure rate). The labour version died here as a marketplace; it has not
been tested as a product.
