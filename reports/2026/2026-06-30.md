**Run type:** scheduled research pass (ninth run under the venture-scale-gated thesis). Acting on the 2026-06-27 takeaway — *keep leading with the portfolio/systemic-square rule (enter a hot shift, map who owns each per-unit square, take the correlated/systemic square they skipped); treat a severe labor shortage as an anti-signal; and re-verify every regulatory trigger is live.*
**Outcome:** **1 surviving idea, 7 logged kills.** This was a hard, high-volume run — I developed ~9 candidate crosses against the freshest shifts of the fortnight (agentic-commerce payment rails going live with Visa+Mastercard+OpenAI+Google on June 10; the Medicare **ACCESS** model paying Original Medicare on outcomes for AI chronic care, live **July 5**; the new AI-augmented CPT codes; the electrical-equipment supply-chain crisis; agent identity/observability; semiconductors; space). Almost every obvious square was saturated or platform-owned, and two of the hottest triggers (ACCESS, agentic commerce) are textbook "everyone reads the same headline at once" — CMS literally pre-selected 150 delivery participants. ## Surviving idea

### ts-0069 — The correlated "time-to-power" completion-realism engine for the data-center buildout (the "RMS for whether — and *when* — AI's data centers actually get built")

**In plain English.** Building a giant AI data center now hinges on one boring thing: getting the heavy electrical gear — the building-sized transformers and switchgear that connect it to the grid. Those parts are back-ordered **three to four years**, and almost all of them come from the *same handful of factories*. Insurers have started selling "we'll pay you if your data center opens late" coverage, and banks are lending billions against projects opening on time — but they price each project as if its delay is its own private bad luck. It isn't: if one transformer factory has a fire, a strike, or a tariff shock, **hundreds of data centers slip at the same moment**, and the insurer who sold that coverage to all of them gets hit with one enormous, simultaneous bill they never set money aside for. This company builds the missing brain: it traces which project depends on which factory, component, contractor and grid connection, then calculates how badly an insurer or lender would be hit if those shared chokepoints break together — so they can finally price "will it open on time" instead of guessing or refusing to cover it (today, billions of this risk is simply uninsured). Insurers, reinsurers and project lenders pay because getting this wrong is a balance-sheet-ending surprise — and the buildout they're underwriting is worth $1.4 trillion.

- **Who buys it.** *Beachhead:* the exposure-management / underwriting lead at a **delay-in-start-up (DSU) / builders-risk / parametric carrier or specialty MGA** writing AI data-center construction (and their reinsurers), who must price delay risk *now* — post-Aon ($3.5B program), Descartes and Zurich — and is sitting on correlated equipment-delay accumulation across the whole book. *Expansion:* completion lenders and data-center ABS/CMBS investors underwriting on-time energization; then **all large-project DSU/builders-risk** (renewables, LNG, fabs, transmission) sharing the same equipment/EPC chokepoints; finally the industry-standard "completion-realism / time-to-power" model every side prices against.
- **Why no one already serves them.** Per-project AI construction-delay tools (lender due-diligence vendors, construction-finance monitors) score one project independently for go/no-go — not correlated accumulation across a book. Nat-cat modelers (Verisk, Moody's RMS) have decades of *geographic* data and no equipment-supply-chain dependency graph. The parametric/DSU carriers pouring in capacity (Descartes, Aon, Zurich) price each policy on *assumed-independent* delay distributions — the exact blind spot. Supply-chain-risk mappers (Everstream, Interos) map supplier tiers but don't price insured delay-loss accumulation. And cedents structurally want a model *not* owned by their reinsurer — the same independence demand that sustains third-party catastrophe modelers.
- **Scores:** Novelty 4 · Why-now 5 · Market 4 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 4 → **composite 4.2**.
- **Biggest risk.** **The "this is just supply-chain delay prediction" skeptic, plus a narrower beachhead than ts-0064.** (1) Per-project AI delay forecasting *is* a marketed category for lenders — the defense is that the value is the **correlated-fates accumulation model over a shared equipment-dependency graph**, which independent per-project tools and assumed-independent actuarial pricing structurally cannot produce (the same line that made ts-0048/ts-0054/ts-0064 AI-native), and the moat is a proprietary cross-project equipment/EPC/queue dataset. (2) The first buyer set (data-center DSU/parametric carriers + their reinsurers) is specialized and smaller than ts-0064's utility/PUC universe — so the expansion story (all large-project completion risk + completion lenders) has to be real, not decorative; if the transformer crunch eases faster than expected the *overbuild-delay* fear deflates, though completion lenders need the same truth whichever way delivery breaks.

## Takeaway for next run

The portfolio/systemic-square rule has now produced the survivor **five runs running** (ts-0048 flows-not-marks; ts-0039 independent-not-self-run; ts-0054 accumulation-not-per-agent; ts-0064 demand-realism-not-capacity; ts-0069 completion-correlation-not-per-project-delay). It remains the most reliable generator in this ledger. Two refinements banked this run: (a) **a pre-staffed government program is an anti-signal as strong as a labor shortage** — ACCESS handed out 150 vendor slots before launch, so the hot trigger was already saturated by construction; check whether the buyer has pre-selected vendors before crossing a regulatory wave; and (b) **a one-day duopoly platform move (Visa+MC+OpenAI+Google) claims its own squares** — the rail/merchant/fraud layers accrue to the platforms, leaving only the systemic-accumulation square, which here collapsed into a thesis we already hold (ts-0054). One watch item: the **defense-autonomy T&E** lane (ts-0032) got a fresh tailwind — the DAWG budget request jumped from **$225.9M (FY26) to $54.6B (FY27)** and DoD published an autonomy-testing handbook — worth promoting to `watching` if a dated solicitation lands.

Next run: (1) keep watching the buildout — watch whether a parametric carrier (Descartes) or a CAT modeler (Verisk/RMS) ships an *equipment-correlated* delay-accumulation product, which would dent ts-0069; (2) the world-model capital wave ($2B+ in Q1) is still un-cracked here — hunt the **data-locked vertical** the $1B generalists structurally won't take; and (3) pressure-test a genuinely *untouched* big shift (the reindustrialization capital flow, or the AI-driven labor-displacement spend) for the systemic square, not the occupied per-unit one.
