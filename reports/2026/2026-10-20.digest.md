*Trend Scout — 2026-10-20*

*0 survivors, 6 kills.* No idea cleared all three gates this run.

*What we tried.* This week's winners were:
- Greptile: AI that checks software changes before they go live; raised 50M dollars, and it checks over 1% of all code changes on GitHub.
- Town: an AI assistant that runs your inbox and calendar; worth 1bn dollars four months after launch.
- Chariot Claims: AI that gathers thousands of consumers into individual cases against big companies; raised 10M dollars.
- Pathways: AI that writes the environmental fact sheets building-material makers need; a 1M dollar contract with Heidelberg.
- Solvera: an AI-run engineering firm that designs building heating, wiring and plumbing; about 1M dollars of work in three months.

*What died, in plain English.*
- Helping companies defend against mass consumer arbitrations: only about 80 such waves a year, too few buyers.
- AI checking factory control code: Copia and PLC Copilot already do it, and Siemens' own tool reads those projects.
- AI-run independent reviewer of state government IT projects: the federal government stopped requiring that review for Medicaid upgrades.
- An inbox assistant for financial advisors: Jump already serves about one in ten US advisors.
- Automated environmental fact sheets for European building-material makers: One Click LCA already sells this.
- An AI-run engineering firm for US data centres: nothing stops existing firms or Solvera from doing it, and AI raises existing firms' margins.

*Lesson.* This week's big raises were general-purpose products that can reach almost any niche with a settings change. The only openings worth testing are buyers who sit on the opposite side from the leader's own customers.
