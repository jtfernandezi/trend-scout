# Trend Scout — Report 2026-10-24

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (fifty-seventh run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).
**Outcome:** **Zero surviving ideas. 6 new kills (ts-0541 to ts-0546).** No brief this run. No held-book entry
changes status.

This run followed the 2026-10-22 method note:

- **The skip list was respected.** ASI, Arda, Eka, Longeye, Daytona, Otter, Pigment, Baba, Town, Greptile,
  Tenstorrent, Blackwall and Flapping Airplanes repeated on this week's trackers and were not re-pointed.
- **"Who pays" was checked before the wedge (note two).** It decided nothing this run, because every flank with a
  real conflict (ts-0543, ts-0544) had a paying buyer and a seat that was already occupied.
- **Central-build check (note one).** The one public-sector flank, Medicaid work-requirement verification
  (ts-0542), is not a central build. But federal contractors are giving states the tooling at a discount or for
  free, which has the same effect.
- **Note three (AI-moderated interviews for strategy and PE diligence) was not worked.** No new interviewing
  breakout appeared this week. It stays open for a run with a fresh pattern.

**The finding worth carrying forward: the week's best-evidenced vertical breakouts (Legora, Tractian, Cognitive
Credit, Prism Data) are all in categories with a deep incumbent bench, and each one's channel-conflict flank was
already occupied by an older, non-AI-native incumbent that has bolted on AI.** Legal bill review belongs to Wolters
Kluwer, restructuring intelligence to Octus, and public-benefits verification to Equifax and the big systems
integrators. The one truly structural block this run, the US connected-vehicle rule that bars Neolix's Chinese
driving software, shuts out a foreign leader but leaves a domestic field that is already funded (Also and
DoorDash, Nuro, Serve, Coco). **New rule: a regulatory block that excludes a foreign leader is only an opening if
no domestic company has raised for the same job.** Check that before arguing the wedge.

## Breakout startups scanned

1. **Legora**: **550M dollar Series E at 8.8bn dollars led by Atreides, 2026-10-21** (Fidelity, Capital Group,
   J.P. Morgan, Baron). **ARR above 200M dollars**: 1M to 100M dollars in under 18 months, then the next 100M
   dollars in under six; more than 140,000 users at more than 2,400 firms and legal teams (company-reported).
   **Mechanism:** frontier models read, draft and reason over legal work product at junior-lawyer level.
2. **Tractian**: **130M dollar Series D at 1.4bn dollars led by Sapphire, co-led by M&G's Catalyst, 2026-10-22**.
   More than **2,000 facilities**; **ARR reported above 100M dollars** in September (The Information, not confirmed
   by the company). **Mechanism:** cheap wireless sensors plus models that diagnose machine failure modes and turn
   them into work orders.
3. **Cognitive Credit**: **40M dollar strategic growth round led by Summit Partners**, October 2026. Covers more
   than **3,500 issuers** and is used by **more than 80% of the 25 largest asset managers** (company-reported).
   **Mechanism:** AI credit research tied back to the source financial documents.
4. **Prism Data**: **20M dollar Series A led by Story Ventures**, October 2026. Used by **three of the top five
   US fintech lenders and six of the top ten digital banks**, with **API usage up more than 100x in two years**
   (company-reported). **Mechanism:** models turn raw bank transactions into a person's income and cash-flow
   picture for underwriting.
5. **Mach9**: **34M dollar Series A co-led by First Round and Bessemer, 2026-10-21**. About **100 customers,
   2,500+ projects in all 50 states**, more than 100,000 miles surveyed (company-reported). **Mechanism:** models
   extract features from LiDAR point clouds and draft civil CAD for a licensed professional to sign.
6. **Neolix**: **nearly 400M dollar Series E co-led by Boyu Capital, 2026-10-20**. More than **30,000 L4
   delivery vehicles in more than 300 cities and 20 countries** (company-reported). **Mechanism:** cheap
   purpose-built driverless vans, run at fleet scale, for last-mile goods.
7. **Blacksmith**: **66M dollar Series C at 1bn dollars led by Sequoia**, two months after its Series B. **ARR up
   17x since January** (tens of millions per Fortune), weekly CI jobs up from about 5M to more than 90M, and more
   than 10,000 customers. **Mechanism:** fast CI plus an agent that fixes failed checks, because validation is the
   new bottleneck when agents write the code. **Not re-pointed:** horizontal, and the safety-critical flank is
   already closed at ts-0033 and ts-0399.
8. **Formic**: **50M dollars of Series B equity plus debt**. Hundreds of robots at more than 100 facilities, 99.6%
   uptime, **97% renewal**. **Mechanism:** robots-as-a-service for factories. **Not re-pointed:** the deployment
   and integrator flanks are closed at ts-0198 and ts-0377.

## Method note for the next run

**One. Foreign-leader blocks need a domestic-field check first.** Before arguing a geography or national-security
wedge (connected-vehicle rule, ICTS, CFIUS, data-residency bans), search "[job] startup raises [country]" for the
market being closed. If a domestic company has raised for the same job in the past year, kill on occupancy.

**Two. Channel-conflict flanks of seat-selling leaders in mature professional markets (law, credit research)
have been occupied for years by information incumbents (Wolters Kluwer, Octus, Equifax).** Only test these if the
incumbent is not AI-native and bills in a way AI would cannibalise. In this run all three incumbents sell
subscriptions or a share of savings, so AI helps them.

**Three. Note three from 2026-10-22 is still open:** AI-moderated interviewing for corporate strategy, consulting
and PE diligence, against expert networks billed per call. Work it when a fresh interviewing pattern appears.

**Four. Skip for now:** everything on the 2026-10-13 to 2026-10-22 lists, plus Legora, Tractian, Cognitive
Credit, Prism Data, Mach9, Neolix, Blacksmith, Formic and Arena. Also skip legal bill review, liability-management
intelligence, Medicaid work-requirement verification, nuclear equipment health, DOT base mapping and US
autonomous delivery.
