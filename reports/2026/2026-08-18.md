# Trend Scout — Report 2026-08-18

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (twenty-ninth run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).

**Outcome:** **2 surviving ideas (composites 4.2 and 4.0, one brief), 17 logged kills.**

This run deliberately left the AI build-out. Twenty-eight runs have concentrated on power, equipment, defence and physical AI, and the ledger's duplicate-detection is now excellent there and thin everywhere else. So the scan went to five structural shocks the book has never touched: the collapse of the private long-term care insurance market, the permanent deletion of European chemical capacity, the SaaS repricing of January–February 2026, the majority-machine web, and the breakdown of the federal research grant machine.

The 2026-08-15 transaction pre-prompt — *does this reasoning let a market clear, or only a decision get informed?* — earned its keep immediately, and in the cleanest possible way. The single best candidate this run and the single most instructive kill are **the same object viewed from the two sides of that question**. Waterlily already predicts, for an individual older person, when they will need care, for how many hours, and what it will cost, against 500 million data points and 50,000 families. It sells that prediction to financial advisors and long-term care carriers: a model sold to an observer. The surviving idea (ts-0220) is the version that takes the risk instead — using the same class of underwriting to advance a family cash against their house so care gets bought now. One is a report. The other is a balance sheet. The pre-prompt is what separated them, and it should stay.

The sharpest pair of facts this run produced: **the private long-term care insurance market has fallen from more than a hundred writers at peak to roughly fifteen in 2026, while the home care workforce is projected to generate 6.1 million job openings between 2024 and 2034 — second among all US occupations — with 95% of home and community-based providers reporting staffing shortages and 77% turning away referrals.** Demand is unbounded, supply is wage-constrained, and the largest pool of money that could bid for that labour — roughly $14 trillion of home equity held by people over 65 — is locked behind a federal product that is *forbidden by design* from taking a person's health into account. A 72-year-old with a three-year care horizon and a 72-year-old with a twenty-year horizon get the identical reverse mortgage offer. That is not a market failing for lack of capital. It is a market that cannot price, and pricing it is now a frontier-model problem.

## Signals scanned

1. **Model releases are now a weekly cadence and frontier price collapsed (July–August 2026).** Google shipped **Gemini 3.7 Flash on 2026-08-13**, 23 days after 3.6 Flash and its third Flash release in under two months, with DeepSWE climbing 49.0% → 65.3%, FrontierCode 1.1 Main 34.4% → 43.6% and AutomationBench 17.0% → 30.4%. Qwen3.8-27B landed 2026-08-14. **GPT-5.6 Luna is now the cheapest closed frontier-class model at $0.20 / $1.20 per million tokens after an 80% cut on 2026-07-30**; Moonshot's Kimi K3 put 2.8T open weights out on 07-26. In 2024 there were three or four major releases a *year*; there are now that many a *month*. The commercial fact that matters is the price: reading a very large, very messy corpus per decision is no longer a budget item.

2. **The web became majority machine.** **Cloudflare Radar put bots at 57.5% of HTML traffic against humans at 42.5% in June 2026** — the first time automated requests held the majority — and **AI/LLM indexing bots quadrupled their share from 2.6% to 10.1% in eight months**. Akamai reports **63% of AI bot triggers target the publishing sector**. AI crawlers pull from the HTML/DOM without executing JavaScript, so they bypass Google Analytics entirely and the real share is probably understated.

3. **Publisher referral traffic is in structural collapse.** Search referrals down **60% for small publishers, 47% for mid-sized, 22% for large**. Semrush data has **USA Today's US organic Google traffic down nearly half June 2025 → June 2026, CNN ~25%, Politico 23%, Business Insider more than 85%**. AI chatbots grew referral volume more than 200% but still contribute under 1% of publisher referrals. Reddit, Reuters and USA Today are weighing blocking Google outright.

4. **The toll booth is being built, with a date.** Cloudflare's **Pay Per Crawl is becoming Pay Per Use** — paying publishers when content creates value rather than when a bot fetches — and **from 2026-09-15 Cloudflare's defaults block "mixed-use" crawlers from any page carrying ads**. Apple is reportedly in multi-year pay-per-use negotiations with publishers; People Inc. told investors that Cloudflare blocking is what created its licensing leverage.

5. **The SaaSocalypse: over $1 trillion of software market capitalisation repriced in two weeks.** Between late January and early February 2026 the SaaS sector lost more than **$1T in market cap** on a single realisation — that per-seat licensing punishes the customer for becoming efficient and punishes the vendor for delivering it. Gartner expects **70% of businesses to prefer usage-based pricing over per-seat by 2026**. Agents are increasingly priced against **HR budgets rather than IT budgets, at $800–2,000+ per month positioned as full-time-equivalent replacement**; Intercom's Fin charges $0.99 per resolution and Zendesk $1.50 per automated resolution behind a 72-hour quiet period.

6. **Europe is permanently deleting its chemical industry.** Cefic reports **plant closures up sixfold since 2022, totalling 37 million tonnes of capacity — roughly 9% of European output** — with **20,000 jobs lost and 89,000 indirect at risk**. Announced investment capacity fell from **2.7 Mt in 2022 to 0.3 Mt** year-to-date 2025. **49% of closure announcements cite energy cost competitiveness**, with European gas averaging two and a half times US prices through 2025. Germany 25%, Netherlands 20%, UK 12%, France 10%. Separately, **roughly half of chemical companies report having reformulated or phased out products containing chemicals of concern in the past three years**, with a similar share expecting to continue.

7. **The private long-term care insurance market has effectively ceased to exist.** Roughly **fifteen active writers of standalone LTC in 2026, down from more than a hundred at the early-2000s peak** — John Hancock, Prudential, MetLife, Lincoln Benefit, CNA and Allianz all stopped writing years ago. Hybrid product underwriting is still manual and takes days to weeks. **Genworth launched its first medically underwritten single-premium immediate annuity in early 2026**, the first real sign the impaired-life category is thawing in the US, where it is otherwise rare and standard in the UK.

8. **Care demand is unbounded and supply is wage-constrained.** **95% of home and community-based providers report moderate or severe staffing shortages and 77% have been forced to turn away referrals.** The home care workforce is projected to generate **over 6.1 million job openings 2024–2034, second among all US occupations**. **About 75% of older adults living at home with long-term care needs rely on unpaid family caregivers**, solely or in part. There is no Medicare coverage for long-term care.

9. **Deepfake-enabled fraud is compounding fast.** At least **$3.7B in visible global losses, ~89% of it in 2025 and H1 2026**, with congressional analysis suggesting fewer than 5% of voice-clone victims report. Deloitte projects **US gen-AI-enabled fraud losses of $40B by 2027** from $12.3B in 2023 (32% CAGR). Biometric liveness is being defeated at onboarding — one Amsterdam case involved 47 fraudulent accounts opened against a selfie-versus-ID check.

10. **Cargo theft turned into identity fraud.** US cargo theft losses reached **~$725M in 2025, up 60%**, and **Q2 2026 losses more than doubled year on year to $304.6M**. **Strategic theft — carrier identity theft, double brokering, fictitious pickups, cyber-enabled broker account compromise — is now about a third of cargo crime**, with deceptive-pickup schemes up 31%.

11. **The legal profession's ownership rule is being dismantled by capital.** Arizona has approved **136 alternative business structures**, and **59% of newly licensed 2024 ABSs with known ownership were wholly owned by nonlawyers**. Investors now holding stakes include Pravati, Melody, Kayne Anderson, Counsel Financial, Bespoke and Virage; KPMG Law US entered via an ABS-licensed affiliate. Staff losses, shutdowns and new review hurdles produced the worst federal research funding bottleneck in modern history; a national STAT survey documents layoffs, lab closures and early-career scientists leaving research.

13. **Autonomous science became national infrastructure.** NSF is establishing the **Autonomous Robotic Metallurgist Materials Innovation Platform at Texas A&M (announced 2026-08-03)**, the first national self-driving laboratory for metals; Rice took a ~$20M NSF award for AI-driven autonomous experimentation in semiconductor synthesis; the SPEED programmable cloud-lab test bed runs $20M over four years. 14. **World models reached real-time playable state.** DeepMind's **Genie 3** generates interactive environments from text, image or sketch **at 24 fps with world state held for minutes**, with physics simulated for control; NVIDIA Cosmos is self-hostable for Text2World/Image2World/Video2World; World Labs' Marble is commercial. ## Surviving ideas

### 1. Care-horizon underwriting — impaired-life pricing that converts a house into paid care at home

**In plain English.** Almost everyone wants to grow old in their own house, and for most families the house *is* the money. But the insurance that used to pay for help at home has essentially disappeared — barely fifteen companies still sell it, down from more than a hundred — and the one government-backed way to get cash out of your house, the reverse mortgage, is not allowed to look at your health at all. So someone with three years left before they need a nursing home gets the same deal as someone with twenty. This company reads a person's real medical and functional history to work out how long they will realistically stay at home and how much help they will need, and on that basis hands the family money now — repaid when the house is eventually sold — so they can hire a carer this month at proper wages instead of joining a waiting list. The family pays for it out of the equity they were going to spend anyway; they buy it because the alternative is a daughter quitting her job.

**Who buys it.** The adult child — most often a daughter in her fifties — at the trigger moment: a parent's fall, a dementia diagnosis, or a hospital discharge that comes with a new care requirement, when the family discovers in-home care runs $6,000–10,000 a month, Medicare pays none of it, and the only asset is the house.

**Why no one already serves them.** The federal reverse mortgage is barred by program design from underwriting health, so it is structurally wrong for exactly the people who need it most; the home-equity investment companies (Point, Hometap, Unlock, Splitero, Fraction) underwrite the *house*, not the person, and have no clinical capability; the long-term care insurers who could underwrite health fled after mispricing the risk once and cannot re-enter under legacy reserve and rate-approval constraints; and Waterlily, the only company that predicts individual care trajectories, sells the prediction to advisors rather than taking the risk.

**Scores** — Novelty 4 · Why-now 4 · Market 5 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 4 → **composite 4.2**

**Biggest risk.** This is a balance-sheet business selling a health-underwritten financial product to elderly homeowners: it is regulated state by state, adverse selection is the precise failure that destroyed the long-term care insurance industry the first time, and you find out whether your care-trajectory model was right over a decade rather than a quarter.

### 2. Capacity now — a merchant that buys Europe's stranded process trains and sells American plants a date

**In plain English.** Europe is shutting chemical factories permanently — about a tenth of its capacity has already gone — and when a plant closes, its reactors, columns and dryers are usually scrapped, because working out whether a particular second-hand vessel could safely do a *different* job at a *different* factory takes a senior engineer several days per item, which costs more than the item is worth. Meanwhile American companies trying to build new plants for medicines, chemicals and batteries are being told to wait two to four years for the same equipment, and the wait is what kills the project. This company uses a model to read the engineering paperwork on thousands of stranded machines and match them to the specific jobs new plants need doing, then buys the ones that fit and sells the new plant something it cannot buy anywhere else: an in-service date next year instead of in four.

**Who buys it.** The project director or owner's engineer at a US specialty-chemical, active-pharmaceutical-ingredient or battery-materials plant under construction, at the moment one or two long-lead items push the in-service date past a financing milestone.

**Why no one already serves them.** Used-equipment dealers are catalogue businesses — their sales engineers can only assess what a buyer already thought to ask about, so they wait for demand rather than originating a substitution inside a live design; EPC contractors are paid a percentage of installed cost and are structurally disincentivised to specify a used asset; and the companies doing the closing book the equipment as a decommissioning cost line, not an asset worth marketing.

**Scores** — Novelty 4 · Why-now 4 · Market 4 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 4 → **composite 4.0**

**Biggest risk.** Relocation, re-rating and requalification cost real money and real months, and if that total exceeds the value of the schedule you are selling, the trade never clears — which is the reason the market looks the way it does today, not an oversight. Also flagged honestly: this shares a template with ts-0202 from two runs ago, and the operator should decide whether that is a pattern worth repeating or a rut.

## What this run establishes

1. **The transaction pre-prompt works, and this run is the proof.** ts-0238 and ts-0220 are the same underwriting capability pointed at the same population. One sells a forecast to an advisor and is occupied by a funded company. The other takes the risk and is open. That is not a coincidence of this domain — it is what the pre-prompt predicted would happen, and it should now be applied *before* the novelty search rather than after, because it would have saved four of this run's seventeen checks.
2. **A legally-mandated blindness is a better why-now than a new regulation.** Every compliance-rail idea this ledger has killed had a why-now of the form "a new rule creates work." ts-0220's why-now is the opposite shape: an old rule *forbids* the incumbent product from using the information that would price it correctly, and that prohibition has been sitting there for decades doing nothing — until the information became cheap to process. Look for prohibitions, not mandates.
3. **Leaving the build-out cost accuracy and bought coverage.** Seventeen checks produced two survivors, and three of the kills (Sensi.AI, Givers, Waterlily) are companies a domain-native founder would have named instantly. That is the same tax the 2026-08-13 run paid on physical AI. It is worth paying once per new surface and not twice — the aging/care surface now has six ledger entries and should be cheap to search next time.
4. **Two more squares can be closed.** The **agentic-web economy** (ts-0225, ts-0234, ts-0235 this run, on top of ts-0175/0176/0182) is now comprehensively occupied by Cloudflare, the labs and a full generation of GEO startups — treat it as closed, like the compliance rail. The **agent-as-employee** square (ts-0233, on top of ts-0131 and ts-0204) is a platform-owner primitive and should be closed too.

## Takeaway for next run

1. **ts-0220 has one question and it is quantitative, not a scan.** Does health-informed underwriting actually move the advance rate enough to matter? Take the HECM principal-limit-factor table, take published care-onset and time-at-home distributions conditional on a few common diagnoses, and compute the spread between an age-only advance and a health-informed one for three archetypal 72-year-olds. If the spread is 10 points of home value, this is a product. If it is 2, it is a feature of a reverse mortgage. **That is an afternoon with a spreadsheet, and it decides the company.**
2. **ts-0221 has one question too, and it is the same shape as ts-0202's.** What does relocation plus re-rating plus requalification actually cost as a percentage of new-build, for a glass-lined reactor and for a distillation column? Pull two real decommissioning sale records and two new-equipment quotes. If the all-in used cost is above ~60% of new, the schedule premium has to carry the whole trade and the business is thin.
3. **The phone call is now eight runs old and the queue has grown to ten.** Add a hospital discharge planner or an elder-law attorney, and a project director at an API plant under construction. Twenty-nine runs of desk research produced ten candidates waiting on one conversation each. A thirtieth will not validate any of them, and at this point the honest read is that the research pass is no longer the bottleneck by a wide margin.
