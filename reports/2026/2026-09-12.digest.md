*Trend Scout — 2026-09-12*

*2 survivors, 14 kills.*

*1. Coverage archaeology as a machine (composite 4.3)*
When a company gets sued today over pollution from the 1960s — or a church or school gets sued over
abuse from the 1970s — the money to pay the settlement has to come from insurance policies bought
before 1986, because insurers stopped covering this in 1986. Those old policies are usually real and
the paperwork proving it is usually lost, in warehouse boxes, microfilm and handwritten payment
ledgers at insurers that have changed names four times since. Right now a dozen tiny firms find them
by paying researchers to read boxes by hand; we do it with a machine that reads everything at once,
works out which dead insurer is which company today, rebuilds what the missing policies said from the
standard forms of that year, and hands over a court-ready chart — paid out of the money recovered, so
it costs the customer nothing to find out.
*For:* the general counsel of a manufacturer or airport just served with a PFAS or contamination
claim, and the people running a diocese, school system or town through bankruptcy inside an abuse
lawsuit window. PFAS liability alone is estimated at $120–165bn, with over $100bn unresolved.

*2. Benefit of record (composite 4.0)*
A company pension scheme that promised people a pension for life eventually pays an insurance company
to take the whole thing over so it can shut down — but first somebody has to establish exactly what
every member is owed, and the rules are scattered across forty years of legal documents and
amendments that often contradict each other. Nobody really knows, so the insurer adds a big margin for
the risk that the numbers are wrong and the deal drags on for years. We read all of it with a machine,
work out what each person is genuinely entitled to and why, and stand behind the answer — so the
insurer can drop its margin and the scheme can close in months.
*For:* the pricing team at a pension buyout insurer and the trustees on the other side of the same
deal. About 300 UK schemes are reaching buyout this year against £40bn+ of deals, and the industry's
own trackers say the queue is getting *longer* because of exactly this data problem.

*Also worth knowing:* both survivors are the same shape — reconstruct an entitlement nobody has read
in forty years, and sell the determination with the liability attached. In both cases the party who
should own the record is barred from being the one who audits it, and the people doing the work today
are hourly research firms whose website is their technology. Two mechanisms are now exhausted for this
book: AI machining software (four narrowings, four occupied) and counter-drone (a $1.5bn acquisition
closed into the only remaining channel last month).
