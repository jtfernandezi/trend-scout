## Surviving idea

### ts-0001 — Buy-side SBA capital-stack structuring engine for self-funded acquirers

**The cross (needs both halves, collapses without either):**
- *AI half:* frontier LLM reasoning over the dense, freshly-rewritten SOP 50-10 8
  rules + a buyer's messy deal inputs, producing an optimized, compliant
  capital stack and the seller-facing negotiation collateral. Two years ago this
  was a $5k+ human-advisor task.
- *Structural half:* SOP 50-10 8 (2025-06-01) turned deal structuring into an
  acute, deal-killing problem precisely as the $5T boomer transfer floods the
  lower-middle market with exactly these sub-$5M acquisitions. Without the rule
  change there's no crisis (old rules were lenient/near-zero-down); without
  modern AI it's a retainer.

**The exact buyer.** A first-time **self-funded searcher / ETA buyer** under a
signed LOI on a **$400k–$2M SDE** owner-operated service business (HVAC,
plumbing, landscaping, pest control) — at the financing-structuring stage, not
the browsing stage.

**The trigger moment.** Their SBA lender tells them the seller note they assumed
would cover most of the down payment must be on **full standby for the entire
10-year loan term** to count toward the 10% equity injection — and the
60-something seller, who expected to be paid, balks. The LOI/diligence clock is
running and the deal is days from collapsing over structure, not price.

**Why incumbents structurally can't/won't serve this.**
- *Lender-side software* (Aloan, LenderAI, Biz2x, iBusiness) organizes and
  underwrites the file **to protect the bank, after the structure is fixed** —
  wrong side, wrong incentive.
- *Retained buy-side advisors* (e.g. Munera) "pay for themselves in 18–24
  months" — uneconomic and too slow for a sub-$2M deal.
- *ETA content/communities* (ClearlyAcquired, EBIT, ProjectionHub) educate but
  hand you a blog post, not a compliant structure + a one-page seller
  explainer + a lender-ready term sheet.
- *Brokers* represent the seller. Nobody arms the small self-funded buyer.

**Why now.** SOP 50-10 8 is datable to 2025-06-01 and is *actively killing
deals right now*; the boomer-transfer volume surge is simultaneous. This is
urgent in 2026 in a way it simply was not in 2024.

**Scores (rubric):** Novelty 4 · Why-now 5 · Market/venture-scale 3 ·
Solo-buildable 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 4 → **composite 4.0**.

**Honest caveats.** Composite sits *exactly* on the 4.0 line, not clearly above
it. The load-bearing risk is **market/venture-scale (3)**: the wedge buyer is
episodic and low-LTV (buys one business, maybe once a decade). The venture story
requires expanding from the structuring wedge into the transaction/financing
layer — lender-match take-rate, post-close portfolio SaaS, moving up-market to
independent sponsors and PE tuck-ins. Founder-fit is strong (decision-support,
B2B/prosumer workflow, reachable ETA-community GTM, leverages strategy/finance);
no technical-cofounder blocker. Full brief: `ideas/briefs/0001-sba-buyside-deal-structuring.md`.

## Surviving ideas

Both survivors sit on the **same brand-new, totally unoccupied regulatory rail**
(California's STOP Act), but are *different products with different buyers* — one is
bottoms-up workflow SaaS sold to tiny shops, the other a top-down verification rail
sold to the handful of distributors who are the legal enforcement chokepoint. The
strongest (ts-0007) gets the full brief; the second is here as an option to weigh.

### ts-0007 — STOP Act compliance + certification copilot for engineered-stone fab shops

**In plain English.** California just passed a law to stop countertop workers from
dying of a lung disease (silicosis) they get from cutting fake-stone ("quartz")
countertops. Starting July 1, every little countertop shop — often a 5-to-10-person
business run by a Spanish-speaking owner with no safety staff — has to train its
workers and sign a yearly government form swearing it's following the rules, and
within a year it must get state-certified or its suppliers are legally banned from
selling it the stone slabs it needs to stay in business. This is a phone-and-laptop
tool, in Spanish, that walks the owner through the training, keeps the worker
health records the law requires, and spits out the exact signed form the state wants
— so a tiny shop can stay open without hiring a $10k safety consultant. The owner
pays because the alternative is fines, a lawsuit, or being cut off from slabs.

- **Who buys it.** The owner of a 3–15-person engineered-stone countertop fab shop
  in California (frequently Spanish-first, no EHS staff), at the moment the **July 1
  2026** training/attestation requirement lands — or when their slab distributor
  tells them they must be state-certified by **July 2027** to keep buying slabs.
- **Why no one already serves them.** General EHS platforms (VelocityEHS, KPA,
  Cority, J.J. Keller) are built and priced for mid/large firms with full-time safety
  managers and English workflows; the only thing aimed at these shops today is static
  OSHA / Natural Stone Institute PDFs and one-off consultants. A novelty search found
  **no software product** encoding SB 20's new, CA-specific, phased certification
  regime for a tiny Spanish-first shop.
- **Scores:** Novelty 5 · Why-now 5 · Market 3 · Solo-build 4 · YC-fit 4 ·
  Founder-fit 4 → **composite 4.2**.
- **Biggest risk.** The base market is small (~hundreds-to-low-thousands of CA shops,
  low ACV); the venture story depends on expanding to the national OSHA silica
  population (construction, foundries, masonry) **and** becoming the certification
  *record* that the distributor rail (ts-0008) runs on.

### ts-0008 — Slab-distributor certification-verification rail

**In plain English.** The same California law has a second, sharper edge: the
companies that *sell* the stone slabs are legally on the hook to check that the shop
they're selling to is certified, and by mid-2027 they simply can't sell to one that
isn't. There are only a few dozen of these distributors, but they feed thousands of
shops, and right now their order systems have no way to check a shop's certification
against the new state list. This is the software that sits in the distributor's
sales process and instantly answers "is this buyer allowed to receive slabs?" —
keeping a clean record proving they checked. They pay because one wrong sale is a
liability, and being the chokepoint means whoever owns this verification step
effectively owns the rail between every distributor and every shop.

- **Who buys it.** Compliance / sales-ops lead at a stone-slab distributor or importer
  (Caesarstone, Cambria, MSI, Arizona Tile, regional importers) selling into
  California, as the **2027** supplier-certification bar and the **2026** state
  database spin up and they need a defensible "we verified before we sold" process.
- **Why no one already serves them.** Stone-industry ERPs and order systems have no
  concept of a per-customer state silica-certification gate; generic compliance/KYB
  vendors won't touch a niche state public-health database. The rail sits between
  ~50–100 distributors and thousands of shops with built-in distribution.
- **Scores:** Novelty 5 · Why-now 3 · Market 4 · Solo-build 3 · YC-fit 4 ·
  Founder-fit 3 → **composite 3.7**.
- **Biggest risk.** The *hard* enforcement trigger is July 2027, a year out, so
  urgency in 2026 is "prepare," not "bleeding now"; it's an enterprise sale + system
  integration, likely needing a technical cofounder. Strongest as the second move
  *after* ts-0007 has signed up the shops and become the de-facto compliance record.
