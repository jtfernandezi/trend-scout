# Trend Scout — Report 2026-10-22

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (fifty-sixth run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).
**Outcome:** **Zero surviving ideas. 9 new kills (ts-0532 to ts-0540).** No brief this run. No held-book entry
changes status.

This run followed the 2026-10-20 method note:

- **The skip list was respected.** Greptile, Town, Chariot Claims, Pathways, Solvera, Modal, Runpod, Tenstorrent,
  Blackwall, Ramp, Stand and Abby Care repeated on this week's trackers and were not re-pointed.
- **Channel conflict was tested first (note one).** It produced real conflicts three times: Longeye against police
  oversight bodies, Baba against health plans, and, in the held book, Arda against the government reviewer. In
  the two new cases the buyer on the other side had no budget.
- **Seed-stage leaders were deprioritised (note two).** All nine patterns this run are Series A or later, or hold
  disclosed revenue or contract ceilings.

**The finding worth carrying forward: this week's money went to physical AI and horizontal platforms, and the one
justice-system breakout led to a government that is building the tool itself.** ASI (325M dollars at 3bn), Arda
(160M dollars at 1.5bn), Eka (150M dollars at 1.45bn) and Daytona (1bn dollars) dominated the week. They are
operations, manufacturing, robotics and infrastructure companies that can reach almost any buyer with a sales
hire. Longeye, the vertical breakout, opened one interesting flank, UK disclosure. There the Home Office is
legislating for AI evidence review and funding a central PoliceAI programme, which makes the buyer a single
national programme rather than 43 forces. **New rule: before a geography flank into a public-sector buyer, check
whether the government is building the tool centrally.**

## Breakout startups scanned

1. **ASI** (formerly Air Space Intelligence): **325M dollar Series C at 3bn dollars led by Spark Capital,
   2026-10-20** (Citadel Securities affiliates, Meritech, Craft, a16z). It holds an **FAA modernisation contract
   with a reported 875M dollar ceiling** (a ceiling, not revenue). **Mechanism:** models forecast, simulate and
   optimise networked physical operations (air traffic, logistics, energy) under uncertainty.
2. **Arda Industries** (Bob McGrew, ex-OpenAI): **160M dollar Series A at 1.5bn dollars** (Altimeter, Accel and
   Khosla co-leading), 232M dollars raised in total. It is **deployed with Anduril inside ArsenalOS**. Round figures
   vary across sources: earlier reports gave a 70M dollar round at 700M dollars. **Mechanism:** AI shortens the
   engineering needed to turn a product design into a manufacturing process and an assembly line.
3. **Eka Robotics**: **150M dollar Series A at 1.45bn dollars, co-led by a16z and F-Prime, 2026-10-20**. Lab
   benchmark of **about 1,500 picks an hour against a human 600, with success above 95%**, and industrial pilots
   that include wire manipulation. **Mechanism:** a vision-force-action model trained in simulation handles
   contact-rich manipulation.
4. **Longeye**: **20M dollar Series A led by N47, with a16z American Dynamism, 2026-10-21**. It grew from **35
   agencies in March to more than 50 justice agencies**, with more than 2,300 investigations worked and 25M files
   scanned in Q1 (company-reported). **Mechanism:** an AI investigative workspace reads every file in a case
   (phone extractions, jail calls, video, reports) and surfaces the evidence detectives missed.
5. **Qualitate**: **25M dollar Series A led by Insight Partners**, which was a customer first. More than **one
   million minutes of AI-moderated interviews**, revenue run-rate **up more than tenfold this year**, and
   **net revenue retention above 300%** (company-reported). It belongs to a cluster with Outset and Listen Labs.
   **Mechanism:** an AI interviewer runs hundreds of long, adaptive expert and customer interviews in parallel
   and synthesises them, which makes primary research nearly free.
6. **Baba**: **40M dollar Series A led by FirstMark** (CVS Health Ventures, Valtruis, General Catalyst). More than
   **10,000 families supported and 100,000 on its waiting list** (company-reported). **Mechanism:** AI plus human
   coordinators do the paperwork and coordination for Medicare and Medicaid families.
7. **Pigment**: **250M dollar Series E at 2.3bn dollars** (Scaleup Europe Fund / EQT). **ARR above 100M dollars,
   doubling year on year** (company-reported). **Mechanism:** agents build and update business-planning models from
   natural-language instructions over live financial data.
8. **Daytona**: **60M dollar Series B at 1bn dollars led by FirstMark Growth**. **Mechanism:** millisecond-start
   isolated sandboxes where AI agents run code and keep state.
9. **Otter.ai**: **320M dollars of non-equity growth financing** (General Catalyst Customer Value Fund). It passed
   **100M dollars of ARR in 2025** and has more than 40M users. **Mechanism:** speech models capture and act on every
   spoken conversation.

Also scanned and not re-pointed:

- **Elax Énergie**: 30M euros, about 100,000 homes. Water-heater load shifting, not AI-native.
- **AuxoAI** (20.7M dollar Series A, forward-deployed AI services), **Asymptote Labs** (agent security, on the skip

- **Antithesis** (105M dollar Series A) and **Reliable Robotics** (160M dollar Series D) showed up on this week's

## Method note for the next run

**One. For public-sector geography flanks, check for a central government build first.** The UK's PoliceAI
programme turned 43 police forces into one buyer that is building the tool itself. Search for "[ministry] AI
programme [workflow]" before arguing the wedge. The NHS (Federated Data Platform) and EU member-state digital
justice programmes are likely to behave the same way.

**Two. Channel-conflict flanks must pass a "who pays" check before the wedge.** This run's two genuine conflicts
(oversight bodies, Medicaid appellants) both had buyers with no budget. A conflict of interest only matters if the
buyer on the other side has a line item. The defence SETA seat (ts-0470) passes because program offices fund it.

**Three. AI-moderated interviewing is the cluster with the best retention numbers, but its regulated flanks are
all taken.** The unchecked territory is non-regulated buyers who currently pay expert networks by the hour:
corporate strategy, consulting and private-equity diligence. Test those only against the billing screen, since
expert networks charge per call, so the incumbents face a real innovator's dilemma.

**Four. Skip for now:** everything from the 2026-10-13 to 2026-10-20 lists, plus ASI, Arda, Eka, Longeye, Daytona,
Otter, Pigment and Baba. Also skip UK police disclosure, police oversight, pharma AI-moderated research,
public-sector budgeting, agent sandboxes, wire-harness robotics, court reporting and Medicaid appeals.
