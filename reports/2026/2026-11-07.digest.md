*Trend Scout — 2026-11-07*

*1 survivor, 7 kills.*

*AI-native subcontractor-default insurer for mid-size builders* (composite 3.8)
Big builders can insure against one of their specialist firms (electricians, plumbers and so on) going bust
mid-project, but insurers only sell this to the largest builders. We would insure mid-size builders, using software
that reads each specialist firm's accounts and the job paperwork to warn the builder early and pay out when one fails.
_For:_ finance chiefs at general contractors handing out 30-150 million dollars of work a year, mostly in the Sunbelt.

*What we tried.* This week's winners were Zipline (drone delivery, 1.5bn dollars at 21.5bn dollars), Pliant (business
payment cards, 100M dollars a year in revenue), Rivio (AI that audits Brazilian hospital bills, R$180M), Clark (AI audit
staff for accounting firms), Bedrock (self-driving excavators, 600M dollars), Sand.ai (AI video, 10M dollars a year in
revenue in two months) and Vera Health (an AI assistant used by 800,000 clinicians).

*What died, in plain English.*
- AI hospital-bill checking in India: already done, and hard to make big.
- Malpractice insurance for accountants: Clark already guarantees its own work, and the market is small.
- Drone delivery for small shops: Zipline's Uber Eats deal already covers them.
- Payment cards for AI agents: Ramp, Brex, Visa and Mastercard already do it.
- Insurance for self-driving construction machines: big insurers already offer it.
- Doctor malpractice insurance priced by AI: Indigo already does it.
- AI video ads for drug companies: existing compliance tools already cover it.

*Also.* Last run's apartment-insurance idea passed its fact check: Florida's bill to weaken the law it relies on died.

*Lesson.* Taking on the risk yourself still works, but only where insurers sell the product to big customers alone.
