*2 survivors, 15 kills.*

*1. Retracement — the AI-native land survey firm (composite 4.2)*
Before anyone builds a solar farm, a data centre or a fibre line, a licensed surveyor has to read two
centuries of old deeds and go find the iron pins to say exactly where the property line is — and the
US is running out of those people (56,200 licensed surveyors in 2010, 47,770 in 2020, average age
nearly 60), just as a record 43.4 GW of solar and $42.5B of fibre hit the ground. We do the reading
and the reasoning by machine, send a small crew to confirm what's physically there, and sign the
survey ourselves.
*For:* solar, storage and data-centre developers, fibre builders, title agencies and CRE lenders who
are quoted six to ten weeks for something they need in days.

*2. The government-contract ledger (composite 4.3)*
When a small defence startup wins its first real government contract, the government only pays if its
bookkeeping meets a federal standard built for $50M prime contractors — so a twelve-person company
either hires a specialist accountant by the hour or buys a 1990s system that costs six figures to
install. We build the accounting system that just does it: reads the rules and the contract, sorts
every dollar correctly, files what the government asks for, and stands behind the audit.
*For:* the founder or first finance hire at a defence or dual-use hardware startup, at the moment a
cost-type award (typically a $1-1.5M SBIR Phase II) is in reach.

*Also worth knowing:* ten of the fifteen kills died because someone funded is already shipping the
identical product. Two mechanisms are now formally exhausted — ambient capture of physical work, and
agent governance.
