## Surviving ideas

### ts-0586 — Camera-underwritten premises liability: an AI-native MGA that prices apartment and hotel liability on what the property's own cameras show

**In plain English.** Apartment owners are being sued, and losing big, when a tenant or visitor is attacked and a
jury decides the building's security was inadequate. Insurers have answered by raising prices or refusing to cover
these buildings at all, even well-run ones. We would be an insurer that looks at the security cameras a building
already has, and has software watch them to check that the gates close, the lights work and the cameras are actually
recording. Well-run buildings pay less and can get coverage again, and if they are sued they have a dated record
showing their security was working.

- **The proven pattern:** Solink is raising 100M dollars+ on 500,000 cameras showing that AI can read video that
  businesses already record. Nirvana (1.5bn dollars, premium doubling) shows that an insurer pricing on live sensor
  data wins a hard liability line. Kalepa (75M dollars, cash-flow positive) shows insurers will pay for agents that do
  the underwriting work.
- **Who buys the narrowed version:** owners and managers of Class B and C apartment communities in Florida, Georgia
  and Texas that already have cloud cameras, at the moment their general-liability renewal is non-renewed or jumps,
  bought through wholesale habitational brokers.
- **Why the leader can't follow:** camera-AI vendors would have to become licensed, risk-bearing insurers, and
  pricing their own customers' liability would turn their product into evidence against those customers. Kalepa would
  be competing with the carriers it sells to. Incumbent habitational carriers price from forms and ZIP codes and have
  no installed layer at the property.
- **Expansion path:** Sunbelt multifamily GL, then all US habitational GL and umbrella, hotels, retail centres,
  parking and nightlife, then a premises-liability carrier with loss control and claims-defence evidence as added
  revenue lines, in a general-liability market of more than 100bn dollars of premium a year.
- **Scores:** Wedge 3 · Pattern 4 · Market 4 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 4 → composite 3.8.
- **Biggest risk:** a carrier and a camera vendor partner on a discount programme (as Progressive did against
  Nirvana), and a new MGA must win fronting capacity in the hardest liability line while avoiding adverse selection.

## Method note for the next run

**One. Insurer-of-record is a fresh axis, so use it again.** Three of this run's kills were software flanks that
seed-stage specialists had already taken. The survivor took the leader's mechanism and **bore the risk** on it
instead. Next run, try the same move on other sensor-rich or document-rich breakouts. The question is whose data
could price a liability line that carriers are fleeing, and whether the breakout would have to become a regulated
risk-bearer to use it.

**Two. Verify ts-0586's two weakest facts first.** Check whether Florida HB 1423 / SB 956 passed in 2026, and whether
Verkada, Flock or a habitational carrier has announced a camera-linked liability programme.

**Three. Skip for now:** everything on the 2026-10-13 to 2026-11-03 skip lists, plus Kalepa (except the
insurer-of-record axis), Nevis, Traversal, Applied Compute, Solink software flanks, Sentra, premium audit, surety,
