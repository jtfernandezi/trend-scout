# Trend Scout — Report 2026-08-06

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (twenty-fourth run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).

**Outcome:** **0 surviving ideas, 9 logged kills.** Second consecutive zero-survivor run; eleventh in twenty-four.

The last run's takeaway said not to run another general generative pass. I ran one anyway, because that is what the schedule asks for — but I deliberately changed the *input* rather than the method. Every one of the last four runs led with a frontier-capability signal and then went looking for an industry. This run led with **the biggest structural dislocations of 2026 that this ledger has never touched**, and went looking for the capability afterwards. That produced a genuinely fresh signal set — the Iran war and the Hormuz closure, the collapse of marine war-risk capacity, the data-center consent revolt, the utility affordability revolt, the fertilizer shock — none of which appear anywhere in 164 prior entries.

It did not produce a survivor, and the *pattern* of how the candidates died is the finding. Seven of the nine kills were killed by a vendor's own published marketing, and in three cases that marketing was keyed to the identical 2026 event I was crossing on. Icertis has a blog post about force majeure and the Strait of Hormuz. Windward has one called "Marine Insurance in 2026: Pricing Risk in a Changed Ocean." Transect sells "community sentiment" as a data layer. **A dislocation being new to this ledger does not make it new to the vendors who serve that industry — they were already there when it happened.** Novelty of the *signal* is not novelty of the *idea*, and I have now spent two runs confusing the two.

## Signals scanned

1. **Multi-day autonomous agency shipped as a product (2026-08-03).** Qwen3.8-Max went generally available claiming autonomous completion of software projects lasting more than ten days, reproduction of research papers involving thousands of lines of code, iterative chip-design optimisation, and continuous plan revision from multimodal feedback — scoring 86.1 on OSWorld-Verified, ahead of GPT-5.6 Sol Max (83.2) and Fable 5 (85.0). GPT-5.6 Sol set 53.6 on Agents' Last Exam (long-running professional workflows across 55 fields) and Sol Ultra hits 91.9% on parallelizable long-horizon work. GPT-5.6 Luna is priced at $0.20 per million input tokens. The economic unit an agent maps to has moved from *a task* to *a project*.

2. **The 2026 Iran war and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz.** War broke out 2026-02-28; the strait closed 2026-03-04. The IEA calls it the largest supply disruption in the history of the global oil market — at least 10 million barrels per day off across Kuwait, Iraq, Saudi Arabia and the UAE, Brent past $120, tanker traffic down 90–95%, and the Suez route abandoned for the Cape of Good Hope with conflict surcharges of $2,000–3,500 per container.

3. **Marine war-risk capacity collapsed as a measurement failure, and the state backfilled it.** Available capacity for a single VLCC transit fell from $2–3B pre-crisis to $500M–$1.5B. Primary hull insurers and P&I clubs cancelled cover *because their reinsurers withdrew* — owners willing to pay any rate found no taker. Munich Re, Swiss Re and Hannover Re imposed Gulf war-risk aggregation sub-limits. Lloyd's launched a new marine war-risk consortium (2026-06-19). The US International Development Finance Corporation stood up a maritime reinsurance facility at $20B and expanded it to $40B two weeks later, with Chubb as lead alongside Travelers, Liberty Mutual, Berkshire Hathaway, AIG, Starr and CNA. Commentators describe the Red Sea 2023–25 and Hormuz 2026 together as a permanent structural repricing of marine war risk.

4. **Qatari LNG force majeure, declared and repeatedly extended.** An Iranian strike on Ras Laffan removed roughly 17% of Qatar's LNG export capacity (~$20B/yr of revenue). QatarEnergy declared force majeure on long-term contracts with Italian, Belgian, South Korean and Chinese buyers on 2026-03-24 and extended it on 2026-07-28 toward mid-October. The 33 Mtpa North Field East expansion is halted. TTF more than doubled to above €61/MWh; JKM passed $25/MMBtu; flexible cargoes are diverting from Europe to Asia.

5. **The fertilizer shock, and fertilizer's reclassification as a strategic national asset.** Urea went from ~$350/t in late 2025 to over $800/t by late March 2026 — roughly 55% between February and May. Around 5 Mt of urea and 40% of global phosphate trade left the international market. India expanded subsidies and restricted rice exports; China restricted fertilizer exports and expanded grain reserves. The FAO published a global agrifood implications paper. Farmers are choosing between cutting input use and accepting lower yields, or absorbing the cost.

6. **The data-center consent revolt.** 75+ projects worth about $130B blocked or delayed in Q1 2026 — the most in any quarter since tracking began in 2023. Active opposition groups more than doubled to 833 across 49 states. Over 300 bills were introduced in statehouses in the first six weeks of the year; 69 local governments had enacted bans by May. Gallup puts local opposition at 71% with 48% strongly opposed — higher than opposition to a nearby nuclear plant. Water is the second front: three companies secured multi-million-gallon-per-day supplies in Virginia, Louisiana and Indiana in February alone, peak cooling demand could need 697M–1.45B additional gallons per day within four years, Illinois is considering mandating water-efficient cooling, and Google began pushing industry water standards on 2026-06-03.

7. **The utility affordability revolt.** Utilities requested $18.6B of rate increases in H1 2026 alone, against $29B for all of 2025 and $15B in 2024 — a record $9.2B in Q2 by itself. Residential rates rose ~10.2% year on year. 94.9 million customers received final disconnection notices in 2024 and 13.4 million households were actually cut off; NEADA projects roughly $25B of household arrears by end-2026.

8. **Construction labor as the buildout's real constraint.** US construction must add 349,000 net new workers in 2026 and 456,000 in 2027 against a ~500,000-worker gap, much of it retirement rather than growth. Immigrants are 34% of the workforce and over 60% in drywall, roofing and plastering, under active enforcement. And **$86B of data-center construction alone accounts for ~296,700 jobs — roughly 85% of the entire industry's workforce need for the year.** Large projects run 20% behind schedule with overruns up to 80%; 98% face delays; average duration runs 37% long.

9. **The memory shortage deepened into industry-wide contraction.** DRAM spot prices are up nearly 700% year on year (Bloomberg, July 2026). Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron — over 95% of global DRAM — have reallocated capacity to HBM. Intel's CEO: "there's no relief until 2028." Apple raised prices across iPad, Mac, HomePod, Vision Pro and Apple TV effective immediately on 2026-06-25 and its shares fell over 6%; smartphone shipments are forecast down 12.9% and PCs down 11.3% for 2026.

10. **YC Fall 2026 RFS and the casualty backdrop.** The thirteen requests are The Primer, The Future of American Defense, A Cloud for Small Software, Multiplayer AI, Compute at Sea, AI-Powered Consumer Products for 1 Billion People, AI for the Aging Population, New Operating Systems for the Physical World, The Best Time to Build in Crypto, Data for the Real World, Proving You're Human, AI-Native Compliance Infrastructure, and Self-Maintaining APIs. Separately: 135 nuclear verdicts in 2024 (+52% YoY) totalling over $31B, and $16B added to US prior-year liability reserves.

## No surviving ideas — what this run actually establishes

Three things, in descending order of usefulness.

**1. Leading with a fresh structural signal does not help, and I should stop treating it as a lever.** The premise of this run was that the last four runs failed because they led with capability and reached for an industry, so leading with an unmined dislocation would find empty squares. The dislocations were genuinely unmined *by this ledger* — nothing in 164 entries touches the Iran war, the war-risk market, the consent revolt or the affordability revolt. Every one of them was already occupied by the vendors who serve the affected industry. Icertis was selling contract intelligence to energy and shipping companies before Hormuz closed; when it closed, they wrote a blog post about it within weeks. Windward was selling marine risk analytics before the war-risk market seized. The vendor's presence predates the event, so the event grants no head start to an outsider. **Both search directions — capability-first and structure-first — now have a failure rate high enough to be uninformative.**

**2. The one fact worth carrying forward is about labor, not about any of the ideas.** $86B of data-center construction consumes roughly 296,700 jobs, about **85% of the entire US construction industry's net workforce need for 2026**. That means the AI buildout has effectively pre-bought the country's marginal construction labor, and every housing project, hospital, school, factory and transmission line is now bidding against it for the remainder — while 34% of that workforce is foreign-born (over 60% in drywall, roofing and plastering) and under active enforcement. I could not find a product in it this run: the schedule-prediction lane is nPlan's, the field-copilot lane is NavigateAI's (ts-0026), the supervision-ratio lane is legally closed (ts-0122), the design-for-prefab lane is Augmenta's (ts-0017). But the fact is large, under-narrated, and not yet correctly attacked. It is the best raw material this run produced.

**3. The held book is where the value is, and it did get better today — twice, without any new search.** Two of the four candidates the operator is actually holding hardened on incidental evidence:

- **ts-0141 (firmware re-hosting)** — the memory signal beneath it went from a spike to a consensus two-year window. DRAM spot up ~700% YoY, over 95% of global DRAM reallocating to HBM, Intel's CEO on the record with "no relief until 2028," IDC putting relief at 2027–28. And the damage is now visible in shipped product rather than in spot prices: Apple raised prices across its whole hardware line on 2026-06-25, phones are forecast down 12.9% and PCs down 11.3%. Generating and killing ts-0172 this run also re-confirmed that the adjacent framings of this signal are occupied while this one is not.
- **ts-0133 (evidence-constrained reconstruction)** — 135 nuclear verdicts in 2024, up 52%, over $31B in awards, and $16B added to prior-year liability reserves. The stakes per physical-fact dispute are rising, which is exactly the willingness-to-pay driver. That the reserving-side idea from the same signal (ts-0166) died as occupied leaves the reconstruction wedge intact.
- **ts-0032 (DoD autonomy assurance)** picks up a directional note: the 2026 Iran war is the first state-on-state war of the frontier-AI era, which pulls hard on autonomous-systems fielding decisions. Novelty caution from 2026-07-28 is unchanged.

## Takeaway for next run

The instruction from 2026-08-04 stands and is now better evidenced: **stop running general generative passes.** Two search directions have been exhausted, and this run cost nine candidates to establish that the second one is exhausted too. Concretely, for the next scheduled slot:

1. **Do not scan.** Do a single-idea run: pick one held entry (ts-0141 or ts-0133) and spend the whole pass constructing the technical claim a buyer would have to disagree with — not a market map, which I have, but the specific thing the product must do that nPlan/Icertis/Ketryx-class incumbents structurally cannot. That is testable without a novelty search.
2. **If a generative pass is run anyway, change the unit, not the direction.** Every candidate in the last three runs has been a B2B analytics or assurance product sold to an enterprise buyer. The ledger has 173 entries and essentially zero consumer, marketplace, or own-the-asset ideas — the three shapes where "a vendor already serves this industry" is not a valid kill, because there is no incumbent vendor relationship to be occupied. That is the only structurally unmined direction left.
3. **The operator's marginal hour is still worth more on a phone call than on a scan.** Four candidates in twenty-four runs are waiting on a real buyer conversation, not on more desk research.
