This run ends a three-run streak of zeros. It followed the 2026-09-29 method note:

- **Sourcing started from the long tail.** I used early rounds and smaller deals: Charter Space (5M dollar seed), Atomic
  (12.5M dollar Series A), Modulate, Destro AI, Ahron and Zenithon. The two strongest patterns were still mid-size
  rounds with hard numbers: EliseAI and Flow Engineering. None of the six breakouts below had appeared in the ledger
  before. A grep found "Flow Engineering", "Charter Space", "LIHTC", "voucher" and "housing authority" zero times in
  the 466 prior entries.
- **A category's age was checked first.** It predicted most kills again. Voucher administration (Nan McKay, Quadel),
  hospital purchasing (GHX, Medline) and technology-performance insurance (Munich Re) are all old categories, and
  each of their flanks was held.
- **The "who must sign for it" question produced the survivor.** Flow Engineering's agents check a contractor's
  hardware against its requirements. The government must sign off on that same hardware, and its own reviewer is a
  seat the contractor's tool vendor is legally barred from.

**The finding worth carrying forward: conflict-of-interest law is a wedge class this ledger had not used.** Earlier
runs used delegated authority (ODA, municipal advisor, certifier seats) and channel conflict. FAR 9.5
organisational-conflict-of-interest rules are a third and harder form. A regulation does not merely make it awkward
for the leader to serve both sides. It *disqualifies* the leader from the reviewer seat when it serves the reviewed
party. Any AI leader that sells to regulated contractors has a mirror-image seat on the government side that it
cannot take.

## Surviving ideas

### 1. ts-0470 — The AI-native SETA firm: own the government's technical-review seat on defense hardware programs that the contractors' AI tools are legally barred from

**In plain English.** When the military buys a new rocket, drone or satellite, the company building it hands over
mountains of engineering documents that claim the design meets every requirement. Someone on the government's side
has to read all of it and say whether that's true before more money is released. The government has fewer and fewer
engineers to do that reading, and the old consulting firms that help bill by the hour. We'd be that independent
reviewer, using AI to read and cross-check everything in days rather than months. We'd be paid by the program
office, which right now is approving hardware it hasn't had time to check.

- **The proven pattern:** Flow Engineering raised a 50M dollar Series B at 750M dollars (2026-09-30). Its agents keep
  hardware requirements, CAD and test evidence in sync for Anduril, Rivian, Stoke and others, and Rivian went from
  40 to 1,500 users in seven months.
- **Who buys the narrowed version:** chief engineers and program managers in under-staffed program offices that buy
  from fast-moving, non-traditional vendors: Space RCO, SDA, Army and Air Force portfolio acquisition executive
  offices, and DIU-transitioned programs. The trigger is a design review or milestone decision with a contractor data
  package too big to review.
- **Why the leader can't follow:** under FAR 9.505, a vendor whose customers are the contractors under review has an
  impaired-objectivity conflict of interest, which usually disqualifies it from the government's reviewer seat. The
  incumbent SETA firms bill staff-hours, so a machine-speed review cannibalises their revenue.
- **Expansion path:** SBIR Phase I/II leads to sole-source Phase III and small-business SETA task orders. From there
  it expands across DoD's market of tens of billions of dollars in advisory-and-assistance services (Booz Allen alone
  has about 12bn dollars of revenue), then into NASA, DOE and FAA verification work, then into the owner's-engineer
  seat for utilities, hyperscalers and transit agencies.
- **Scores:** Wedge 4 · Pattern 4 · Market 4 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 4 → composite **4.0**
- **Biggest risk:** the buyer still pays by labor category on time-and-materials vehicles. If the firm can't win
  fixed-price or outcome-priced task orders, being faster shrinks its revenue, as it would for the incumbents. Facility
  clearance and contract-vehicle access also make the first contract slow.

## Method note for the next run

**One. Mine conflict-of-interest seats on purpose.** For each AI leader that sells to regulated suppliers, ask
which reviewer, auditor or evaluator seat on the buyer or regulator side its customer base disqualifies it from.
Candidates to test, **occupancy first**: Medicaid managed-care external quality review (EQRO, 42 CFR 438.354
independence rules), FERC/NERC compliance auditing for utilities, independent engineers for project-finance lenders,
and NASA IV&V. Skip any that the certifier-seat closure (2026-09-24) already covers.

**Two. Check the leader's own careers page before working a data-model flank.** EliseAI's opening for an "affordable
housing compliance analyst" would have killed ts-0467 in one search.

**Three. Skip for now:** assisted and affordable housing (all flanks), hospital purchasing, technology-performance
insurance, voice-deepfake detection, and agent-channel hotel distribution.
