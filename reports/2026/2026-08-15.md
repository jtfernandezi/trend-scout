# Trend Scout — Report 2026-08-15

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (twenty-eighth run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).

**Outcome:** **2 surviving ideas (composites 4.6 and 4.2, one brief), 16 logged kills, and 2 held-book ideas updated with substantial new evidence.**

Last run's takeaway said the binding constraint is a phone call, not another scan. That is still true, and it is now seven runs old. What this run adds is a *different kind* of candidate — the first one in the ledger that is a transaction rather than a model. Twenty-seven runs produced a book that is almost entirely "read a hard corpus, sell the answer to someone who is deciding." Two of those (ts-0064, ts-0069) already sit on the AI build-out and both sell a risk score to an observer. This run went looking for the place where the same underlying reasoning capability could be pointed at a *market that cannot clear* rather than a decision that needs informing.

The single sharpest pair of facts this run produced: **a generator step-up transformer now averages 144 weeks and data centres alone need roughly 74 GW of three-phase transformer capacity in 2026 — about the same as everything replaced annually across the entire US grid** — and, simultaneously, **data-centre project cancellations more than quadrupled to 25 in 2025, with 30–50% of the 2026 pipeline expected to slip or die and only about a third of announced capacity actually under construction.** Those two facts together mean a large population of paid-for factory positions is stranded in the hands of projects that no longer exist, while other projects are having their energization dates set by the same equipment. They cannot trade, because every unit is engineered-to-order and nobody can quickly answer whether one project's half-built transformer can be made to satisfy another project's site. That question is an engineering-judgement task over a few hundred pages of bespoke specification — and as of the last fortnight it is cheap (DeepSeek V4-Pro's max-effort tier over a 1M-token context, GA 2026-08-12) and fast enough to answer inside a negotiation (GPT-5.6 Sol Ultrafast at a claimed 750 output tokens/second against ~53 standard, previewed 2026-08-13).

## Signals scanned

1. **Frontier reasoning entered interactive latency (2026-08-13).** OpenAI and Cerebras previewed **Ultrafast**, running GPT-5.6 Sol at a vendor-claimed **750 output tokens per second against ~53 on the standard tier — up to 14×**. The speed comes from Cerebras' wafer-scale architecture holding model weights in 44 GB of on-chip SRAM rather than shuttling them from off-chip memory. Limited preview, no published price, no GA date, no model ID. The commercial consequence is not cheaper tokens — it is that a 20,000-token reasoning trace falls from roughly six minutes to under thirty seconds, which moves frontier-grade deliberation out of the batch tier and into live conversations, live negotiations and live control.

2. **Reasoning effort became an explicit dial, and agent competence kept commoditising (2026-08-12).** **DeepSeek V4-Pro 0813** reached GA with a **1M-token context, 384k-token maximum output** and **three effort settings (low / high / max)** for both Pro and Flash, aimed squarely at agentic tool-use — 87.9 on Terminal Bench 2.1, 62.7 on DeepSWE, 61.5 on NL2Repo, with vendor-reported gains up to 49.9 points and no independent verification yet. It leads on coding-style benchmarks and loses to Claude Opus 4.7 on SWE-bench Pro (55.4 vs 64.3). Alibaba shipped Qwen3.8-Max on 2026-08-03 (2.4T sparse MoE, 95B active, 1M context). Five frontier models landed in the first sixteen days of July; models now ship like patches.

3. **The agent platform grew an authorization and locality substrate (July–August 2026).** Claude Opus 5 landed 2026-07-24 with a 1M-token context. August added an **advisor** — a model at least as capable as the agent's own, consultable by the primary thread mid-turn — plus **`inference_geo`** to control where inference physically runs, self-hosted Claude Code environments in public beta, and support for the **MCP 2026-07-28 spec with OAuth/OIDC authorization**. Agents are becoming authenticated principals with a delegation chain and a jurisdiction.

4. **YC's Fall 2026 RFS is a physical-world list.** Thirteen requests: The Primer (tutoring), The Future of American Defense (requested by the sitting Secretary of the Army), A Cloud for Small Software, Multiplayer AI, **Compute at Sea**, AI-Powered Consumer Products for 1 Billion People, **AI for the Aging Population**, **New Operating Systems for the Physical World**, The Best Time to Build in Crypto, **Data for the Real World**, Proving You're Human, AI-Native Compliance Infrastructure, Self-Maintaining APIs. Applications closed 2026-07-27, decisions 2026-08-28, batch runs October–December in SF.

5. **Capital committed decisively to owned physical assets in the first week of August.** **Hadrian** raised **$1.37B Series D at $7.87B** on 2026-08-06 — co-led by WCM, Washington Harbour, Valor, 137 Ventures and Baillie Gifford, with JPMorganChase's Security and Resiliency Initiative anchoring — for highly automated domestic factories, now just under 3M sq ft across four sites including a 2.2M sq ft plant in Cherokee, Alabama making submarine components, and expanding into munitions and autonomous systems. **Base Power** raised **$1B at $13B** on 2026-08-03, with 23,000+ home batteries installed, roughly **100 installs a day (~8 MWh)** and a plan to double that by year-end. Valar Atomics raised $1B. Five deals cleared $4.6B in one week.

6. **The grid cannot allocate, and the queue is now the market.** A **2,600 GW national interconnection backlog** with median time to commercial operation approaching five years. **ERCOT is working a 438 GW large-load queue, 87% of it data centres**, with 198 GW of large load applying in Q1 2026 alone; it has approved an initial large-load interconnection process with a **curtailment fast lane**. PJM built BYOG credits and DR pathways, and a **DOE emergency order dated 2026-05-18** lets PJM curtail data centres with backup generation. Roughly **30% of anticipated data-centre energy capacity is now expected to come from on-site generation**. The live argument in the trade press is whether data-centre flexibility is a "regulatory fiction."

7. **Electrical equipment is the binding constraint, and the industry disagrees about why.** Switchgear lead times moved **beyond 60 weeks from an average of 44 in late 2025**; standard power transformers average **128 weeks**, generator step-up transformers **144 weeks**, high-capacity units up to **four years**. **GSU demand rose 274% and substation transformer demand 116% between 2019 and 2025.** Data centres alone need roughly **74 GW of three-phase transformer capacity in 2026 — about the entire annual US grid replacement volume.** Manufacturers hold a production slot for **10–30% of equipment cost**, and without a reservation nominal lead time extends a further 8–16 weeks. About **$2B** of North American capacity expansion (Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy, Prolec GE's $300M+, Virginia Transformer's $40M Georgia expansion) does not arrive until **2028**. And a real fight is running about the root cause: several market participants argue there is **no true manufacturing shortage — that procurement practice is the bottleneck**.

8. **The 2026 data-centre pipeline is visibly failing to convert.** Project cancellations **more than quadrupled to 25 in 2025 from six in 2024**; **30–50% of large data centres scheduled to come online this year are expected to be delayed or cancelled**; only about a third of announced capacity is actually under construction. Named causes, in order: electrical equipment shortages, power access, and public opposition — **at least 188 local opposition groups across 40 states**.

9. **The professions' pipeline collapsed while private equity rolled them up.** Accounting degree completions fell roughly **30% in a decade, from ~79,000 in 2014–15 to 55,152 in 2023–24**; **82% of public accounting firms report significant retention problems**, with the three-to-seven-year cohort leaving for industry. Fewer than **200 PE platform investments produced roughly 900 subsequent roll-up transactions in 2025**, and the AICPA is actively re-examining whether independence rules survive PE-backed structures. Separately, current US workforce reductions are being made by *profitable* firms and explicitly target middle management, developers, finance and administrative roles with AI named as the driver.

10. **Farm labour completed its conversion from employment to procurement.** H-2A approvals jumped **25% to over 400,000 in FY2025**, with **more than 250,000 positions certified in the first half of FY2026** — ahead of every prior first half on record — and DOL projecting **550,000 by 2030**. An end-2025 rule changed the wage data source and, for the first time, split H-2A into **two wage categories with skill-level-based rates** and a reduction where housing is provided. Estimated 2026 crop losses: **$5–7B in the California Central Valley, $1.5B in Florida, $800M in New York and the Pacific Northwest.** Harvest automation ranked as growers' highest-priority technology investment.

11. **Home health demand is three times its served supply.** About **12 million Medicare beneficiaries are eligible for home health on an ongoing basis; only ~4 million used it last year**, and **over 25% of referred patients are turned away by providers** on staffing grounds, with Medicare Advantage prior authorisation cutting visit counts and functional outcomes further.

12. **A $50B federal pot landed that may not be spent on care.** CMS awarded the **Rural Health Transformation Program — $50B across all 50 states, $10B a year 2026–2030**, averaging **~$200M per state** in 2026 (Texas $281.3M highest, New Jersey $147M lowest), against **417 rural hospitals vulnerable to closure and 41.2% operating in the red**. States may use **at most 15%** of the money to pay providers for patient care.

13. **AI-designed medicine got its first efficacy readout.** Insilico's **rentosertib (ISM001-055)** produced positive Phase 2a results in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis — **98.4 mL FVC improvement at 60 mg against a 20.3 mL placebo decline over 12 weeks** — the first controlled human demonstration of both safety and efficacy for an AI-designed molecule against an AI-discovered target. **173 AI-discovered programmes** are in clinical development with 15–20 entering pivotal trials in 2026, and reported Phase I success is running **80–90% against roughly half that historically**.

14. **Enterprise agents still stall on plumbing, not intelligence.** **88% of agent pilots never reach production**; **74% of enterprises have already rolled back or shut down a customer-facing agent** after a governance failure; only 21% have a mature governance model. The named blockers are identity and authentication (agents have no phone, fingerprint or session cookie), isolation, data residency and observability — not model capability.

15. **Golden Dome consolidated onto one vendor.** SpaceX took **$4.16B** for the space-based airborne moving-target-indicator layer, **$2.29B** for relay communications and **$1.6B** for all 18 SBST launches, while SDA awarded **$1.75B for 36 tracking satellites** — and ULA and Blue Origin remain indefinitely grounded with no return-to-flight dates.

16. **Nuclear's regulatory basis was rewritten.** **10 CFR Part 53** — risk-informed and technology-inclusive — took effect **2026-04-29**, and **EO 14300** requires the NRC to reach final licensing decisions within **18 months** for new reactors and roughly 12 for renewals.

## Surviving ideas

Two, and they are honestly related: they rest on the same technical claim — *a machine can now decide whether one piece of engineered-to-order equipment can satisfy a different buyer's bespoke specification* — pointed at two different companies. One is a venue with a take rate; the other is a factory with an inventory position. Different buyers, different capital, different risk. The operator should pick, not blend.

### 1. Slot Exchange — a clearing market for long-lead electrical equipment positions, cleared by a machine that reads specifications

**In plain English.** The big electrical equipment that connects a factory or a data centre to the power grid — transformers the size of a small house — now takes two to three years to get, and the only way to get one is to put money down years early to hold a place in a factory's build queue. At the same time, somewhere between a third and a half of the data centres planned for this year are being cancelled or delayed, so a lot of companies are sitting on places in the queue they no longer need, while other companies are stuck waiting years for the same thing. They can't trade with each other, because every one of these machines is custom-built to the buyer's own hundred-page rulebook, and nobody can tell quickly whether one company's half-built unit could be adapted to another company's site. This is a marketplace that answers that question in minutes instead of months, and takes a cut of every trade.

**Who buys it.** Beachhead: the development or procurement lead at a data-centre or industrial developer the week their project goes on hold or their lease commencement moves — they are holding a paid slot reservation worth 10–30% of equipment cost plus a delivery position they can no longer use. On the other side: any developer, colo operator or utility whose energization date is currently being set by a 128–144 week transformer.

**Why no one already serves them.** The existing secondary market (Sunbelt Solomon, Transformer Exchange, Machinio, Demo Dynasty) is a used-and-surplus business that buys plant-closure lots and sells what it physically holds — it has no relationship with factory queues; the software layer says so in print, with Build.inc positioning itself as visibility and decision support and stating that "AI cannot manufacture transformers" and that human judgement remains central to negotiation; the OEMs are structurally hostile to a venue that prices their backlog against them; and the buyers can't build it themselves because the asset only exists in a pool of competitors who will show a neutral third party what they will never show each other.

**Scores.** Novelty 4.5 · Why-now 5 · Market 5 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4.5 · Founder-fit 4.5 → **composite 4.6**

**Biggest risk.** Assignability. OEM consent, utility standards approval and site re-permitting can each block a transfer, and if consent is routinely withheld the market never clears no matter how good the matching engine is — the first month of work is a contract review, not a model.

### 2. The make-to-stock transformer company — standardisation proved by machine, so grid equipment can be inventoried instead of engineered

**In plain English.** Every large electrical transformer in America is made to order, because each utility has its own decades-old list of requirements, so nothing can be built in advance and kept on a shelf — which is the main reason the wait is now two to three years. This company reads every utility's requirement list, works out the small handful of standard designs that would satisfy almost all of them, builds a factory that makes only those in volume, and keeps them in stock. Customers who currently wait three years get one in weeks, and will pay a great deal for that.

**Who buys it.** Beachhead: utilities, municipal utilities and rural co-ops buying distribution and medium-power units — where units are already documented as substitutable and where the political pressure is worst, because data-centre orders are crowding ordinary storm-replacement and new-housing connections out of the same factory queue. Second: behind-the-meter developers quoted 144 weeks for a step-up transformer they need to energize on-site generation.

**Why no one already serves them.** The four domestic OEMs are adding engineered-to-order capacity, not changing the model — ETO is where their margin, their application-engineering moat and their lock-in live, and standardising commoditises them. Utility standards groups have attempted joint specification for decades and stall at the same place every time: nobody can prove a common design satisfies each member's accumulated clauses, which is exactly the artifact a model can now produce. Hyperscale Power's bet is solid-state physics, not fungibility.

**Scores.** Novelty 4 · Why-now 4.5 · Market 5 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 3.5 → **composite 4.2**

**Biggest risk.** Capital intensity crossed with qualification time — utilities qualify new transformer suppliers over years, and a factory built against a model's coverage estimate is an expensive way to be wrong about which clauses were ritual.

## Held book — two updates with real evidence

A new load-bearing driver appeared: electrical equipment is now an independent, shared, *correlating* constraint on energization rather than a footnote to it.
- **ts-0069 (correlated construction-delay / time-to-power) — the peril got larger and better documented.** Equipment is now named as the most common single cause of data-centre delay, and because the whole industry queues at the same four manufacturers, equipment delay is close to perfectly correlated across a book — precisely the accumulation this idea exists to price. Note the adjacency: ts-0202 *trades* the positions ts-0069 prices the delay risk of. A founder could do either, not both.

## What this run establishes

1. **The ledger's dominant idea shape has a blind spot, and it is transactions.** Twenty-seven runs produced a book of models sold to observers — score the risk, price the exposure, rank the probability. Two of them (ts-0064, ts-0069) sit on this exact build-out and both sell an answer to somebody watching. The idea that survived best this run is the first that *moves an asset* and earns on the move. That is worth treating as a standing prompt, not a one-off: for any corpus this project identifies as newly machine-readable, ask whether the reasoning enables a market to clear, not just a decision to be informed.
2. **A blocked idea can name an unblocked one.** ts-0218 died because ISOs deliberately made queue positions non-transferable. That kill is what surfaced ts-0202: the equipment position is the same kind of scarce right, one link downstream, and it is an ordinary private commercial contract rather than a regulator-issued privilege. Kills are not only a duplicate filter; read as a set, they map where the transferable rights are.
3. **"Is it a shortage or a procurement problem?" is the most valuable open question in the build-out.** Practitioners argue in print that transformer manufacturing capacity is adequate and that procurement behaviour is the bottleneck — buyers hedge with duplicate speculative reservations, OEMs cannot tell a hedge from a real order, so they quote scarcity, which makes buyers hedge harder. That is the same phantom-demand bullwhip ts-0064 identified in the interconnection queue, one layer down in the physical supply chain, and it is an information failure a neutral party can actually fix.
4. **Three pre-filters held and should stay.** No idea whose why-now is only a rule or a funding date (killed ts-0209, ts-0215, ts-0216). No idea where the platform owner ships the primitive (ts-0204, ts-0210). No idea where a government-funded technical programme or a funded first mover already occupies the square (ts-0207, ts-0208, ts-0211, ts-0212, ts-0219).

## Takeaway for next run

1. **ts-0202 has one question, and it is answerable in a week by reading paper, not by scanning.** Are electrical equipment purchase orders and slot reservations assignable, and on what terms? Pull three actual transformer purchase agreements and three slot reservation agreements and find the assignment, novation and OEM-consent clauses. If assignment requires OEM consent that is routinely withheld, the venue is dead and the company is the ts-0203 factory instead. If it is consent-not-unreasonably-withheld, or if reservations are freely transferable, the venue is live. **That is a contract review, not a research pass, and it decides which of this run's two survivors is real.**
2. **The phone call is still the constraint, and it is now seven runs old.** Eight candidates now wait on a conversation with a real buyer: a sustaining-engineering lead at a Tier-1, a litigation-support director at a trucking firm, a head of quality at an aerostructures supplier, a VP of operations at a pork fabrication plant, and now a data-centre development lead holding a stranded transformer position. Twenty-eight runs of desk research produced them. A twenty-ninth will not validate any of them.
3. **Adopt the transaction pre-prompt.** For every future cross, after establishing that a corpus is newly machine-readable, ask explicitly: does this let a market clear, or only a decision get informed? The second is a model sold to an observer, and this ledger already has a dozen. The first is rarer and worth more.
