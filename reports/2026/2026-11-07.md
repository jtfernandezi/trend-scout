**Patterns worked and whether they are exhausted:**
- **Insurer-of-record is now partly mined.** It produced survivors on two consecutive runs (ts-0586, ts-0594), but
  three of this run's four attempts were already taken. Robotics insurance has HDI/Axis, malpractice has Indigo, and
  Clark is moving to bear the risk on its own work. The axis still works where the risk is a **counterparty's
  behaviour that only shows up in documents or video** and the incumbent product is gated by size.
- **Zipline, Pliant and Sand.ai: one attempt each, all closed.** These are horizontal platforms whose partnerships
  (Uber, Visa/Mastercard) or existing competitors already cover the narrower buyer.
- **Rivio: the geography flank is closed.** India was already killed at ts-0503. Its revenue-guarantee mechanism
  (audit the bill, then guarantee the cash) could be tried in another market with a single dominant payer standard
  next run.

## Surviving ideas

### ts-0594 — AI-native subcontractor-default insurer: SDI for the mid-size general contractors that SubGuard won't write, underwritten sub by sub and monitored project by project

**In plain English.** When a big building goes up, the main builder hires dozens of smaller specialist firms
(electricians, plumbers, drywallers). If one goes bust halfway through, the builder has to pay to find and rush in a
replacement. The biggest builders can buy insurance against that, but insurers only sell it to companies doing more
than about 75-150 million dollars of this subcontracted work a year. Mid-size builders get stuck with slow,
expensive bonds or no protection at all. We would be the insurer for the mid-size builders. Our software reads each
specialist firm's finances and the project's paperwork (invoices, schedules, daily site logs) to spot one that is
heading for trouble, so the builder can act early and is paid back when one does fail.

- **The proven pattern:** AI-native insurers that price each risk on its own evidence are winning hard commercial
  lines: Kalepa (75M dollars, revenue tripled), Indigo (premium doubled pricing individual doctors) and Nirvana
  (1.5bn dollars). Agents now read construction paperwork at scale: Shackleton (1bn dollars+ of projects), Bedrock
  (4.1bn dollars) and Document Crunch.
- **Who buys the narrowed version:** CFOs and risk managers at general contractors with 30-150M dollars of annual
  subcontract volume in Sunbelt markets, at the moment a sub default turns into a months-long bond-claim fight, or
  when an owner or lender demands default protection, placed through construction-practice brokers.
- **Why the leader can't follow:** Zurich SubGuard and Arch underwrite the *GC's* prequalification process behind a
  750K dollar+ deductible. Serving mid-size GCs means underwriting and monitoring *every sub*, which is a different
  product and cost structure. Those carriers are also the biggest surety writers, and a cheap mid-market SDI product
  would cannibalise sub-bond premium and antagonise their surety-agent channel. Software vendors (Procore, Oracle
  TradeTapp, Shackleton) would have to become risk-bearers and grade their own subcontractor users.
- **Expansion path:** mid-market GC SDI, then nationwide SDI including large-GC programmes, then sub-side surety, GL
  and payment financing priced on the cross-GC subcontractor performance graph, then builders risk and owner-side
  products. The end state is a construction-risk carrier in a market with about 2 trillion dollars a year of US
  construction spend.
- **Scores:** Wedge 3 · Pattern 4 · Market 4 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 4 → composite 3.8.
- **Biggest risk:** sub default is cyclical and correlated (one downturn hits every book at once), so fronting and
  reinsurance capacity is hard to win. A GC also has every incentive to enrol its weakest subs, so adverse selection
  must be priced from day one.

## Method note for the next run

**One. Insurer-of-record needs a size gate to find openings.** Both survivors share a shape. The incumbent risk
product exists only above a size threshold (SDI's 75M dollar+ floor) or is priced on proxies (ZIP-code crime scores),
because per-unit underwriting used to be uneconomic. Look for other insurance or credit products with explicit
minimums, such as captive programmes, wrap-ups (OCIP/CCIP), trade-credit insurance, warranty and indemnity cover for
small M&A deals, and representations and warranties insurance below 20M dollars of deal value.

**Two. Verify ts-0594's two weakest facts first.** Check whether any SDI carrier (Zurich, Arch, Berkley, Liberty,
Markel) launched a lower-threshold programme in 2025-26. Ask a construction broker for the real SDI rate as a
percentage of enrolled subcontract value (this run used a 0.5-1.5% range), and check whether Procore's API terms
allow a third party to read pay applications for underwriting.

**Three. Skip for now:** everything on the 2026-10-13 to 2026-11-05 lists, plus Zipline, Pliant, Sand.ai, Vera
Health, Clark (except as pattern evidence), Bedrock, Harbinger, Humble Robotics and Ocient. Also skip India hospital
claims, accountants' malpractice, robotics insurance, per-physician malpractice and agent payment rails. YC Fall 2026
Demo Day is 2026-12-02.
