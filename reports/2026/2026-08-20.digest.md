*Trend Scout — 20 Aug 2026*

**2 survivors, 15 kills.** This run went to six shocks the book has never touched: every Colorado River rule expiring on 31 December, a unanimous Supreme Court decision that created a brand-new liability for a whole industry, record US farm debt, the property tax revolt, the entry-level hiring collapse, and China absorbing Europe's car plants.

**1. Firm water — composite 4.7**
In the West, water is owned separately from land, but proving you're allowed to move it takes years and hundreds of thousands in lawyers and hydrologists — so only giant deals ever happen. Satellites can now measure exactly how much water each field used, and the government just accepted that measurement as official. This buys or rents water from thousands of small farmers at once and sells a city or a factory a guarantee: this many gallons, this price, ten years — which matters because in Arizona you can't get permission to build at all without proving a hundred-year supply, and farmers there are being cut 15–35% in January.

**2. Underwritten carrier selection — composite 4.3**
When a middleman picks which trucking firm hauls your freight, the Supreme Court ruled in May that he can now be sued if that truck kills someone — a defence the industry relied on for thirty years vanished overnight, and truck verdicts routinely run into tens of millions. Brokers make thousands of these picks a week and most of their insurance specifically won't cover it. This reads every trucker's full safety history, decides in real time whether a given firm is a defensible choice, and — unlike the score vendors, who all disclaim liability — signs its name to the decision and pays the claim if it's wrong.

Both survivors are the same shape, and that's the finding: **a court or regulator involuntarily created a risk, and frontier AI is the only thing that lets a balance sheet take the other side of it.** The best kill makes the same point in reverse — a genuinely novel, unoccupied property tax guarantee, killed purely because the risk is perfectly correlated across every parcel in a state. A good model doesn't make a risk insurable; independence does.
