*1 survivor, 14 kills.*

*1. Fix the chair first — the AI-native complex rehab and home mobility company (composite 4.2)*
Hundreds of thousands of Americans can't leave the house without a powered wheelchair, and when one breaks they wait months, because the two big companies that sell these chairs make their money sending a technician on a truck and a new custom chair takes two to five months anyway — so we diagnose the broken chair from the user's photos and a short video, ship the right part or send a local technician the same week, insurance pays as it always has, and then we're the company that builds their next chair, designed from video and printed to fit. *For:* wheelchair users and their families, and the rehab hospitals with nobody to promise them a date.

*Why it survived when nothing else did:* the nearest AI-native player in custom seating (Kalogon, Medicare-coded 3D-printed cushions, funded June 2026) sells *through* Numotion and NSM rather than against them. When the best new entrant takes the supplier position under the incumbent, the operator position is still empty — and here it's empty for a structural reason: the incumbents are levered distributors whose margin is somebody else's catalogue and whose service revenue is billed per truck roll.

*Why now:* three companies funded in twelve months proving AI design + 3D printing + owning the insurance claim collapses a craft-bound custom device category (Hike Medical $22.5M, CustoMED $6M, Kalogon $5.75M), and separately over $500M into AI medical billing this year — Candid Health's $120M Series D came with 190% year-on-year growth.

*Notable kills:* autonomous billing for workers' comp (DaisyBill got there first), dental (Overjet), self-funded employer claim audit (Cotiviti and five others), agent security for enterprises (the most crowded square in AI right now), rural value-based care (Aledade is literally this), and driverless paratransit (removing the driver removes the wrong cost — the job is securing a wheelchair, not steering).

*The pattern in the kills:* nine of fourteen died because someone funded was already doing the exact same re-point. That's the opposite of recent runs, where ideas died because the market leader could copy them. When a mechanism is genuinely good, the same clever narrowing occurs to several teams at once.
