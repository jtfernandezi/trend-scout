# Trend Scout — Report 2026-10-17

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (fifty-fourth run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).
**Outcome:** **Zero surviving ideas. 8 new kills (ts-0518 to ts-0525).** No brief this run. **One held entry
has new evidence:** ts-0424 (AI-native FAA certification house) moves from `new` to `watching`.

This run followed the 2026-10-15 method note:

- **Non-health traction first.** No pattern this run came from health operations. Patterns came from
  certification, insurance, energy, B2B payments, fintech and construction robotics. Scarlet's customers are
  medical-device makers, but every flank of it was pointed at non-health buyers apart from ts-0519 (IVDR).
- **Leader roadmap checked before each adjacent flank** (note two). This changed one decision: Scarlet says the
  round funds "broadening certification services", which counted against ts-0519.
- **Mandate seats sized before the wedge** (note one). This decided ts-0520 (CRA) and ts-0525 (driver-assistance
  approval). Both have real conflict-of-interest blocks, but forced volume is small.
- **Regional direct fetches ran first.** DealStreetAsia had RapidCanvas (29M dollar Series B, enterprise AI apps,
  no traction disclosed), Li Auto's chip unit and repeats from last run. Inc42's weekly roundup headline named
  Boldfit and Origin. Origin is the only AI-native regional name, and it is scanned below. This is the fourth
  run running with no regional AI-native breakout that has hard traction.
- **Two names that 2026-10-13 could not verify are now confirmed.** Causa Prima (Fortune, tech.eu) and Scarlet
  (tech.eu, Dealroom) were listed on startups.gallery but not checked then.

**The finding worth carrying forward: "AI-native certifier" is now a funded category, and its flanks die on
incumbent assessors.** Scarlet (50M dollar Series B) and AIUC (40M dollar Series A in September) show investors
paying for AI-first bodies that hold an approval seat. I pointed the mechanism at four more regimes: EU
machinery and robots, in-vitro diagnostics, the Cyber Resilience Act, and driver-assistance type approval. In all
four, the incumbent testing, inspection and certification bodies are already designated or are marketing the
service: TUV SUD, TUV Rheinland, SGS Brightsight, Applus, IDIADA. They charge per file or per review day and are
short of capacity, so automation raises their throughput and they gain from buying it. This is the ts-0435
(CMMC) lesson again.

The only certifier seat where the block survives is one where the natural holders cannot or do not compete for
third-party work. ts-0424 (FAA ODA) is that case, because ODA holders are mostly OEMs certifying their own
aircraft and tool vendors cannot sign. That is why it gets the evidence update and the four new flanks do not.

## Breakout startups scanned

1. **Scarlet**: **50M dollar Series B led by Headline, 2026-10-12** (Notion, Atomico, Creandum), more than 75M
   dollars raised in total. It holds EU MDR notified-body, UK approved-body and FDA third-party review status, and
   is the only one of about 52 EU notified bodies that specialises in software and AI. Named customers: Flo
   Health, DeepHealth, NHS England, Skin Analytics, Heidi, Tandem Health. It cut certification by a year or more
   (company-reported). **Mechanism:** a certification body built AI-first holds the regulated approval seat and
   reviews technical files far faster than legacy bodies.
2. **Stand**: **70M dollar Series C at 1bn dollars, 2026-10-15** (Saga Ventures, Lowercarbon, Eclipse). **Insured
   property value grew from about 1bn to 10bn dollars in a year**, and it hired a former Nephila executive as CFO.
   **Mechanism:** a physics simulation of each structure prices wildfire risk the admitted market abandoned, and
   specific mitigation becomes a condition of cover.
3. **Causa Prima**: **20M dollar seed led by Base10, announced 2026-10-13**, three months after a 10M dollar
   pre-seed (QED, FJ Labs, Creandum). Revenue is not disclosed, so this is the weakest traction on the list.
   **Mechanism:** agents on each side of a supplier relationship negotiate payment terms and settle billing
   disputes, and the platform takes a cut when early-payment deals clear.
4. **Voltus**: **225M dollar Series D, 2026-10-08** (Generation, Activate, Vitol). **8.5GW on the platform**,
   targeting 20GW by 2030, with a three-year Google agreement (June) and an Octopus BYOC partnership.
   **Mechanism:** a virtual power plant's accredited capacity offsets a data centre's load, so it gets
   interconnected sooner.
5. **Ramp**: **1.85bn dollars at a 60bn dollar valuation, 2026-10-15** (Dragoneer). **Annualised revenue above
   2bn dollars**, and the valuation is up 16bn dollars in about four months. **Mechanism:** a corporate card and
   bill-pay rail with agents doing finance-team work on top. **Not re-pointed:** agent spend controls (ts-0021)
   and the obvious segment flanks are occupied (Pleo, Payhawk and Spendesk in Europe; Clara in Latin America).
6. **AIUC**: **40M dollar Series A led by Ribbit** (September 2026). AIUC-1 is a certification standard plus
   insurance for enterprise AI agents. It is outside the two-week window, but it makes the certifier category a
   cluster rather than one company. **Not re-pointed:** agent certification was closed at ts-0357, and
   evaluation is on the skip list.
7. **Origin**: **20M dollar seed** (Accel, Zacua; San Francisco and Bengaluru). It has pilots with two of the
   largest US drywall-finishing contractors and targets 150 deployments by 2027. **Mechanism:** learned policies
   drive a finishing robot that applies compound, sands and primes. **Not re-pointed:** it has pilots, not paid
   deployments, and Canvas already holds drywall finishing.

Also scanned and not re-pointed:
- **TypeSafe AI**: 870M dollar Series A at 7.5bn dollars (a16z), three weeks after launch. It is a horizontal
  model, and its traction claim (a third of the Fortune 500) is unverified.
- **Capitolis**: 120M dollar Series E (Citi). It optimises bank capital, which is not AI-native, and the round
  funds an acquisition.
- **Hone**: 60M dollar seed (Benchmark). No traction.
- **Modal**, **Arena**, **Albert Invent** and **Suki** were scanned on earlier runs.

  the natural holders do not compete for third-party work (ts-0424). Combined with ts-0435, ts-0436, ts-0437 and
  ts-0357, the ledger has now tried nine certifier seats.

  sign.
- **Causa Prima: no durable flank.** A seed-stage leader can follow into any adjacent dispute type, and the
  domain-specific dispute markets (freight, retail deductions at ts-0412) have specialists who own the data.

## Method note for the next run

**One. For certifier and assessor seats, ask who naturally holds the seat today and whether they compete for
third-party work.** If the holders are independent bodies that charge per file and are short of capacity
(notified bodies, testing labs, C3PAOs, CPA firms), they buy automation and the seat dies. Five certifier kills in
two runs were killed this way. Seats survive only when the natural holders are the regulated parties themselves
(OEM ODAs) or tool vendors who cannot sign.

**Two. Stop treating "several raises for the same mechanism" as a reason to re-point.** Do it only after checking
the second-tier names. This week Scarlet and AIUC formed a cluster, but the incumbents (TUV, SGS, UL) were
already the deeper occupants. Check the incumbents of the regulatory regime before counting startups.

**Three. The non-health tape this week was thin on Series A/B AI-native breakouts with disclosed revenue.** Most
large rounds were horizontal (TypeSafe, Modal, Labelbox, Hone) or not AI-native (Capitolis, ICON, Scanntech).
Next run, also scan funding trackers for Series A rounds with disclosed ARR in industrial and energy operations
(Gigaton, Fyld and Gridsight were last seen months ago and could be checked for new milestones).

**Four. Skip for now:** everything from the 2026-10-08 to 2026-10-15 lists, plus notified-body and
type-approval seats (machinery, IVDR, CRA, automotive), wildfire and catastrophe MGAs, VPP and data-centre
capacity flanks, and freight or retail dispute agents.
