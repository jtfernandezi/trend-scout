**Trend Scout — 2026-08-06**

**0 survivors, 9 kills.** Second zero-survivor run in a row.

I changed the method this run: instead of starting from a new AI capability and hunting for an industry, I started from the biggest structural dislocations of 2026 that this project has never touched — the Iran war and the closed Strait of Hormuz, the collapse of marine war-risk insurance, the revolt against data centres, the utility bill revolt, the fertiliser shock. All genuinely new to the ledger. All nine ideas I built from them were already occupied.

**Why they died — and it's the same reason each time.** Seven of nine were killed by a vendor's own marketing page, and in three cases that page was written about the exact 2026 event I was crossing on. Icertis has a blog post about force majeure and the Strait of Hormuz. Windward has one called "Pricing Risk in a Changed Ocean." Transect already sells "community sentiment" as a product feature. The lesson: a crisis being new to me doesn't make it new to the software companies who were already selling into that industry when it hit. They wrote the blog post within weeks. **Both of my search directions are now exhausted.**

**The one fact worth keeping.** $86bn of data-centre construction this year needs about 296,700 workers — roughly **85% of the entire US construction industry's new-worker need for 2026**. The AI build-out has effectively bought up the country's spare building labour, and every house, hospital and factory is now bidding against it. I couldn't find a product in that today, but it's the best raw material this run produced.

**The good news is in the held book, which got better without any new searching.** The memory-chip shortage behind idea ts-0141 hardened from a price spike into a consensus two-year problem — memory prices up ~700% in a year, Intel's CEO saying "no relief until 2028," and Apple raising prices across its whole product line in June. And the litigation idea ts-0133 got stronger too: US courts handed down 135 "nuclear" verdicts in 2024, up 52%, totalling over $31bn.

**Recommendation for next run:** stop scanning. Pick one of the two held ideas and spend the whole session on the technical argument a real buyer would have to disagree with. Four candidates in twenty-four runs are now waiting on a phone call, not on more desk research.
