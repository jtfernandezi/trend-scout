# Trend Scout — Report 2026-10-03

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (forty-eighth run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).
**Outcome:** **Zero surviving ideas. 8 new kills (ts-0475 to ts-0482).** No brief this run.

This run followed the 2026-10-01 method note:

- **Conflict-of-interest seats were mined on purpose.** Six of the eight candidates were built as "the reviewer seat
  the leader's customers disqualify it from". They covered mortgage securitization review, Medicaid secret-shopper
  surveys, the lender's independent engineer, independent model evaluation, AML independent testing and judges' AI.
  None survived.
- **The note's named seats were checked first.** EQRO and the No Surprises Act IDR seat had both effectively died
  already under the "granting body sets the price" rule (ts-0438). NERC CIP audits are run by the regional entities
  themselves, so there is no third-party seat. NASA IV&V is a small in-house facility and is close to ts-0470. That
  left only the independent engineer to test, and it died (ts-0478).
- **The leader's own materials were checked before working a flank.** This killed ts-0481 straight away: Clio bought
  Learned Hand, the judges' AI, on 2026-09-30.

**The finding worth carrying forward: a conflict-of-interest seat is only a wedge if two things are both true.** The
SETA seat (ts-0470) has both. Today's six seats each had at most one.

1. **The conflict must be enforced with disqualification by the buyer or a regulator,** not just named in a rule.
   Courts happily buy from Thomson Reuters, LexisNexis and now Clio, all of which also serve litigants (ts-0481). FAR
   9.5 is enforced by contracting officers. Judicial ethics, in practice, is not.
2. **The incumbents in the seat must bill in a way that AI erodes.** SETA incumbents bill time and materials by labor
   category, so automation cuts their revenue. Securitization review (ts-0476) is priced per loan, and the lender's
   engineer (ts-0478) and AML testing (ts-0480) by fixed fee per engagement. In all three, automation raises the
   incumbent's margin, so the incumbent adopts AI instead of losing to it. The same failure as contingency pricing in
   ts-0447.

Where a seat passes both tests, the occupancy search still applies. Independent model evaluation passed both and was
taken by Vals AI (ts-0479).

## Breakout startups scanned

1. **Armadin**: **255.5M dollar Series B at 2.5bn dollars+, 2026-10-01** (a16z, Accel; In-Q-Tel and GV existing),
   seven months after launch. Runs agentic attack campaigns in production for Fortune 500 and government customers.
   **Mechanism:** swarms of agents attack a customer's own infrastructure continuously, because models can now chain
   exploits the way a human red team does.
2. **Halluminate**: **30M dollar Series A, 2026-10-01** (Oak HC/FT). **Nine people, profitable at a mid-eight-figure
   run rate, and four of the five top US closed-model labs pay it.** Proximal passed 200M dollars of annualised revenue
   on coding environments in the same category. **Mechanism:** expert-built simulated workplaces and graders that labs
   train agents in, for jobs where public data runs out.
3. **Chamelio**: **26M dollar Series A, 2026-09-22**, five months after its seed. **ARR up 4x**; hundreds of in-house
   legal customers including Wiz and monday.com. **Mechanism:** agents do in-house legal work rather than just store
   contracts. Same cluster: **Arceus** (17M dollar Series A, AI-native law firm with a flat 500 dollar contract review)
   and Harvey (15.5bn dollars).
4. **Kahua**: **~250M dollars from Bain Capital at 1bn dollars+, 2026-09-29.** 2,500 clients, including the Port
   Authority of NY and NJ, running 400bn dollars of capital projects. **Mechanism:** one system of record and AI layer
   for owners running multi-party megaprojects.
5. **Bretton AI** (formerly Greenlite; 75M dollar Series B in February, revisited as an AML pattern): **1.2M+
   investigations completed, average contract 201k dollars, up from 25k dollars at seed.** **Mechanism:** agents do
   the level-1 and level-2 financial-crime investigations that analysts used to do.
6. **Elio Mortgage**: **5.1M dollar pre-seed, 2026-09-29** (Motive Partners, Social Leverage). A licensed live
   brokerage, not a software vendor. **Mechanism:** a licensed operator runs origination on its own agent platform.
   Small, but the clearest new instance of the AI-native licensed-operator pattern.
7. **Inbolt**: **12.5M dollars (34M dollars total), 2026-09-30.** On 200+ robots in 100+ factories (Ford, Stellantis,
   Toyota). **Mechanism:** 3D vision lets already-installed industrial robots adapt to part variation in real time.

## Method note for the next run

**One. Apply the two-question screen before spending a search on a conflict-of-interest seat.** (a) Is the conflict
enforced, with disqualification, by the buyer or a regulator? (b) Do the incumbents in the seat bill by labor hour,
so AI cuts their revenue? Only seats that pass both are worth an occupancy search. Hourly-billed, legally
disqualifying seats to test next: federal acquisition advisory and assistance services outside defense (civilian
agencies, NASA and DOE under FAR 9.5 and DEAR 970.0905), and state Medicaid program-integrity contractors paid on
labor contracts rather than contingency.

**Two. Source outside the US funding tape.** This week's large US rounds were almost all categories this ledger has
already worked. The Japanese, Korean and European long tail (DriveX, Gwanak, Deepslate, Voltaback) produced no
traction numbers this week. Next run, start from Q3 2026 regional funding reports (Europe, India, Japan) and pick
breakouts with disclosed revenue.

**Three. Skip for now:** offensive-security agents (all flanks), judiciary AI, independent model evaluation, AML
independent testing, private-label RMBS due diligence, Medicaid secret-shopper surveys, and the lender's independent
engineer.
