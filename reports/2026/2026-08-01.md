# Trend Scout — Report 2026-08-01

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (twenty-second run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).

**Outcome:** **1 surviving idea, 11 logged kills — the second consecutive run to break the drought, and the largest single-run kill count in the project's history.**

Last run's two operating rules were carried forward and both earned their keep: **lead with the capability and choose the industry for distance**, and **treat any square a platform owner already sells into as pre-dead**. I led with the two freshest capabilities on the board — the July 28-29 cryptanalysis result, in which a model found a novel structural attack that two years of expert human review had missed, and the July convergence of every frontier lab on multi-hour long-horizon agents — and crossed them into eleven industries.

Ten died. Nine of those ten died to a **named occupant found on the first or second search**, which is now the stable base rate. But the one that survived did so through the same door as last run's survivor, and the repetition is worth naming as a rule: **the square was open because the people best placed to build it are the people whose revenue it deletes.** Last run that was expert-witness firms and animation studios. This run it is contract firmware-engineering shops and the semiconductor vendors whose business model is lock-in.

The survivor also comes from the run's most under-discussed signal. Every founder in San Francisco is reading about the AI capex supercycle. Almost nobody is reading what it is doing to the *other* electronics economy: data centres now consume **~70% of all memory chips made in 2026**, against 20-30% in 2022, and the legacy memory that automotive dashboards, industrial controllers and medical devices depend on is being starved out of existence.

## Signals scanned

1. **AI produced a genuinely novel expert result in cryptography — and it changed a standard.** Anthropic published on **2026-07-28** that Claude Mythos Preview found a previously unknown nontrivial automorphism in HAWK's lattice structure — a new attack technique the model named the *Möbius Bridge* — halving the scheme's security level and dropping the cost of recovering its smallest key from 2^64 to 2^38 operations, in **60 hours**, after two years of expert human cryptanalysis had failed. It also improved on the best 7-round AES attack standing since 2013. The HAWK team **withdrew the algorithm from standardisation**. This follows **CryptanalysisBench** (191 tasks, ETH Zurich / Anthropic / Haifa / TU Berlin / Tel Aviv, 2026-07-20). Cost of the result: roughly **$100k in API calls**. *(capability — the freshest thing on the board)*
2. **The same week, a model co-produced a first-rank mathematical result.** On **2026-07-20** Levent Alpöge posted an explicit 216-character counterexample disproving the **Jacobian Conjecture**, open since 1939, found with Claude Fable 5 — immediately formalised in Lean and submitted to DeepMind's Formal Conjectures repo. Read alongside signal 1, the capability class is: **given a formal object, an automatic checker and enough compute, models now find structure that expert humans missed for years.** *(capability)*
3. **Every frontier lab converged on the multi-hour agent in one month.** Grok 4.5, the GPT-5.6 family, Kimi K3, Claude Opus 5 (07-24, near-Fable performance at roughly half the input price) and DeepSeek-V4-Flash (07-31) all target the same thing: carrying a long task from research through code to structured output without losing the plan. Combined with the July 9 price collapse, **verified, empirically-grounded long-horizon work is now cheap**. *(capability)*
4. **The AI buildout is cannibalising the memory supply the rest of the electronics economy runs on.** Data centres take **~70% of all memory chips made in 2026** versus 20-30% in 2022. DRAM supply grows only 16% (NAND 17%) against a historical 20-30% norm. **Q2-2026 conventional DRAM contract prices rose a record 90-95% quarter-on-quarter**; DDR2 rose 55-60% with a further 35-40% expected in Q3; spot DRAM is up ~700% YoY; automotive LPDDR4 +70%. Memory is now **35% of a PC's bill of materials**, up from 15-18%. Smartphone shipments are forecast to fall **12.9%** to 1.12bn units — the sharpest drop since 2008-09 — and PC makers have raised prices 15-20%. **No meaningful new capacity arrives until late 2027-2028**, and SK Hynix warned on 07-10 that the shortage may persist past 2030. Context: origination under $150k has fallen ~70% in twenty years, and only about a third of homes under $100k are bought with a mortgage versus 80%+ above. *(structural)*
7. **M&A hit a record and the remedy regime changed shape.** Global announced M&A reached **$2.6T in H1 2026**; $5B+ transactions up 44% by count and **148% by value**; megadeals up 57%, headlined by Paramount/Warner Bros at $170B. ## Surviving idea

### ts-0141 — The firmware re-hosting engine: when the AI boom eats the memory the rest of the world runs on

**In plain English.** Almost everything with a plug or an engine — a car's dashboard, a factory controller, an infusion pump, a lift controller — runs on a small memory chip chosen fifteen years ago. Those chips are now disappearing, because the factories that made them have been switched over to making memory for AI data centres, which this year swallow about seventy percent of the world's supply. When a chip is discontinued, the manufacturer has to redesign the product and rewrite the software that runs on it, then prove to a regulator or a customer that the new version behaves exactly like the old one. That job costs between twenty thousand and two million dollars each time, takes a year or more, and is done by hand by contract engineers who are already fully booked. This company builds a machine that does it: hand it the old software and the replacement chip, and it rewrites the software, runs it against the original on real hardware until it can show the behaviour is identical, and produces the paperwork proving it. Manufacturers pay happily because the alternative is a production line that stops.

**Who buys it.** The embedded-software or sustaining-engineering lead at an automotive Tier-1, industrial-controls, medical-device or aerospace manufacturer, at the moment an end-of-life or product-change notification lands on a memory part or microcontroller inside a product with a ten-to-fifteen-year service life and a regulated change-control process — a redesign already budgeted at $20k-$2M that today gets handed to a contract engineering firm.

**Why no one already serves them.** The obsolescence-intelligence vendors (Z2Data's Vituoso, SiliconExpert, PartAnalytics, Accuris, ComponentSense) monetise notification, lifecycle prediction and alternate-part recommendation — their product stops exactly where the engineering starts, and they have no compiler, toolchain or test-harness competence. The embedded toolchain vendors (IAR, Green Hills, Segger, Vector) sell per-architecture seats and have no reason to make silicon interchangeable. The chip vendors' business model *is* lock-in, so portability is strategically hostile to them. And the incumbent solution is contract firmware houses whose entire revenue is the labour the engine deletes — the same structural disqualification that left last run's survivor open. General AI legacy-migration tooling stops at the boundary too: the published state of the art notes plainly that *"testing embedded systems is not straightforward with LLM agents"*, which is precisely the hard part and precisely the moat.

**Scores.** Novelty 4 · Why-now 5 · Market 4 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 5 → **composite 4.3**

**Biggest risk.** Safety-critical firmware is the least forgiving place to put an autonomous agent, and the customer still owns the qualification: the company's real product is *evidence of equivalence*, not code, and if the evidence isn't good enough to survive a regulator or a Tier-1's change board, nothing else matters. A founder should expect the first eighteen months to go into the hardware-in-the-loop verification harness rather than the migration agent, and should expect the first customer to run the engine's output alongside a human team for a full cycle before trusting it.

## What this run establishes

**1. Two runs in a row, the survivor came through the same door — and it is now a rule worth using as a search heuristic, not a post-hoc explanation.** Last run: expert-witness firms and animation studios sell the labour the reconstruction engine deletes. This run: contract firmware houses sell the labour the migration engine deletes, and the parties with the data (chip vendors, obsolescence-intelligence platforms) are *strategically opposed* to the product rather than merely slow. The generalisation: **look for markets whose only current suppliers are billable-hour firms, and whose adjacent data owners have a business reason to not want the problem solved.** That is a much narrower and more productive filter than "find an industry with no AI incumbent," and it is the inverse of the platform-absorption pre-filter — same question, opposite sign.

**2. The freshest capability on the board turned out to be commercially the least useful, and that is itself informative.** The Mythos cryptanalysis result is the most striking AI event of the month and it generated three candidate crosses (ts-0142, ts-0144, ts-0152). All three died, for the *same* reason in different clothes: the domains where "find the novel structural weakness" is valuable are domains with very few buyers, and the defenders in those domains are already sophisticated enough to run the capability themselves and publish it. Novel-capability headlines are not where the companies are. **The company was in the boring second-order consequence of the capex boom — memory allocation — not in the capability headline.**

**3. Nine of eleven kills were "named funded occupant, first search," which is now simply the base rate and should stop being reported as a finding.** What varies between runs is not the hit rate of the kill check but whether the *territory* contains a square that a billable-hour incumbent is structurally unable to enter. Two runs of evidence say that is the thing to search for directly.

**Held book.** Unchanged this run; no re-validation was performed because the whole book was made current on 07-25 and nothing found today bears on any held entry. The commit candidates remain **ts-0048** and **ts-0032** (both briefed, both needing a conversation rather than more desk research), with **ts-0133** as the highest-scoring entry in the ledger at 4.5. **ts-0141** joins as the second-highest at 4.3 and is the most *buildable* of the four — its beachhead requires no regulatory change, no government sales cycle and no counterparty network, only a customer whose production line is about to stop.
