## Breakout startups scanned

1. **Operand**: about **31M dollars across seed and Series A led by Khosla Ventures at a 220M dollar valuation**
   (Axios, 2026-11-14), with Felicis, SV Angel and YC. Keith Rabois joins the board, and Clearlake Capital is a named
   user. No revenue was disclosed. **Mechanism:** agents reconstruct a private company's customers, pricing and
   operating metrics from public records and internet traces, so a PE buyer can do diligence without the seller's
   help.
2. **Spines**: **64M dollars** (a 29M dollar Series A-1 plus a 35M dollar growth facility; Fortune, 2026-11-09). One
   outlet reports a **run rate of about 50M dollars**, earned from authors who pay for production, translation,
   distribution and marketing. **Mechanism:** agents collapse the per-title cost of producing a book, so a publisher
   can release tens of thousands of titles profitably.
3. **Ancep**: **8.5M dollars led by Creandum with Kleiner Perkins** (2026-11-10). One customer division reports revenue
   up 10% within six weeks (company-reported). **Mechanism:** agents make small commercial-insurance placements
   profitable, so brokers stop turning that business away.
4. **Zus Health**: **33M dollar Series B led by Drive Capital** with Sanofi Ventures (2026-11-13), more than 100M
   dollars raised in total. No customer numbers were disclosed. **Mechanism:** national record networks let one party
   assemble a patient's full record, and AI summarises it for value-based care.
5. **Readily** (YC): **11M dollar Series A led by Define Ventures** (October). Revenue is up **10x year on year**
   (company-reported), and its customers cover 25M+ people across 46 states. **Mechanism:** models read the whole
   regulatory feed against an organisation's own policies and flag the gaps. This is the pattern the 2026-11-12 note
   asked us to work.
6. **Lumbra**: **25M dollar Series A co-led by Point72 Ventures and Ansa** (2026-11-10). It has 40+ staff and US
   intelligence-community contracts. **Mechanism:** agent orchestration in which every generated claim traces back to
   source data, for high-stakes classified analysis.
7. **Uforce**: **700M dollars at 6bn dollars** (UK National Wealth Fund and Valor, 2026-11-12), building uncrewed air,
   sea and land vehicles. **Agile Robots** raised **500M dollars at 8.5bn dollars** (SoftBank) the same day.
   **Mechanism:** cheap autonomous platforms with onboard perception replace crewed patrols and crewed work cells.

**Patterns worked and whether they are exhausted:**
- **Operand: two attempts, both closed.** The mechanism is strong: diligence that needs no cooperation from the
  target. But every buyer that also lacks the target's cooperation already has a data incumbent: credit bureaus and
  credit insurers for trade credit, and the economic consultancies for antitrust. **Lesson: "no cooperation from the
  target" is not a moat when the buyer already has a proprietary data source about that target.**
- **Spines: one attempt, closed.** A channel conflict blocked Spines, but the retailer that controls distribution
  (Amazon, through Audible and Kindle) already serves the segment. Note two from the last run caught it before
  any scoring.
- **Ancep, Readily and Lumbra: one attempt each, closed by occupancy.** Each segment already has a funded
  multi-vertical player: Harper for small commercial insurance, Norm Ai for financial-services compliance, and
  Helsing and Palantir for allied intelligence work.
- **Zus: one attempt, closed.** The purpose-of-use block is real (records pulled for treatment cannot be used for
  benefits claims), but Turnout, Mindset Care and VetClaims.ai already serve disability claimants.
- **Uforce: one attempt, closed** by Saildrone and by budgets that depend on donors.

**Meta-signal.** This is the third straight zero-survivor run, and occupancy did nearly all of the killing (8 of 8
this run, 7 of 8 last run). The patterns this week are real. But at Series A and seed, the segments next to each
winner are already funded within 6-12 months. Re-pointing this week's raises finds the gap after someone else has.
The structural axis that produced the last survivors (ts-0586 and ts-0594, insurer-of-record behind a size gate) is
also worked out. See the method note.

## Method note for the next run

**One. Mine from the requirement, not the raise, for one run.** Three zero-survivor runs say the adjacent segments
around fresh raises are already taken. Start next time from a new **mandate, deadline or rule** effective in 2026-27
that creates a buyer with a hard date: an EU rule's application date, a CMS final rule or a state law's effective
date. Then ask which breakout's mechanism serves that buyer. A buyer created by a date is less likely to be occupied
than a segment next to a known winner.

**Two. Check "who already holds proprietary data about the target" before the occupancy search.** It killed both
Operand candidates this run and ts-0605/0606 last run. If an incumbent already holds the data the idea would
reconstruct, kill the idea before searching.

**Three. Demo Day sweep on or after 2026-12-02.** Fall 2026 YC companies will publish numbers then, and the sweep is
the best fresh-pattern source left.

**Four. Skip for now:** Operand, Spines, Ancep, Zus, Readily, Lumbra, Uforce and Agile Robots, the agent-security
cluster (WhiteMatter, Bay, Plurai, Edelbyte), plus everything on the 2026-11-10 and 2026-11-12 skip lists.
