# Trend Scout — Report 2026-09-03

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (thirty-fifth run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).
**Outcome:** **2 surviving ideas (composites 4.3 and 4.2, two briefs), 15 logged kills.** No held-book
entry had new evidence bearing on it, so none were updated.

The 2026-09-01 run ended zero-for-nine and left one instruction for this run: **once you have narrowed
the buyer, ask who the leader is in the narrowed market, and ask it before the config-change test.**
That question was applied to every candidate here, and it is what killed ts-0314 (the city and school
district ledger) after two hours of development — Rillet cannot serve a school district, but a school
district does not buy from Rillet, it buys from Tyler, and Tyler is entrenched rather than blocked.

The kill distribution has now inverted for a third consecutive run: **ten of fifteen kills came from
the occupancy test, four from the config-change test, one from the venture-scale gate.** In eight of
the ten occupancy kills the occupant was funded and shipping the exact thing. The occupancy-first
discipline adopted on 2026-08-29 keeps paying — five candidates (bordereaux, agentic model risk,
statutory books, mine tailings, GMP batch records) died inside a single search each.

**The finding that produced both survivors.** The strongest confirmed pattern this week is not a
company, it is a *shape*: the YC Summer 2026 batch (235 companies, Demo Day 2026-09-10) contains an
explicit cluster of startups positioning as **AI-run firms rather than AI tools for firms** —
accounting practices, law practices, insurance carriers. Outside the batch the same shape is funded
(Casey, Fernstone, Last Accounting Company), and the AI-native ERP cluster is its enterprise cousin.
So the run stopped hunting verticals and started enumerating **licensed professions where the
incumbent bills hours, the software vendor is channel-conflicted, and the deliverable requires a
signature** — then ran occupancy on each. Most were taken: appraisal, patent prosecution, medical
coding, plan review, Phase I environmental, geotechnical. Two were not.

A caveat the operator should hold: both survivors are the same shape. That is a real concentration
risk and the 2026-08-25 report warned about exactly this kind of template-following. The defence is
that the shape is this week's best-evidenced pattern rather than a habit of the book — the ledger has
no prior entry of this form — but the next run should deliberately mine a different mechanism.

## Surviving ideas

### 1. Retracement — the AI-native land survey firm that stamps its own work

**In plain English.** Before anyone can build a solar farm, a data centre, a house or a fibre line,
somebody has to say exactly where the property line is. That answer comes from a licensed surveyor who
reads a stack of old deeds — some of them two hundred years old, written as "thence north-east to the
oak stump by Miller's fence" — works out which old descriptions win when they contradict each other,
then goes and finds the iron pins in the ground. It is slow, expensive expert work, and the country is
running out of the people who can do it: the number of licensed surveyors fell from 56,200 to 47,770 in
a decade and the average one is nearly sixty. We build the company that does the reading and the
reasoning by machine, sends a small crew to confirm what is physically there, and has our own licensed
surveyor sign and take responsibility for the result. Solar developers, data-centre builders and title
companies pay us the same three to eight thousand dollars a site they pay today, and get the answer in
days instead of two months.

**The proven pattern.** The YC Summer 2026 "agent-native professional services" cluster — AI-run
accounting firms, law practices and insurance carriers positioning as the firm, not the tool — with
funded instances outside the batch (Casey, Fernstone, Last Accounting Company); plus Transfyr's $25M
seed led by General Catalyst on 2026-08-26 proving multimodal capture of skilled physical work.

**Who buys the narrowed version.** A solar, storage or data-centre development manager holding a
portfolio of sites, a CRE lender or title agency ordering ALTA/NSPS surveys for closings, or a national
fibre builder — at the moment a closing, permit, interconnection application or construction start is
gated on a survey and the local firm quotes six to ten weeks.

**Why the leader can't follow.** Channel conflict at the vendors and hourly billing at the
practitioners. Trimble, Hexagon/Leica, Topcon and Carlson sell instruments, dealer services and
per-seat CAD *to* the ~10,000 US survey firms — delivering finished stamped surveys would put them in
competition with their whole customer base and dealer channel, and require fifty-state licensure and
E&O they have no basis for. The firms themselves are 3-8 person owner-operated practices billing the
exact licensed hours this removes, and the private-equity roll-ups buying them are labour-arbitrage
vehicles whose thesis is utilisation. Title insurers are excluded from the other side: they
deliberately except survey matters from coverage so they never carry boundary risk.

**Expansion path.** Volume boundary, topographic and ALTA/NSPS work for energy, data-centre, fibre and
CRE buyers inside a ~$14.17B global (~$12-13B US) surveying market with collapsing labour supply →
construction staking and as-built verification on the same sites → the by-product nobody owns: a
machine-readable national boundary graph carrying every deed call, found monument and resolved
conflict, which title, permitting and land-rights products all currently approximate.

**Scores** — Wedge 4 · Pattern 4 · Market 4 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 5 →
composite **4.2**

**Biggest risk.** A crew still has to go to the site and find the pins, so this is a field-services
business with an AI core, and gross margin is capped by that truck roll. The bet is that the *licensed*
hours — research, retracement, computation, drafting — are 60-70% of the cost and the crew is the cheap
part. If that split is wrong, this is a good services company and not a venture outcome.

**Why-now, stated plainly.** The 2026 ALTA/NSPS standards took effect 2026-02-23, added the Table A
Item 20 encroachment summary, and moved adjoining-deed research onto the surveyor — the title insurer
no longer has to supply adjoiner deeds. That is 2-4 extra hours per site of precisely the research
labour a model removes, landing in the same year as a record 43.4 GW of new utility-scale solar
(+60% on 2025) and $42.5B of BEAD fibre.

### 2. The government-contract ledger — DCAA-adequate from day one

**In plain English.** When a small defence startup wins its first real government contract, the
government will only pay it if its bookkeeping meets a federal standard: every dollar has to be sorted
into the right buckets, disallowed costs (alcohol, lobbying, first-class flights) have to be kept out,
hours have to be tracked a specific way, and an auditor has to be able to check all of it years later.
A twelve-person hardware company cannot do this — the standard is the same one a fifty-million-dollar
prime contractor meets. So today they either hire a specialist accounting firm by the hour or buy a
1990s system that costs six figures to install. We build the accounting system that just *does* it:
it reads the rules and the contract, decides where each transaction belongs, produces the filings the
government asks for, and stands behind the audit. The company pays a normal software price and gets
to take the contract.

**The proven pattern.** The AI-native ERP cluster: Rillet at $100M Series C, $1B valuation, 600+
customers and ARR doubled in three months (2026-08-19); Campfire ($100M+, Accel and YC); DualEntry
($90M Series A, Lightspeed and Khosla) — three funded companies at the identical job of making the
general ledger machine-constructed, inside a field of 53 tracked AI-native finance companies.

**Who buys the narrowed version.** The founder or first finance hire at a twelve-to-eighty-person
defence or dual-use hardware startup, at the pre-award accounting system survey — a DoD SBIR Phase II
($1.0-1.5M), an OTA converting to a cost-type follow-on, or a first cost-reimbursable subcontract, on
a timetable that will not accommodate a Costpoint implementation.

**Why the leader can't follow.** Deltek's Costpoint revenue model is a six-figure implementation sold
and delivered through a partner channel, with the complexity itself as the product — automating
compliance destroys both the channel and the seat count, and Deltek is PE-owned and levered, which
makes deliberate cannibalisation harder to authorise, not easier. The govcon CPA firms (Team 80,
Jameson, Redstone GCI, Cherry Bekaert) bill hours and sign engagements as partners; neither survives
being productised into a system that takes standing responsibility for accounting-system adequacy. And
Rillet, Campfire and DualEntry cannot config-change into it: FAR Part 31 allowability, indirect pools,
incurred-cost submissions and a government audit posture are a second ledger sold to a buyer their
entire go-to-market points away from.

**Expansion path.** Defence-tech startups at their first cost-type award (small in dollars, the
fastest-growing cohort in the economy, and one that compounds into large contractors) → the long tail
of small and mid-sized federal contractors priced out of Costpoint → up-market displacement of
Costpoint and Unanet in a category whose leader was taken out at $2.8B a decade ago → the rest of the
govcon back office the ledger already holds the data for — pricing and proposal cost volumes,
subcontractor flow-downs, closeouts and audit defence — across a federal contracting economy obligating
well over $700B a year.

**Scores** — Wedge 4 · Pattern 5 · Market 4 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 5 · Founder-fit 4 →
composite **4.3**

**Biggest risk.** This is the most obvious unclaimed idea in the hottest sector in venture, and the
occupancy search found no AI-native player only because nobody has announced yet — a stealth "modern
Deltek for defence tech" is more likely than not to exist right now. Speed is the whole strategy. The
second risk is that Deltek's answer is an acquisition rather than a product, which caps the outcome
unless the beachhead has already widened past defence.

## Method note for the next run

Three things worth carrying forward.

**One.** The narrowed-market-leader question (added 2026-09-01) works and should stay first in the
sequence. It killed one candidate outright and reframed two others before they cost a full development
pass.

**Two.** Occupancy is now the binding constraint, not novelty of insight. Ten of fifteen kills, and
eight of those ten had a funded occupant shipping the identical product. The practical consequence:
**search occupancy on the two-sentence version of an idea before writing a single line of wedge
argument.** Five candidates this run cost one search each.

**Three.** Two mechanisms are now formally exhausted and should not be re-mined without a
non-obvious buyer: **ambient physical-work capture** (three re-points, three occupancy kills this run)
and **agent governance / agent supply chain** (eight kills across the book). Conversely the
**AI-native firm** shape — take the licence, take the liability, sell the deliverable — produced both
survivors and has one prior entry in the whole ledger. It is the least-mined productive seam available,
but the next run should still lead with a different mechanism to avoid the template-following the
2026-08-25 report warned about.
