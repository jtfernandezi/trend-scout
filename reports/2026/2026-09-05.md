# Trend Scout — Report 2026-09-05

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (thirty-sixth run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).
**Outcome:** **1 surviving idea (composite 4.2, one brief), 11 logged kills, 1 held-book entry updated
with new evidence.**

The 2026-09-03 report left three instructions. All three were followed, and the third is the one that
shaped the run: *lead with a different mechanism than the AI-native firm shape, which produced both of
the last run's survivors.* So this scan started from traction and deliberately worked mechanisms the
ledger has never touched — embedded regulated infrastructure, conversational-native finance for
non-app populations, real-economy financial operating systems, freight operations agents, generative
media, the inference buildout, and expert-labour marketplaces.

**The kill distribution is the finding, again, and it is more extreme than ever: ten of eleven kills
came from the occupancy test, one from the venture-scale gate. Zero died to the config-change test.**
That is not because the config-change test got easier. It is because the occupancy search now runs
first, on the two-sentence version, and it kills the idea before it is developed far enough for the
config question to matter. In nine of the ten occupancy kills the occupant was funded and shipping the
identical product; in two of them (data-hall robots, and data-centre commissioning) the occupant was
joined by a hyperscaler or a four-way software category doing it in-house.

**A mechanism-level result worth recording.** The best traction datapoint in the entire scan was
Light — every new electricity brand entering the Texas market in the first half of 2026 launched on
it, up from 70% in 2025 — and the mechanism it proves ("hold the licence, the balance sheet and the
per-jurisdiction filings, and sell the whole regulated entity as an API") generalises beautifully on
paper. It was tested across **seven** narrowings and every one closed. That is logged as a single
entry (ts-0326) rather than seven, and the conclusion is: **regulated-entity-as-a-service is a solved
mechanism with a named incumbent in every retail market worth entering.** Do not re-mine it.

**A second mechanism to stop mining.** The "AI operator for an underserved small vertical" family now
has five prior ledger kills (franchisees, pharmacy, restaurants in Japan, grocery, and the whole
2026-09-01 Owner.com sweep) and this run added a sixth by a different route (ts-0328, killed on
venture scale). Every remaining candidate enumerated during the run — home care agencies, independent
insurance agencies, congregations, funeral homes, youth sports, small quarries, auto body, marinas —
is either occupied by a named incumbent or too small to clear the $1B gate. The family is exhausted.

**Where the survivor came from.** The one mechanism in the scan with zero prior coverage in 335 ledger
entries is **Mercor's**: frontier models can now screen, match and continuously *grade* a human being's
domain competence, cheaply enough to support a labour business no human recruiting organisation could
run. Mercor is at $2B gross annualised revenue in under three years. Re-pointed once at construction
trades it died instantly to a funded occupant. Re-pointed at a market where a **statute** has just made
machine-scaled competence assessment the binding constraint, it found genuinely empty ground — the
27-state wave of provisional licensure pathways for internationally trained physicians, 13 of them live
and accepting applications, with no vendor anywhere in the implementation.

## Surviving idea

### 1. Attestation — the competence engine for supervised practice

**In plain English.** America is short of doctors, and there are tens of thousands of fully trained
doctors already living here — people who practised for years in Mexico, India, Nigeria or Ukraine — who
are not allowed to see patients because they never did an American residency. Twenty-seven states have
just changed the law: those doctors can now get a provisional licence if they work for two years under
supervision and someone credibly certifies they are good enough. Almost nobody is actually getting
through, because a small rural hospital has no spare senior doctor to watch someone for two years, and
there is no accepted way to test the skill in the first place. We build the thing that does both: we
test the doctor properly, we place them with a hospital that desperately needs one, and then software
reads everything they write — every note, order, prescription and referral — and flags to a real
supervising physician only the cases that need a human eye. Hospitals pay us because they finally get
a doctor. Eventually we employ the doctors ourselves and contract them out.

**The proven pattern.** Mercor — under three years old, **$2B gross annualised revenue as of June 2026,
four months after crossing $1B** — proves the mechanism: frontier models can screen, match and
continuously grade human domain competence at a cost no human recruiting organisation can reach.
Corroborated by the YC Summer 2026 "professional services replacement in regulated industries" cluster.

**Who buys the narrowed version.** The chief medical officer of a rural or critical-access hospital or
FQHC in one of the **13 states with a live pathway**, holding a vacancy that has failed a full
recruiting cycle — and, as the fastest route to the first ten, the state programmes themselves
(Illinois's Clinical Readiness Program with IDPH and the Governor's Office of New Americans;
Washington's CEGPP under SB 5185, effective 2026-06-11), which have applicants queued and no supply of
sponsoring employers. Illinois has issued **four** licences against **24** applicants under review.

**Why the leader can't follow.** ECFMG/Intealth is the incumbent assessor and is in direct
innovator's-dilemma conflict — its whole product is ECFMG Certification feeding the residency Match,
and these pathways exist to *bypass* residency; it is also a credential-verification nonprofit with no
placement vehicle and no rural-employer relationships. Residency evaluation software (MedHub, New
Innovations) is architecturally built around an ACGME-accredited programme with a designated
institutional official, objects a critical-access hospital simply does not have. Physician staffing
firms (AMN, CHG, Avant) bill a markup and structurally refuse competence-attestation liability, which
sits with the hospital's medical staff office.

**Expansion path.** Provisional-pathway physicians in 13 live states → internationally educated nurses,
a far larger annual flow whose **47% first-attempt NCLEX pass rate against 87% for US-educated nurses**
is a competence-remediation market before it is a placement market → advanced-practice and allied-health
supervision, a statutory requirement in most states → become the employer: an AI-native medical group
that employs the clinicians, supplies the supervision, carries the malpractice and contracts into rural
systems, capturing clinician economics rather than a software seat, against a projected **86,000-physician
shortfall by 2036**.

**Scores** — Wedge 4 · Pattern 4 · Market 4 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 5 · Founder-fit 4 →
composite **4.2**

**Biggest risk.** The beachhead is genuinely tiny today — four licences issued in Illinois — and it is
small because state medical boards move slowly and the AMA has published against these pathways. A
company that needs boards to approve its assessment instrument is a company whose growth rate is set by
regulators. The honest bull case is that this is a two-year land-grab into a market that compounds with
each state implementation and then explodes on the nurse side; the honest bear case is that it stays a
services business with 40 customers.

## Method note for the next run

**One. The occupancy-first discipline has now fully inverted the kill distribution — and that is a
warning, not a victory.** Ten of eleven kills this run, six of nine on 2026-09-01, ten of fifteen on
2026-09-03. Thirty runs in, almost every re-point of a proven mechanism is already occupied by someone
funded. The practical consequence is that **searching for empty segments is no longer the productive
move**. The survivor here was not found by asking "who can Light not serve?" — every version of that
question closed. It was found by asking a different question: *where has a law just created a job that
nobody can do yet?* Two of the last three survivors (ts-0307 on the 2026 ALTA/NSPS standard, ts-0324
on the state licensure wave) came from a dated statutory trigger with an implementation gap. The next
run should lead with **statutes that took effect in the last twelve months and have visibly failed to
be implemented**, and only then look for the mechanism that implements them.

**Two. Two more mechanisms are now formally exhausted.** Add to the list (ambient physical-work capture,
agent governance): **regulated-entity-as-a-service** — seven narrowings, seven named incumbents — and
**the AI operator for an underserved small vertical**, which now has six kills and no remaining
candidate that is both unoccupied and large enough to clear the $1B gate.

**Three. One mechanism is newly opened and barely mined: model-underwritten human competence.** It has
one entry in 335 (this run's survivor). Mercor at $2B is the strongest single traction number in the
book's history and the ledger had zero coverage of it before today. The first re-point (construction
trades) died to an occupant in one search, so the seam is not free — but it is the freshest productive
mechanism available, and the places to point it are markets where competence, not credentials, has just
become the legal test.
