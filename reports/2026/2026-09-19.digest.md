*Trend Scout — 2026-09-19*

*0 surviving ideas. 12 kills.*

No idea cleared the bar today, and that is the result rather than a bad day. The last run asked this
one to stop looking at "become the licensed firm" ideas and look at ordinary product ideas instead.
Twelve were worked up properly. Every single one turned out to have a funded company already sitting
in it — three of them had shipped the identical product in the last two months.

*The lesson, in plain English.* If an idea needs no licence, no field staff and no slow government
contract to start, then nothing stops five other funded teams starting it at the same time. The
awkward, barriered businesses this project kept finding are not a rut; they are the seam, because
whatever keeps the big incumbent out also keeps the other eleven startups out.

*The sharpest example.* In March the US mortgage agencies announced that from 3 August every condo
building over ten units would need a full financial and structural review before anyone could get a
mortgage there. That is a large, dated, guaranteed new job. By the time the date arrived, a company
called Rexera had already built it and been bought by a big property-software group. A public deadline
is a starting gun everyone hears — it is only useful when there is also a barrier that slows the
people who hear it.

*One thing got stronger without being touched:* the held idea of an independent safety-testing lab for
robot brains. Two robot-intelligence companies raised 450 and 500 million dollars this quarter and are
now running learned robots inside other companies' factories, and still nobody independently tests
those behaviours before deployment.
