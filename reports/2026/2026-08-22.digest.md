**1. Control of record — composite 4.3**
From January 2027 it becomes illegal in the US for a big landlord to buy another single-family house if it already controls 350 or more of them, and the fine is a million dollars a house or triple the price. Almost nobody can tell whether they're over the line, because American rental homes sit inside tangled chains of shell companies, funds and joint ventures recorded in three thousand separate county offices — and the law counts all of it together, including houses you merely manage or hold a quarter of. This reads every one of those records, works out who really controls what, and gives a buyer a written answer before they sign — with an insurance-style guarantee behind it. Landlords, their lenders and the title companies closing the deals all pay for that certainty, because the alternative is betting a nine-figure fine on a lawyer's best guess.

Two of the run's strongest candidates died for the same reason, and that's the finding worth keeping: a guarantee on the housing exits the same law forces, and fixed fuel pricing for truck fleets, were both **correlated** — one bad year breaks every contract at once.
