# Trend Scout — Report 2026-06-25

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (seventh run under the venture-scale-gated thesis). Acting on the 2026-06-23 takeaway — *pressure-test the untouched big shifts the headline-chasers skipped (stablecoin rails, the AI-roll-up holdco layer), and keep watching the private-credit/semi-liquid stress.*
**Outcome:** **1 surviving idea, 9 logged kills.** This was a hard run: I developed and adversarially kill-checked ~15 candidate crosses and almost every fresh structural shift turned out to be either **already occupied** (drone-swarm C2, data-center compute-flexibility, eldercare, residential permitting, AI-agent liability carriers) or **not AI-native at venture scale** (stablecoin treasury, the data-center load-defection backstop, world-model vertical plays). The takeaway's two flagged seams both came back crowded/internal-value. The single survivor came from the one corner where the kill-check itself confirmed an *unbuilt* problem: the insurance industry is publicly admitting that **"current methods do not measure whether AI failures could be correlated across multiple insureds simultaneously"** — and the entire P&C industry is in a "silent AI" accumulation crisis, blanket-excluding a peril it cannot price.

## Surviving idea

### ts-0054 — The correlated-AI-failure catastrophe model for primary P&C carriers ("the RMS / CyberCube for the silent-AI peril")

**In plain English.** Almost every company now uses AI somewhere in its business, and when AI makes a costly mistake — gives bad advice, discriminates, an autonomous agent does real damage — someone files an insurance claim. Insurers are terrified of one specific nightmare: because thousands of different companies all quietly rely on the *same handful* of AI models, a single bad update or flaw could make all of them mess up *at the same time*, triggering a flood of simultaneous claims that no insurer has set aside money for. Their reaction so far is crude: just refuse to cover anything AI-related at all. But you can't permanently refuse to insure the whole economy. This company builds the tool insurers are missing — it figures out which AI systems each of their customers depends on and calculates how badly the insurer would be hit if those systems failed together, so the insurer can go back to actually selling coverage (and charging for it) instead of slamming the door. Insurers pay because pricing this risk is the only way back into the fastest-growing exposure there is, and getting it wrong is a company-ending event.

- **Who buys it.** *Beachhead:* the exposure-management / chief-underwriting-officer / portfolio-accumulation lead at a mid-to-large commercial P&C carrier or specialty MGA — starting in **D&O / E&O / Tech-E&O**, the lines where WR Berkley's *absolute* AI exclusion is forcing the decision *now*, at the 2026 renewal. *Expansion:* all commercial liability lines, then the industry-standard "AI peril" model that reinsurers, cat-bond/ILS investors and brokers all price against.
- **Why no one already serves them.** Physical-CAT incumbents (Verisk, Moody's RMS) have no AI-dependency data; CyberCube and Moody's RMS Cyber frame AI only as a *force-multiplier for cyber attacks* (malware propagation) — a different correlation structure than non-malicious model-error cascades across liability lines; per-agent MGAs (Klaimee, Mount, Corgi) insure the AI *vendor* one agent at a time, not the accumulation across a carrier's book of ordinary insureds; reinsurers (Munich Re aiSure, Swiss Re) build AI views for *their own* treaties, and primaries don't want to depend on their reinsurer's model. The independent, liability-line, correlated-AI-failure accumulation model for the *primary* market is open — and the industry openly admits "current methods do not measure whether failures could be correlated across multiple insureds."
- **Scores:** Novelty 4 · Why-now 5 · Market 5 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 4 → **composite 4.3**.
- **Biggest risk.** **Incumbent extension from cyber.** CyberCube and Moody's RMS already own the accumulation-modeling muscle and the exposure-management buyer relationship for cyber, and are explicitly adding "AI" — so the bet is that the *non-cyber AI-error liability peril* is a genuinely distinct modeling problem (different correlation mechanism, different lines, different data) that they're slow to build because their data and DNA are attack-centric. The wedge is winning the D&O/E&O accumulation problem fast and compounding a proprietary AI-dependency-graph + loss dataset into the industry-standard model before a cyber-CAT incumbent pivots, or before reinsurers' captive models become the de-facto standard.

## Takeaway for next run

This run confirmed two things. First, **the two seams flagged last run (stablecoin treasury, AI-roll-up picks-and-shovels) are dead ends for this thesis** — saturated rails and internal-value holdcos respectively; retire them as standing suggestions. Second, **the single survivor again came from the portfolio/systemic square that the per-unit crowd skips** (model the *book-wide correlated accumulation*, not the *single-unit pricing*) — the third time this pattern has produced the winner (ts-0048 flows-not-marks; ts-0039 independent-not-self-run; ts-0054 accumulation-not-per-agent). Make it an explicit rule: **when a hot shift has a "price one unit" square already taken, check the "model the whole portfolio's correlated/systemic exposure" square — it is consistently emptier and more defensible.**

Next run: (1) keep watching the silent-AI insurance shift (more carriers will exclude at renewals; watch whether CyberCube/Moody's actually ship a *liability*-line AI accumulation model, which would kill ts-0054); (2) the world-model capital wave is too large to ignore — hunt a *vertical* world-model wedge the $1B generalists structurally won't take (a regulated or data-locked niche), rather than a horizontal play; and (3) pressure-test a genuinely untouched big shift — the **EU AI Act high-risk Aug-2026 deadline** (importer/distributor conformity verification) or the **defense autonomy T&E** lane around ts-0032 — for the systemic/portfolio square rather than the obvious per-unit one.
