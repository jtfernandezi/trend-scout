  It only worked last run because the pool's *entity structure* (hours banks, reciprocity) broke the leader's schema.**

  "Taft-Hartley" plus AI search and the IFEBP exhibitor list carry over to the next run. ts-0559 stays `new`.

**The finding worth carrying forward: the survivor came from changing the *trigger*, not the buyer's size.** Serval's
agents learn one company's systems and run its internal requests every day. The same skill, aimed at the one moment
when *two* companies' systems must become one (a private-equity add-on closing), creates a different buyer (the PE
operating partner), a different price (fixed fee per deal) and a different deadline (Day 1). Serval's subscription
model doesn't reach that buyer, and the consultancies that own the work bill by the hour.

## Surviving ideas

### ts-0568 — The Day-1 machine: an AI-native integration operator for private-equity buy-and-build platforms

**In plain English.** Private-equity firms often build a company by buying many small ones (dental offices, plumbing
firms, accounting practices) and merging them into one. Every time they buy one, someone has to move its staff onto
the parent's email, logins, payroll and accounting systems. Today that takes consultants months and costs a lot. We'd
send in software that studies the new company's systems by itself, works out how everything maps across, and does the
move in weeks for a fixed price per deal. The investor pays because every month a company sits half-merged is a month
of savings they don't get.

- **The proven pattern:** Serval raised a 150M dollar Series C at 3bn dollars (Sequoia, 2026-10-29). Its ARR has
  doubled every quarter since December, and its agents learn a company's systems and run its internal IT and HR
  requests.
- **Who buys the narrowed version:** operating partners and platform CFOs/CIOs at lower-middle-market PE firms doing
  5-20 add-ons a year, when an add-on signs and Day 1 is 30-60 days away, or when half-integrated add-ons are blocking
  a sale.
- **Why the leader can't follow:** Serval sells a per-employee subscription for one company's daily requests. This job
  is episodic, crosses two legal entities and two of every system, has a deadline, and is bought per deal by the
  sponsor at a fixed fee. That means an outcome-priced services operation, not a feature. The consultancies that own
  the work bill hours, so a fixed-fee version in weeks would cannibalise them.
- **Expansion path:** lower-middle-market add-ons, then corporate M&A integration, then carve-outs and TSA exits (the
  highest fees and hardest deadlines), then the standing IT and back-office layer across a sponsor's whole portfolio.
  Every deal adds system-to-system mappings that make the next one faster.
- **Scores:** Wedge 3 · Pattern 4 · Market 4 · Defensibility 3 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 4 → composite 3.7.
- **Biggest risk:** every integration is a little bespoke, so this can slide into a low-margin consultancy. Meanwhile,
  a consultancy armed with Serval-like agents (or Serval partnering with one) could offer the same fixed fee.

## Method note for the next run

**One. Federation is not a wedge by itself.** Before arguing a pooled buyer, check two things: does the leader already
sell multi-tenant or through MSSPs and franchisors, and does the pool's entity structure actually break the leader's
schema (hours banks, reciprocity, cost allocation)? Only the second is structural.

**Two. Try trigger changes on horizontal agent winners.** The survivor came from aiming a daily-operations agent at a
one-time, deadline-driven event (an acquisition close). Other events with the same shape: carve-outs, bankruptcy
wind-downs, system sunsets (end of support), regulatory cutovers, and franchise or dealer transfers. Each has a
different buyer from the daily user.

**Three. Re-check ts-0568 and ts-0559 occupancy.** For ts-0559, search "Taft-Hartley" with "AI" and the IFEBP exhibitor list. For ts-0568, search ACG and PE operating-partner conference sponsors for "AI integration",
and check whether Serval, ShareGate or Quest announce PE or M&A programmes.

**Four. Skip for now:** everything on the 2026-10-13 to 2026-10-29 lists, plus Serval (except the M&A flank), Broccoli,
Avoca, Mave, Upwind, Dropzone, Collective, Cohere, Foundry Robotics, Dulo, FuriosaAI and Kalshi. Also skip MSP service
desks, public-sector ITSM, franchise call agents, whole-of-state SOCs, public-entity risk pools, HOA management and UK
MTD. Bites and SpendRule are fair game if their traction is confirmed.
