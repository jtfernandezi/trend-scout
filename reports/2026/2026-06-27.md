## Signals scanned

1. **The AI grid buildout is a ~$1.4T demand-truth crisis.** US utilities plan ~$1.4T of capex (PowerLines: ~$700B passed to residential ratepayers); top-5 hyperscaler capex ~$602B in 2026. But **developers file duplicate "phantom" interconnection requests across multiple utilities**, so forecasts double-count; NERC's 10-yr peak-demand forecast jumped **24% (+224 GW)** almost entirely on speculative data-center load. **23 states have approved large-load tariffs (51 tariffs, 300+ bills in 6 weeks)** explicitly to weed out speculative requests, and Moody's/S&P/Bond Buyer say data-center financing assumptions are "under scrutiny." Janus Henderson counters that "under-delivery, not overbuild" is the real risk and floats **"probability-of-energization-weighted capacity"** — acknowledged by analysts, productized by no one. *(structural / energy + capital markets — fresh, big)*
2. **CMS now uses AI to deny Original Medicare care.** The **WISeR** model (live Jan 1 2026, six states: AZ/NJ/OH/OK/TX/WA) lets AI/ML + clinician contractors pre-screen treatments, paid a **percentage of "averted" spend** (a shared-savings denial incentive). But the **House Appropriations Committee voted to defund it** amid bias/access concerns — a shaky, possibly-vanishing trigger. *(structural / healthcare — but trigger at risk)*
3. **The CPA pipeline collapsed and AI is filling it.** ~**300k** accountants lost 2020-2024, pipeline **-33%**, accounting-degree completions down 8 straight years; PwC cutting campus hiring ~1/3 ("first years now function like fourth years overseeing AI"). *(structural / labor)*

5. **Reindustrialization runs into a ~500k skilled-worker shortage.** Manufacturing-facility construction spending more than doubled vs 2021; controls/mechanical/electrical engineers are the hardest roles to fill; 70-90k more fab workers needed as planned US fabs come online. *(structural / industrial)*
6. **Agent reliability is becoming measurable and long-horizon.** METR-style task-length doubling every ~4-7 months; frontier agents reliably finish ~2-hour software tasks at 50%; "reliability science" + long-horizon memory benchmarks emerging. *(capability)*
7. **a16z's stated thesis: unstructured, multimodal enterprise data "breaks RAG and agents."** Structuring/governing it is the unlock — but it's the single most-crowded VC theme right now. *(capability / infra — saturated)*
8. **EU AI Act high-risk deadline slipped to Dec 2027.** The Digital Omnibus (political agreement May 7 2026) deferred Annex-III high-risk obligations from Aug 2026 to **Dec 2027** — so the conformity-assessment trigger the last run flagged is now soft (the Colorado SB24-205 / Section-1071 lesson again: verify the trigger is live). *(regulatory — trigger weakened, noted to avoid)*

## Surviving idea

### ts-0064 — The independent "demand-realism" engine for the AI grid buildout (the "RMS / Verisk for whether AI's electricity demand is real")

**In plain English.** Power companies are about to spend roughly $1.4 trillion building electric grid for AI data centers — and ordinary households are on the hook for about $700 billion of it through higher bills. The catch: a lot of that demand may be fake. To grab the best deal, a data-center developer files the *same* project as a power request at five different utilities at once, so every utility counts it and every utility plans to build for it — but only one (or none) will actually get built. Nobody has a trustworthy, independent way to tell which announced data centers are real, which are bluffing, and how many will quietly fall through at the same time if the AI boom cools or transformers stay back-ordered for years. This company builds that truth machine: it ingests the messy public and private breadcrumbs — power-request filings across utilities, permits, equipment order lead-times, construction and satellite signals, who's financing what — and scores how likely each project is to actually get built, and how linked their fates are. Utilities and state regulators pay so they don't overbuild and stick households with stranded, wasted infrastructure; lenders, bond investors and the data-center builders themselves pay so they don't pour money into capacity that never gets used. Either way you fear the wrong demand number, you need this.

- **Who buys it.** *Beachhead:* the resource-planning / load-forecasting lead at a large electric utility (or the staff / consumer-advocate side of a state utility commission) drowning in speculative large-load interconnection requests *right now*, who has to decide how much to build and how to set "you-caused-it-you-pay-for-it" tariffs without stranding ratepayers. *Expansion:* the project-finance lenders, data-center bond/ABS investors and ratings agencies underwriting the $1.4T buildout (whose demand assumptions are openly "under scrutiny"), then the hyperscalers/developers themselves — and finally the industry-standard demand-realism layer every side prices against.
- **Why no one already serves them.** Supply-side capacity-finders (GridCARE, Pearl Street — ts-0014) answer "where can I *get* power," not "is this announced demand *real* and *correlated*"; utility load-forecasting tools and consultancies (Itron, E3, Brattle) use classical single-point historical methods that the phantom-load problem breaks and are advisory, not a productized independent model; ratings agencies and investors (Janus Henderson's "probability-of-energization" framing) publish commentary but ship no platform; flexibility players (Emerald AI, Verrus) make load *curtailable* — a different problem. And utilities/PUCs/lenders structurally want a truth source *not* owned by the developers or their conflicted advisors — the same independent-model demand that sustains third-party catastrophe modelers.
- **Scores:** Novelty 4 · Why-now 5 · Market 5 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 4 → **composite 4.3**.
- **Biggest risk.** **The AI-native gate, in two directions.** (1) A skeptic calls this "sophisticated forecasting/ML plus a data-aggregation play," the ts-0041/ts-0059 kill reasoning — the answer is that classical IRP forecasting is single-point, historical and assumes independent, stable demand, and structurally cannot model strategic phantom requests *or* correlated materialization (thousands of projects whose fates are linked through shared hyperscaler capex, shared transformer supply and shared power limits) — the same independent-rate blind spot that made ts-0048 and ts-0054 AI-native, and the moat is the correlated-fates model plus a cross-utility/cross-source dataset, not a wrapper. (2) If the AI demand boom simply *materializes* on schedule, the "stranded-asset" fear deflates — but the under-delivery camp (Janus Henderson) shows the *demand-realism* truth is needed whichever way demand breaks, which is what makes the why-now durable rather than a one-directional bet.

## Takeaway for next run

The portfolio/systemic-square rule has now produced the survivor four runs running (ts-0048 flows-not-marks; ts-0039 independent-not-self-run; ts-0054 accumulation-not-per-agent; ts-0064 demand-realism-not-capacity). It is the most reliable generator in this ledger — keep leading with it: **enter a hot shift, map who owns each per-unit square, and take the correlated/systemic square they skipped.** Two second-order lessons this run worth banking: (a) **a severe labor shortage is an anti-signal** — it attracts the obvious "AI does the scarce work" play fastest, so audit and controls both came back occupied; the systemic square beats the shortage-the-headline-screams square; and (b) **re-verify every regulatory trigger live** — WISeR is being defunded and the EU AI Act high-risk deadline slid to Dec 2027, so two tempting "just-landed mandate" stories are soft this week.

Next run: (1) keep watching the grid demand-truth shift — watch whether a capacity-finder (GridCARE) or a ratings agency (Moody's/S&P) ships an independent demand-realism *product*, which would dent ts-0064; (2) pressure-test the **reindustrialization capital flow** (the $1.4T+ factory/fab buildout) for a *systemic* square the per-asset crowd skips, rather than the occupied controls-copilot square; and (3) the world-model capital wave is still un-cracked here — hunt the data-locked vertical wedge the $1B generalists structurally won't take.
