# Trend Scout — Report 2026-09-17

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (forty-first run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).
**Outcome:** **1 surviving idea (composite 4.0, one brief), 14 logged kills.**

The 2026-09-15 report left three instructions. All three were followed and two of them paid.

**One — add a fifth question to the occupancy test: what did this buyer do before the moat opened?**
Asked on every candidate. It fired hardest on Japan (ts-0396), where the mandatory
inheritance-registration duty and its **2027-03-31 grace-period cliff** looked like a textbook dated
trigger behind a licence gate no US legal-AI company can pass — and where the people who needed that
work had already built it, years ago, with patented family-register OCR and a Series-B-funded consumer
service. One search answered the whole candidate.

**Two — in Europe, stop working the calendar and start working the register.** Done, and it produced
the run's cleanest kill and the run's survivor, from opposite directions. The German statutory audit
(ts-0401) is the register done right and still dead: the independence rule in section 319 HGB
genuinely bars the AI-native bookkeeping firms from auditing their own clients, and Cortea is already
in the seat with a 12M euro seed and the largest German mid-market audit firm as its partner. **A
structural block on the leader still says nothing about who walked in** — the 2026-09-10 lesson,
holding for the fourth consecutive run.

**Three — the US equivalent of the register is a distressed incumbent plus a new appropriation.** Half
right, and the correction is worth keeping. The appropriation half was tested and failed: the other
section 1115 authorities are not the same opportunity as the reentry benefit, because CMS capped
health-related-social-needs spending at 3% of a state's Medicaid spend and, in SMD #26-003 on
2026-06-11, raised the budget-neutrality bar for all new demonstrations from 2027-01-01. New money is
no longer arriving. **The distressed-incumbent half was right, and the survivor came out of it —
except that the thing forcing the distress turned out not to be money at all. It was an accreditation
ceiling.**

**The finding worth carrying forward, and it corrects a wedge this book has now used three times.**
The book's favourite structural block is *"the incumbent is a staffing business, so automating the
hour deletes its own revenue."* That reasoning produced ts-0382 (the Dutch arbodienst) and was about
to produce a fourth this run — until the water candidate (ts-0408) killed it. Veolia's contract
operations are paid a **fixed fee**, so automating a water plant *raises* its margin; it has already
opened a North America AI command centre to do exactly that. The wedge only holds when the
incumbent's revenue is **per unit of the professional's time or per procedure performed** — because
then efficiency and revenue point in opposite directions. The survivor passes that sharper test with
numbers on the public record: the dominant private autopsy firm charges 1,250 dollars for a full
autopsy and 600 dollars for an external examination, so the imaging-led path that is the only way to
add capacity roughly halves its revenue per case.

**The kill distribution.** Eleven of fourteen kills from the occupancy test, two from the
config-change test (one in the strongest possible form — the incumbent had already shipped it), one
on occupancy plus venture scale. Occupancy has now been the dominant killer for six consecutive runs,
and in nine of this run's eleven the occupant was found on the first search.

**Three mechanism-level conclusions to stop re-mining.**

**(a) The licensed-profession register is now mostly occupied on the *attest and document* side, and
empty on the *judgement with no system of record* side.** Five register candidates were developed this
run — Japanese registration, German audit, US ERISA audit, special-education evaluation, professional
fiduciary — and all five died to funded occupants. The one that survived is the one where the output is
not a document assembled from a known schema but a determination made about a person nobody holds a
record of. Before developing another register candidate, ask whether the profession's output is a
*filing* (assume occupied) or a *judgement over reconstructed evidence* (worth a search).

**(b) "The incumbent is a staffing business" is not by itself a wedge — check how it bills.** Stated
in full above. Fixed-fee contract operators automate themselves happily and profitably; per-hour and
per-procedure firms cannot, because the efficient path destroys their own revenue line. This is now a
checklist question, and it should be asked with the incumbent's actual price list in hand.

**(c) The new-appropriation half of the 2026-09-15 instruction is spent.** HRSN spending is capped at
3% of a state's Medicaid spend with infrastructure capped at 15% of that, and CMS's SMD #26-003 of
2026-06-11 raises the budget-neutrality bar for every new demonstration from 2027-01-01. The reentry
benefit behind ts-0381 was the last of the genuinely new money, not the first of a series. Do not
reissue this instruction.

## Surviving ideas

### 1. The machine coroner — run county death investigations, and read the body with a scanner instead of a knife

**In plain English.** When somebody dies unexpectedly — an overdose, a car crash, a possible homicide,
an old person found at home — the county is legally required to work out what killed them, and only a
specially trained doctor called a forensic pathologist can make that call. America has about 850 of
them and needs about 1,250, and the rules of their own profession say each may only perform 250
autopsies a year, so the shortage cannot be fixed by working harder. The result is that families wait
four months for an answer and murder trials sit waiting for a report. We become the company a county
hires to do this work: a machine pulls together everything the health system already knows about the
person who died — their prescriptions, their ambulance calls, their hospital visits — which today is
one investigator on the phone for a week; a body scanner takes the place of cutting in the many cases
where it can; and the doctor spends their scarce hours on the judgement and the signature instead of
the paperwork. The county pays us a flat annual fee to run the whole thing, which is cheaper and much
faster than what they buy now.

- **The proven pattern.** The AI-native licensed firm. Integral raised an **18M euro Series A on
  2026-09-16** (Mosaic, Reid Hoffman, Cherry, General Catalyst, Puzzle) to be an AI-native accounting
  and tax firm in Germany delivering through licensed professionals; the YC Summer 2026 batch produced
  AI-run accounting firms (Rational, Billow AI, Last Accounting Company) and AI-run law firms (Osmaura,
  Erinys) with YC's own request for startups saying sell the service, not the software; **Savvy Wealth**
  is the paying proof at scale (**100M dollar Series C at a 600M valuation on 2026-09-09**, up from 225M
  in July 2025, 150+ advisers, 19 hours a week saved each).
- **Who buys the narrowed version.** The county board of supervisors together with the elected coroner,
  sheriff-coroner or chief medical examiner who holds the death-investigation contract, in the roughly
  1,500 jurisdictions too small to staff an accredited office and too busy to do without one. Trigger:
  the contract falling due when the county's pathologist retires or resigns, after an accreditation
  lapse, or after a prosecutor puts the report backlog on the record in a pending homicide.
- **Why the leader can't follow.** The incumbent private groups sell per procedure — ProPublica
  documented the model in detail: minimal overhead, the client supplies the facilities and the
  investigators, headquarters is an invoice processing centre, **1,250 dollars a full autopsy against
  600 dollars an external examination** — so the imaging-led path that is the only way to add capacity
  halves their revenue per case, and they own no scanners and no software. Their current growth strategy
  is already outside the standard: the same reporting has five doctors each taking **300+ cases a year**
  against an accreditation ceiling of 250. And the health-AI leaders model a living, insured patient
  inside a billable encounter; a decedent has no record number at the investigating office, no payer and
  no claim, so the revenue-cycle machinery that funds every health-AI product simply is not there.
- **Expansion path.** ~605,000 accepted cases a year at roughly 3,000 dollars of budget each — about
  **1.8bn dollars of standing public spend across ~2,000 offices** — beachheaded in the mid-sized county
  → metropolitan and statewide systems on eight-figure contracts → forensic toxicology and postmortem
  imaging, bought separately today and margin the moment you own the case → the 2.5 million deaths a year
  certified by ordinary clinicians with no training in it → the compounding asset, the only linked national
  record of scene, imaging, toxicology, history and certified cause of death, which is what prices mortality
  for life insurers, detects a change in the drug supply weeks early, and feeds post-market drug safety →
  the coronial systems of the UK, Canada, Australia and the Nordics, same duty, same arithmetic.
- **Scores.** Wedge 4 · Pattern 4 · Market 4 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 4 →
  **composite 4.0**
- **Biggest risk.** The binding constraint binds you too: there are 850 forensic pathologists and you
  must recruit some of them, in a market where the offer has to be "you will do only the examination and
  the judgement." Underneath that sit real hazards — a wrong cause-of-death determination is litigated
  and can free or convict someone; postmortem CT substitutes for autopsy in many deaths but *not* the
  homicides that matter most to your buyer; county procurement is slow; and this is a business whose
  product is other people's worst day.

**A note the operator should hear rather than discover.** This is the second consecutive run whose
survivor has the shape *AI-native licensed provider takes over a distressed public-sector contract*
(ts-0381, county jail health, on 2026-09-15). That is either a genuine seam this book has learned to
find, or a rut. The evidence says seam — the two segments share no buyer, no licence, no data and no
payer, and both came back empty on occupancy while eleven software-shaped candidates around them died
to named funded competitors. But it is worth saying out loud, because if the next run produces a third
in the same costume, the honest read flips.

## Method note for the next run

**One. Ask the billing question before the wedge question.** When the proposed wedge is "the incumbent
cannot automate itself," go and find the incumbent's price list. Per hour or per procedure means the
efficient path destroys their revenue and the wedge is real. Fixed fee, capitation or subscription
means automation is pure margin for them and the wedge is imaginary — that is what killed ts-0408, and
it would have killed it in one search instead of four. This is a cheaper and more decisive question
than any of the four parts of Step 3.

**Two. Split the register in two before spending a candidate on it.** Five of this run's fourteen kills
were licensed-profession candidates and all five were occupied. The pattern separating them from the
survivor is clean enough to use as a filter: if the profession's output is a **filing assembled from a
known schema** — a registration, an audit opinion, an evaluation report, an accounting — assume a funded
occupant exists and spend the first search proving it. If the output is a **judgement made about a
subject for whom no system of record exists**, the segment is probably still empty, because there was
never a record for a software vendor to attach to. Death investigation is the second consecutive
survivor found on that side of the line; jail health was the first.

**Three. Next run should open on a mechanism that is not a professional services firm.** Two runs in a
row have produced the same company shape, and this run scanned three mechanisms with hard traction that
were never converted into a candidate because the services frame kept winning: **CADDi's** historical
engineering record as a decision layer (182bn yen valuation, half the Nikkei 225), **Arcee's** 20M
dollar frontier-adjacent model, and the **YC Summer 2026 atoms cohort** (Isengard at 10M dollars of
revenue, Cosmic at 25M dollars of contracts, Atomarine at 4bn dollars of letters of intent). Each of
those is a product mechanism with real money behind it and no re-point attempted this run. Open there,
and hold the services frame in reserve.
