*Trend Scout — 2026-10-31*

*1 survivor, 8 kills.*

*The survivor.*
- *The Day-1 machine: an AI-native integration operator for private-equity buy-and-build platforms.* Investors who buy
  dozens of small companies and merge them into one pay consultants for months to move each new company onto the
  parent's email, payroll and accounting. We'd use software that studies the new company's systems by itself and does
  the move in weeks, for a fixed price per deal.
  _For:_ PE operating partners and platform CFOs, when an acquisition signs and Day 1 is weeks away. Composite 3.7.

*What we tried.* This week's winners were Serval (AI for internal IT and HR requests, 3bn dollars, revenue doubling
every quarter), Broccoli AI (AI phone agents for plumbers and electricians, 1,500+ customers), Mave and Upwind (AI
security analysts), Collective (back office for one-person businesses) and Cohere (private AI for big companies).

*What died, in plain English.*
- AI help desk for IT outsourcers: several startups already do it.
- The same for governments and schools: just a certification project for the leader.
- AI phone agents for franchise brands: the leaders already sell to them.
- AI security analysts for state-run local programs: leaders already sell to that kind of buyer.
- AI for city and county insurance pools: a startup already targets them.
- AI-run homeowner association management: a YC startup already does it.
- AI tax filing for UK self-employed: banks give it away free.
- Private AI for European co-op banks: that's Cohere's main business.

*Lesson.* Selling to a pool of organisations isn't an opening by itself. Aiming a winning tool at a one-off event
(like an acquisition) can create a new buyer.
