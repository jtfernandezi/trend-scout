# Trend Scout — Report 2026-06-17

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (third run; first under the venture-scale-gated thesis to scan a fresh, AI-native big-market surface rather than the niche-regulatory surface of the prior two runs).
**Outcome:** **0 surviving ideas, 17 logged kills.** An honest zero-survivor run — and a high-signal one. Every one of the 18 candidate crosses I developed against this month's biggest AI + structural signals was already occupied, usually by a *funded* startup, sometimes by hyperscalers/incumbents, and in the strongest cases by the exact named category leader.

## Signals scanned

1. **YC Summer 2026 RFS — the hard-tech pivot.** 15 categories, ~8 needing hardware/capital (agriculture robots, counter-drone defence, space inference chips, lunar manufacturing, semiconductor supply-chain software). Stated thesis: "software is the substrate, not the moat; the next decade of $1B+ outcomes is AI applied to physical, regulated, capital-heavy industries." *(AI/capability + structural)*
2. **Sequoia AI Ascent + a16z 2026 theses.** Sequoia: 2026 is "the year of agents" — systems that pursue goals, AI as the new operating layer for digital work. a16z: capital now wants "software attached to hard consequences — real infrastructure, regulated systems, network effects"; AI replaces back-office systems before front-end apps. *(capability/investor)*
3. **Model cost collapse + agentic releases.** Gemini 3.5 "agentic era" + Gemini Omnia (creation from any input, video first); GPT-5.5 + real-time audio/translation agents; Claude Opus 4.8 (agentic computer use). June 2026: US–China gap effectively closed as Qwen 3.7 Max / MiniMax 3 match Western flagships and collapse the cost of intelligence. *(capability)*
4. **Vertical AI agents are now the dominant investable surface** — 48.3% of 2026 YTD deals and 54.6% of capital; Series A averaging ~$52M. Recent rounds: Rebar ($14M, AI OS for HVAC/electrical/plumbing suppliers), Cohere ($500M @ $6.8B enterprise agentic), Juno (AI SMB tax). *(structural / capital flow)*
5. **Agent infrastructure is standardizing fast.** Agent-payment protocols live (Visa Trusted Agent, Mastercard Agent Pay, Google AP2, Stripe Machine Payments Protocol Mar 2026, x402); NIST AI Agent Standards Initiative (Feb 2026); 5 vendors shipped agent-identity frameworks at RSAC 2026 (Cisco, CrowdStrike, Palo Alto, Microsoft, Cato). *(structural / platform)*
6. **The AI power crunch.** US IT load ~80 GW → ~150 GW by 2028; Morgan Stanley projects a 49 GW shortfall; interconnection queue ~2,600 GW with ~5-yr median waits (12 yr for some data centers); FERC final action due ~Apr 30 2026; behind-the-meter generation going permanent (VoltaGrid + Blackstone $1B). Transformers at 4-yr lead times; ~300k-electrician shortfall. *(structural / energy)*
7. **Physical AI mass deployment.** Boston Dynamics + Gemini Robotics at Hyundai; Amazon's "Sequoia" system and its >1M-robot fleet (both June 2026); logistics the fastest-growing physical-AI vertical; construction needs ~425k workers in 2026. *(structural / labor)*
8. **2026 tariff regime + healthcare PA rule.** Record import duties, continuous duty-mitigation, multi-tier origin documentation now mandatory; CMS Interoperability & Prior Authorization Final Rule (effective Jan 2026) forces electronic PA APIs, 72-hr urgent responses, and written denial reasons. *(structural / regulatory)*

## Why this run produced zero survivors (the takeaway)

This wasn't a lazy run — it was four waves and ~23 web searches across the full watchlist (YC RFS, Sequoia/a16z, model releases, funding flows, agent infra, energy, physical AI, healthcare, trade). The result is itself the finding:

**As of mid-2026, the "frontier AI × big obvious structural shift" surface is fully arbitraged.** Every cross that can be phrased in one sentence — "AI for the interconnection queue," "AI for nuclear licensing," "AI for the tariff mess," "insurance for robots," "reputation for agents" — has already been founded, frequently funded inside the last 6 months. The agent-infrastructure layer in particular (identity, reputation, payments, observability, forensics, FinOps, spend controls) is now a *standards-and-incumbents* game, not greenfield.

The implication for the next run is methodological, not defeatist: **stop crossing AI with the headline trend.** Future surviving ideas will come from one of:
- a **non-searchable framing** — a buyer/workflow pain that doesn't yet have a category name to Google;
- a **proprietary-data or deep-tech moat** where the wedge is a hard dataset/model, not a workflow anyone can copy;
- a **structural shift that hasn't yet been connected to AI in public** (the "second-order" consequence of a trend, not the trend itself);
- a vertical with a **large labor budget but no named AI-native challenger yet** (most of the obvious ones — legal, accounting, RCM, trades, title, insurance, customs — are now taken).

Per the thesis, forcing a differentiated-sliver-of-an-occupied-space through the gate would violate the bar, so nothing was promoted. No brief is written this run (nothing scored — every candidate was gated out before scoring).
