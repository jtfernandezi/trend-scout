*Trend Scout — 2026-11-03*

*0 survivors, 10 kills.* No idea cleared all three gates this run.

*What we tried.* This week's winners were:
- Fragment: AI that fixes purchasing mix-ups (wrong prices, short deliveries) for big manufacturers; 56M dollars, dozens of Fortune 1000 customers.
- TrashLab: software and an AI phone agent for local rubbish-collection companies; hundreds of customers, plus two funded rivals.
- Tutor Intelligence: factory robots rented by the hour; 75M dollars from a16z, in almost 4x as many sites as last year.
- CarbonAI: AI that does the analysis on commercial property loans for lenders.
- Also Scarlet (paperwork for medical AI approvals) and Tandem (an AI note-taker for European clinics).

*What died, in plain English.*
- Fragment-style AI for defense suppliers: just a security certification for the leader.
- The same for ships' spare parts: the main ship-maintenance software already added it.
- Checking utility storm-repair bills: a startup already does it.
- Helping towns claim disaster money from FEMA: FEMA is moving to fixed payments to states, so the work shrinks.
- Checking oil-well cost bills for part-owners: an industry data giant already does it, and the market is small.
- Recovering struck-down tariffs for importers: a one-time event that is mostly paid out already.
- Buying up local rubbish haulers and running them with AI: big waste companies and investors already do this.
- AI broker for hazardous waste: startups already do the paperwork, and big players own the disposal sites.
- Rented robots for ammunition factories: other startups already have contracts there.
- AI loan analysis for insurers and small banks: CarbonAI or a rival can just sell to them.

*Lesson.* In most industries the company that already holds the records (maintenance logs, billing data) adds the AI
agent first. The next openings are where records are still kept on paper and email.
