*Trend Scout — 2026-09-10*

*0 survivors, 17 kills.*

Nothing cleared the bar today. Seventeen ideas were worked up properly against six companies that are
visibly winning right now, and all seventeen died. That is a real result rather than a wasted run, and
one of the kills taught us something that changes how we search.

*What we learned.* Four of the seventeen had exactly what we look for: a cast-iron reason the big
company *cannot* copy us. A drug maker is legally barred from paying for certain prescriptions. A
charity has to be independent of its donors. A software company won't take on a medical device's legal
liability. Every one of those four still died — because somebody else had already quietly set up shop
in the gap. *A locked door does not mean an empty room.* From now on the first question is "is anyone
already there?", asked before we spend an hour arguing why the leader can't follow.

*The near misses.* The best wedge of the day was a charity: manufacturers may not legally pay a
Medicare patient's drug bills, so an independent foundation is the only route — and no rival funded by
a drug company could ever take that seat. It died because a charity has no owners, so it can never be
a billion-dollar company. The only idea where we found no competitor at all was getting brand-new
building materials approved for use in construction — killed because the people who grant those
approvals are already selling the software themselves.

*Still on the shelf, and looking better.* Our idea for an independent lab that safety-tests the
"brains" of warehouse and factory robots has been waiting since June. This week its customer changed
for the better: insurers have started pricing robot cover on what the machine is actually allowed to
do, so an insurance company — not a robot maker — would now happily pay for that testing. The safety
rulebook for walking robots doesn't arrive until 2027-28, and nobody has taken the seat yet.
