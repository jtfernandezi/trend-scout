# Trend Scout — Report 2026-08-29

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (thirty-third run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).
**Outcome:** **1 surviving idea (composite 4.2, one brief), 14 logged kills, 1 held-book entry updated
with new evidence.**

This run was deliberately structured against the last three. The 2026-08-25 report flagged that three
consecutive runs had produced survivors of the same shape — an AI-native business that takes a
position and gets paid for being right — and warned that the pattern was starting to look like
template-following. So this scan led with *traction*, not with statutes: the funding roundups of the
last three weeks, the category clusters inside them, and a census of the ledger's own blind spots.

The census is worth stating, because it explains the shape of the run. Across 282 prior entries the
book has zero coverage of restaurants, telecoms, advertising, grocery and prisons, and one entry each
for payroll, recruiting, pensions, music, mining, hotels and policing. It has 44 entries containing
the word "underwrite", 30 on space, 18 on construction and audit, and 17 on banking. The ledger is
not a map of the economy; it is a map of where the operator has already looked.

Fourteen candidates died. The distribution is the finding: **nine of the fourteen were killed by the
occupancy test, and in every one of those nine the occupant was already funded and already selling
the exact thing.** Only two died to the config-change test and only two to segment viability. That
inversion is new. In earlier runs the config-change test did most of the killing, because the ideas
being tested were narrowings of a leader's product. This run the ideas were re-points of a proven
mechanism into a different market, and what killed them was that somebody had already made the same
re-point and raised money for it. When the mechanism is genuinely good, the re-point is obvious to
everyone at once.

The survivor is the one candidate whose occupant turned out to be a *supplier to* the incumbent
rather than a replacement for it — which is the tell worth carrying into the next run.

## Held book updated

**ts-0240 · Underwritten carrier selection** (watching) — new evidence, confirming and complicating.
*Montgomery v. Caribe Transport II* was decided **9-0**: state negligent-hiring claims against freight
brokers are not preempted by the FAAAA. Two months later a Texas jury returned **$604M** against
C.H. Robinson, its motor carrier and the driver over a 2021 fatal pileup, with the largest share of
liability potentially falling on the broker. August 2026 adds two facts the original entry lacked:
FMCSA is under direct industry pressure to publish an adequate-carrier-vetting standard, and brokers
tightening vetting are beginning to shut carriers with Conditional safety ratings out of brokered
freight entirely. The second fact is the demand signal — the market is pricing selection risk with no
underwriter present. The first is the risk: a published federal standard would commoditise the
determination and leave only the indemnity. Keep watching; do not promote until FMCSA's direction is
clear.

## Surviving idea

### 1. Fix the chair first — the AI-native complex rehab and home mobility company

**In plain English.** Hundreds of thousands of Americans cannot leave the house without a powered
wheelchair, and when one breaks they routinely wait months for a repair — the two big companies that
sell these chairs make their money sending a technician on a truck, so a fast, cheap repair is
against their own interest, and a new custom chair takes two to five months to arrive. We fix that
first: the user sends photos and a short video, our system works out what has actually failed on a
nine-year-old chair from a manufacturer that no longer supports it, identifies the exact part, and
either ships it with instructions or sends a local handyman-grade technician the same week. Insurance
pays for the repair, as it always has. Then, because we are the company that showed up, we are the
company that builds the next chair — measuring the person's posture and reach from ordinary video,
designing the seat to fit them, and printing it, instead of assembling somebody else's catalogue
parts over four months.

**The proven pattern.** Three funded companies in twelve months proving that AI design plus additive
manufacture plus owning the insurance claim collapses a craft-bound custom device category: Hike
Medical ($22.5M seed and Series A, 2026-08-25, orthotics and prosthetics combining software,
insurance and 3D printing), CustoMED ($6M, AI-powered 3D-printed orthopaedic solutions) and Kalogon
($5.75M in June 2026, shipping a Medicare-coded 3D-printed variable-density lattice seating line).
The documentation half is validated separately by the 2026 autonomous-RCM cluster — Candid Health at
190% YoY growth and 180% net dollar retention, inside more than $500M raised for machine-repaired
claims in one year.

**Who buys the narrowed version.** The power-wheelchair user or their caregiver — an adult with a
spinal cord injury, MS, ALS, muscular dystrophy or advanced cerebral palsy — at the moment the chair
dies and they are immobilised in their home, with the payer (Medicare, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid or
commercial) billed behind them. Secondary trigger: the five-year Medicare replacement window opening,
or a rehab hospital discharge planner who needs a custom chair configured before the patient leaves.

**Why the leader can't follow.** Channel conflict and labour-model cannibalisation, on both halves at
once. Numotion (150 branches, 500+ ATPs, 1,000+ service technicians) and National Seating & Mobility
are *distributors*: their gross margin is the spread and the rebate on Permobil, Quantum and Sunrise
product, so manufacturing their own seating attacks the supplier relationships that are their P&L —
and their service revenue is billed per visit against a field organisation whose utilisation remote
diagnosis destroys. Both are private-equity-owned and levered, which makes deliberate cannibalisation
harder to authorise, not easier. The manufacturers cannot follow either: Permobil already ships
connected-chair diagnostics through MyPermobil, but it reaches only its own new chairs and it sells
*through* Numotion and NSM. And Kalogon, the nearest AI-native player, distributes its cushions
through Numotion, NSM, Reliable Medical and Paragon — it is a supplier to the incumbents, not a
replacement for them. Nobody in the chain is structurally free to serve the mixed-vintage, multi-brand
installed base directly.

**Expansion path.** Repair on the CRT installed base (roughly $0.5-1B of US service spend, urgent,
unserved, and the cheapest possible way to earn a patient) → the custom chair and seating sale on the
same relationship, inside a US CRT market of $5.27B in 2024 heading to $8.26B by 2034 → all
custom-fitted mobility and seating → the full home medical equipment base an ageing and disabled
population lives on, a roughly $60B US category whose service layer alone is a $44B-to-$90B global
market, and where every incumbent is a low-margin distributor of someone else's catalogue.

**Scores** — Wedge 4 · Pattern 4 · Market 4 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 5 →
composite **4.2**

**Biggest risk.** The market is reimbursement-capped and grows at about 4.6% a year, so this is an
operations company with a software core rather than the reverse — margins are earned by execution, not
by a multiple. And Hike Medical sits one device class over with $22.5M already raised; if the
underlying capability is genuinely general, the honest question is whether complex rehab is the second
market Hike enters rather than a company anyone starts from scratch.

## Notes for the operator

Two things are worth carrying forward.

**First, the kill distribution flipped.** Nine of fourteen deaths were occupancy, only two
config-change. That is the opposite of every run since the method changed on 2026-08-19, and it means
the failure mode has moved. Narrowing a leader's product is no longer the main way these ideas die;
re-pointing a leader's *mechanism* is, because when a mechanism is good the same re-point occurs to
several funded teams inside a year. The practical consequence for the next run is that the occupancy
search should happen **before** the wedge is written, not after — nine candidates were developed to a
full four-part specification and then killed by a single search.

**Second, the survivor's tell is reusable.** Every other candidate died to an occupant who was trying
to *replace* the incumbent. The one that lived has an occupant — Kalogon — who sells *through* the
incumbent, because the incumbent is a distributor and the occupant is a supplier. A market where the
best AI-native player has taken the supplier position is a market where the operator position is still
empty, and the reason it is empty is usually structural: the incumbent's margin comes from
distributing, and the AI-native company took the easier revenue. That is a specific, checkable
question to ask of the next twenty candidates — *is the nearest funded competitor selling to the
incumbent or against it?* — and it is cheaper to answer than a full wedge check.

One honest caveat on this run. The scan led with traction rather than with dated legal events, which
is what the method asks for, and it produced one survivor rather than the two or three the last four
runs produced from statutes. That may mean the traction-first route is weaker; it may equally mean
five consecutive statute-led runs have exhausted the dated-liability seam and the ledger is finally
showing it. One run is not enough to tell. Worth deliberately running the next pass statute-led again,
so the two are comparable.
