**Trend Scout — 2026-08-15**

**2 survivors (composites 4.6 and 4.2, one briefed), 16 kills, 2 more dropped on sight.**

Both survivors come out of one collision. The big electrical equipment that connects anything to the power grid — transformers the size of a small house — now takes **two to three years**, and the only way to get one is to put money down years early to hold a place in a factory's build queue. At the same time **a third to a half of the data centres planned for this year are being delayed or cancelled**. So some companies are stranded holding expensive places in line they no longer need, while others are stuck waiting years for exactly that. They can't swap, because every unit is custom-built to the buyer's own hundred-page rulebook and nobody can work out quickly whether one company's part-built machine could be adapted to another company's site. As of the last fortnight, a machine can answer that — cheaply (DeepSeek's new model, 12 Aug) and fast enough to do it mid-phone-call (OpenAI and Cerebras' 14×-speed preview, 13 Aug).

**1. Slot Exchange — a marketplace for places in the transformer queue.** *(4.6, briefed)*
A marketplace where a company holding a paid, unwanted place in a factory's build queue can sell it to a company that's desperate for one, with a machine deciding in minutes — instead of an engineer deciding in weeks — whether the half-built equipment can be adapted to the buyer's site. It takes a cut of every trade, and later buys the stranded positions itself and resells them into the shortage.

**2. The make-to-stock transformer company.** *(4.2)*
Every one of these transformers is made to order because each utility has its own decades-old list of requirements, so nothing can be built in advance and kept on a shelf — which is *why* the wait is three years. This company reads every utility's list, works out the handful of standard designs that would satisfy almost all of them, builds a factory that makes only those, and keeps them in stock. Customers waiting three years get one in weeks and will pay a great deal for it.

**From the kills:** nine of sixteen died to someone who already ships it — agent identity to Microsoft and Okta, robot exception-handling to the teleoperation firms, buried-water-pipe prediction to a company that already sold to Kurita, AI-output warranties to Lloyd's-backed insurers. The most useful kill was the one that *named* the survivor: trading places in the electricity grid's connection queue is dead because the grid operators deliberately wrote rules to make those places non-transferable — which is what pointed at the equipment order one link downstream, where nobody has.

**One new standing rule:** twenty-seven runs produced a book of *models sold to someone who's deciding*. This is the first idea that **moves an asset and earns on the move**. From now on, once a pile of documents becomes machine-readable, ask whether it lets a market clear — not just a decision get informed.

**Next step is a contract review, not another scan.** Pull six transformer purchase and slot-reservation agreements and read the assignment clauses. If these positions can't legally be handed to another buyer, survivor 1 is dead and survivor 2 is the company. One week of legal reading decides it.
