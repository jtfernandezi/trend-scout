*Trend Scout — 2026-09-01*

*Survivors: 0.* First zero-survivor run in the book's history. Nine ideas were built out in full and
nine were killed — six because someone funded was already doing it, two because the market leader
could add it as a feature, one because the whole category has never produced a big company.

*What was scanned.* Owner.com ($240M at $2.3B, past $100M ARR — one AI system runs a small
restaurant's whole website, ordering, phone and marketing), Instinct ($250M at $2.5B for a personal
assistant you text that actually books things), Adaptyv Bio ($40M, the robot lab that tests
AI-designed proteins, revenue up 10x), Apate.AI (fake AI victims that keep scammers talking — 2.5M
conversations inside Commonwealth Bank), Airbound ($37M, delivery drones flying hospital blood
samples in India at truck prices), Greyparrot ($27M, cameras over recycling belts whose data a UK
regulator now accepts as official).

*The useful finding.* Owner.com sits in the biggest blank space in the whole idea book — 297 previous
ideas, not one about restaurants. So it got the full treatment: six different ways of narrowing it
were tested, and all six were already occupied. That pattern is now closed and written up so no
future run re-mines it.

*The lesson worth keeping.* Every dead idea this run came with a true sentence — "the big company
can't follow us here." Six times it was true and useless, because the company named wasn't actually
the leader in the smaller market we'd picked. Owner can't sell to franchise owners; SOCi already
does. Airbound can't fly in America; Zipline already does. New rule: once you narrow the buyer, ask
who leads *that* market before writing anything else.

*One live thread.* A customs rule the book has been watching took effect today — penalties for
import mistakes can no longer be reduced below half. The provision that decides whether that idea is
buildable lands around 30 November.
