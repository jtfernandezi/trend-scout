## Meta-signal: the mandate-first experiment failed, and why

The 2026-11-14 note predicted that a buyer created by a date would be less likely to be occupied than a segment next to
a fresh raise. **Six of six mandate candidates died, five of them to occupancy or config change.** The pattern is
consistent:

1. **A rule with a public date gets a vendor queue before it takes effect.** Every rule tried here was final for 12-24
   months before its date, and that was long enough for incumbents (SAS, Maximus, Allyant, Safexpert) and compliance
   startups (Trusaic, Fruition) to position.
2. **Mandated compliance work is usually not AI-native at depth.** It is reconciliation, form-filling or tagging. Where
   AI does the job, the price falls toward cents (Title II remediation), and the market shrinks as it is served.
3. **Dates slip.** The Title II deadline moved a year. The CMMC Phase 2 suspension killed ts-0435 the same way, and the
   EU AI Act's high-risk date moved to 2027-12. A dated trigger is weaker than it looks.

Combined with three straight raise-first zero runs, the lesson is that **adjacent-segment re-pointing in crowded
2026 categories is close to exhausted at this cadence.** The last survivors (ts-0586, ts-0594) came from a different
shape: **becoming the licensed, capitalised risk-bearer or seat-holder in a market where the software leader would have
to change business model.** That structural move, not the source of the pattern, is what survived the tests.
