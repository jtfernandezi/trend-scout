# Trend Scout — Report 2026-08-11

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (twenty-sixth run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).

**Outcome:** **1 surviving idea (composite 4.1, briefed), 9 logged kills, 8 further crosses discarded as ledger duplicates or obvious occupants before spending a check.** The zero-survivor streak ends at three.

The last three runs all closed with the same instruction: stop running general generative passes, because three search *directions* — capability-first, structure-first, unit-first — had each been exhausted, and the 08-08 Axiom Trust result argued the direction was not the variable at all. I ran a pass anyway, because that is what the schedule asks. What I changed was neither the direction nor the unit but the **filter**: I applied the corrected heuristic the 08-04 run derived from its own failure and then never got to test.

That rule is: **a billable-hour market is necessary but not sufficient — occupancy is the default, and what makes an exception is a technical barrier a services-replacement startup cannot clear in a seed round.** Nine of this run's candidates failed it, mostly in the way the rule predicts. One passed, and it passed for exactly the stated reason: the workflow around it is thoroughly occupied, the *judgement* inside it is not, and the reason the judgement is unoccupied is that it requires certification-grade physics recomputation rather than document generation.

Second thing worth stating up front: the sharpest fact this run produced is not about AI. **Boeing's permitted production rate is now a function of a quality metric.** In March 2026 Boeing leadership stated that numerical caps have been replaced by Safety Management System requirements — as long as traveled work and quality escapes stay stable, rate increases are cleared. Against an eleven-year order backlog, that makes an internal quality statistic one of the most valuable numbers in manufacturing. I have not seen that framed as a market anywhere.

## Surviving idea

### 1. As-built disposition engine — the engineering judgement inside factory nonconformance, at the one place a regulator has made production rate a function of quality evidence

**In plain English.** When a factory building aeroplane parts finds something slightly wrong with a part — a hole drilled a fraction off, a scratch too deep, a bracket a millimetre out of position — somebody has to decide whether the part is still safe to fly, or must be reworked, repaired or thrown away. Today a senior engineer works that out by hand, and it takes days, and there are fewer of those engineers every year because they are retiring. Meanwhile the unfinished job travels down the line with the aeroplane, and the amount of that unfinished work is the exact number the American aviation regulator now watches to decide whether Boeing is allowed to build faster. This is software that makes that decision in minutes and shows its work: it looks at the defect, pulls up the original engineering that certified the design, recalculates whether the real part is still strong enough, and writes the justification an authorised engineer signs. Aircraft manufacturers and their suppliers pay for it because every day of that queue is money, and because clearing it is what unlocks permission to build more planes — against a backlog of seventeen thousand aircraft they cannot deliver for eleven years.

**Who buys it.** The head of quality or chief engineer at an aerostructures Tier-1 or an airframer's final assembly line, at the moment a nonconformance report is raised on a part that is already installed or already late — the point where the job either closes or becomes traveled work.

**Why no one already serves them.** The quality-management vendors (ETQ, Sparta, Siemens Opcenter, QT9, SG Systems, iFactory) own the *record* and route the review but have no structural-analysis competence anywhere in their architecture; the simulation vendors (Ansys, PhysicsX, Neural Concept, Luminary Cloud) sell design-stage surrogates to engineering teams and have no presence on the factory floor or in the record of airworthiness; GroundControl (YC S25, 70+ facilities) sells the documentation half and has publicly located the problem in paperwork rather than part nonconformance; and the party doing the work today is a billable-hour engineering services firm — Quest Global publishes its own thought leadership on resolving aerospace nonconformances — whose revenue is precisely what the engine deletes.

**Scores.** Novelty 3.5 · Why-now 4.5 · Market 4.5 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 4 · Founder-fit 4 → **composite 4.1**

**Biggest risk.** The disposition is a *signed* act by an individual with delegated authority under AS9100 and, for some dispositions, customer or government source-inspection approval — so this is a sale into the most conservative organisation inside the most conservative industry, and the honest failure mode is that the engine becomes a decision-support tool the engineer still redoes by hand, which halves the value and destroys the pricing. GroundControl is the nearest neighbour, is already inside the buyer, and does not have far to walk.

## What this run establishes

**1. The corrected 08-04 heuristic works, and this run is its first real test.** The rule — *billable-hour market **plus** a technical barrier a services-replacement startup cannot clear in a seed round* — was derived from a run in which the billable-hour half alone produced six candidates and killed all six. Applied properly, it did two useful things today. It correctly predicted the kills: ts-0183, ts-0184, ts-0188 and ts-0191 all sit in billable-hour markets whose technical barrier turned out to be low enough that a funded company had already cleared it. And it correctly located the one survivor, by the specific test of *where inside a served workflow the incumbents stop*. The general lesson is worth stating in the form the operator can reuse: **the occupied thing is almost always the document, the record or the routing; the unoccupied thing is the judgement the document reports.** GroundControl automates the FAI report; nobody computes the disposition. ETQ holds the nonconformance; nobody decides it. That is a repeatable place to look, and it is narrower and more useful than "find a billable-hour market."

**2. Government funding of a capability is now a first-class kill, and it fired twice in one run.** ts-0185 died to a Navy-funded Senvol programme and ts-0186 to DoD acquisition reform making producibility a formal requirement. This is the ts-0139 pattern, and it is worse than ordinary occupancy: when the government funds the capability, it does not merely staff the square, it writes the acceptance standard, and a private company then has to sell against the standard rather than into a gap. **Any cross whose structural half is a government programme should now be checked for a government-funded technical programme *before* it is checked for startups.** That check is cheap and this run would have saved two candidates' worth of work by running it first.

**3. The compliance-rail template should be formally closed, not merely deprioritised.** The European Parliament pushed EU AI Act high-risk obligations to December 2027. That is the fourth data point after ts-0043, ts-0097, ts-0102 and ts-0110, and it removes the last live forcing function. The deeper reason is structural rather than schedule-driven: a compliance rail's why-now is a date, and dates move; an idea whose entire urgency is a deadline has a why-now that the legislature can delete. **Add to the standing pre-filters: no idea whose why-now is solely a compliance date.**

**4. Held book.** No new evidence bearing on **ts-0133**, **ts-0048** or **ts-0077** this run. **ts-0141 (firmware re-hosting)** picks up an incidental confirmation rather than a hardening: memory shortages have escalated into Washington lobbying, which is what a two-year structural squeeze looks like at the political layer, and is consistent with the 08-06 revalidation. **ts-0032 (DoD autonomy assurance)** picks up a directional note: Archer's 2026-08-10 acquisition of Boeing's Wisk, Insitu and SkyGrid, folding them under a purpose-built aerospace-and-defence foundation model with Boeing taking a stake, consolidates autonomy inside the primes — which cuts both ways for an independent assurance lab, raising the stakes of the fielding decision while shrinking the number of counterparties who might pay an outsider to make it. Novelty caution from 2026-07-28 unchanged.

## Takeaway for next run

1. **Run the survivor's technical claim, not another scan.** For ts-0192 the question a buyer will actually argue is: *what must a disposition engine prove that a stress engineer with Ansys and the certification data package cannot?* The candidate answer is that the engineer can compute one margin against one idealised geometry, while the engine can compute the *joint* effect of the actual as-built deviation stack on the certified basis and carry the evidence — but that is a claim, and it is testable against one real nonconformance report without any novelty search. That is the same instruction the last three runs gave for ts-0133 and ts-0141, and it is now the instruction for three candidates rather than two.
2. **Add the two new pre-filters** established above — check for a government-funded technical programme before checking for startups; and reject any idea whose why-now is only a compliance date. Both would have saved work today.
3. **The binding constraint is still a phone call, and it has been for five runs.** Five candidates now wait on one conversation with a real buyer: a sustaining-engineering lead at a Tier-1, a litigation-support director at a trucking firm, and now a head of quality at an aerostructures supplier. Twenty-six runs of desk research produced them. A twenty-seventh will not validate them.
