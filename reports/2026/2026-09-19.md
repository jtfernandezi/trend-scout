# Trend Scout — Report 2026-09-19

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (forty-second run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).
**Outcome:** **0 surviving ideas, 12 logged kills, one held-book entry updated.**

This is a zero-survivor run, and it is a zero-survivor run *by design of the previous run's
instruction* rather than by accident. The 2026-09-17 report ended with a specific experiment: stop
opening on the AI-native-licensed-firm shape, which had produced the last two survivors, and open
instead on product mechanisms with hard traction. That instruction was followed literally. Twelve
candidates were developed to a full four-part specification off six product mechanisms — answer-engine
visibility, agentic industrial procurement, continuous due diligence, video-trained manipulation,
consumer capture hardware, and the post-August Fannie Mae condo cliff — and **all twelve died, eleven
of them inside one or two searches.**

**The finding is the run.** The services frame was not a rut. It was where the openings are, and this
run is the control experiment that shows why. A product-shaped opening in 2026 is occupied in weeks,
because the thing that makes it a product — no licence, no bonding, no clinical staff, no physical
plant, no procurement cycle — is exactly what lets five funded teams reach it simultaneously. Three of
this run's twelve kills were to occupants who shipped the identical product **within the last sixty
days**: Rexera's Condo Project Review landed on the Fannie Mae cliff before the cliff arrived and the
company now sits inside RealPage; Profound already sells a pharma tier with medical review as a named
use case; the agentic deduction-dispute agents are the named trade story of 2026. The licensed-firm
shape survives searches not because it is cleverer but because the barrier that keeps the leader out
also keeps the other eleven startups out.

**The kill distribution.** Eight of twelve on the occupancy test, three on the config-change test (all
three in the strongest form — the leader had already executed it), one on occupancy plus segment
viability. Occupancy has now led the kill table for seven consecutive runs.

## Four mechanism-level conclusions

**(a) The answer-engine category is closed, and this ledger should stop entering it.** ts-0411 and
ts-0415 join ts-0037, ts-0225 and ts-0235: five kills, five different angles — visibility, publisher
monetisation, attribution, the regulated brand, the fraud surface — and every one met a funded
occupant. Profound's own numbers explain why: revenue tripling in six months with a third of the
Fortune 100 already buying means every adjacent square is worth a funded team's time. **Do not open
another candidate here.** If the operator wants exposure to this shift it is an investment thesis, not
a company to found.

**(b) A dated regulatory cliff is now a *negative* signal for occupancy, not a positive one.** This
book has treated a hard compliance date as a why-now gift. ts-0421 shows the inversion: Fannie Mae and
Freddie Mac announced the retirement of Limited Review on 2026-03-18 for an 2026-08-03 effective date,
and by the time the date arrived Rexera had shipped the product and been absorbed by RealPage. A
publicly announced date with a lead time is a starting gun that everyone hears. **The dated trigger is
only worth anything when it sits behind a barrier that slows the people who hear it** — a licence, a
bonding requirement, a clinical workforce, a procurement cycle. A cliff in front of an open field is a
race this book cannot win from a standing start.

**(c) The instruction-three experiment resolved the 2026-09-17 open question, in the opposite
direction to the one it expected.** The last report worried that two consecutive survivors with the
same costume — AI-native licensed provider takes over a distressed public contract — might be a rut,
and told this run to open elsewhere. It did, across six mechanisms and twelve candidates, and returned
nothing. That is decent evidence the costume is a seam rather than a rut, and it identifies the
property that makes it one: **the same barrier that blocks the category leader also blocks the other
eleven startups**, which is not true of any product-shaped opening. The next run should go back to the
licensed and capital-barriered side deliberately, and should not treat that as a failure of
imagination.

**(d) The billing question introduced on 2026-09-17 worked, but it is a second-round filter, not a
first-round one.** It was asked of ts-0417, ts-0418 and ts-0421 and it passed cleanly in all three —
consultants, forensic auditors and condo-document reviewers all bill per unit of human work, so the
efficient path really does delete their revenue. All three died anyway, to occupancy. **Per-hour
billing tells you the incumbent will not build it. It says nothing about whether someone else already
did.** Keep the question, but keep it after the occupancy search, not before it.

## Surviving ideas

**None.** Twelve candidates, twelve kills, no idea cleared all three tests.

Reporting this honestly is worth more than forcing the twelfth-best candidate through. Two were close
enough to name, and both are worth understanding as near-misses rather than as unlucky:

- **ts-0421 (condo project of record)** had the best four parts of the run — a genuine dated cliff, a

  automation, and a compounding national asset where the second loan in a building is free. It lost to
  a competitor who found the same opening earlier and then acquired platform-owner distribution. There
  was no error in the reasoning, only in the timing, and the timing lesson is conclusion (b).

  genuinely still be open; it died on reachability rather than on a shipped product. A founder with an
  existing route into the site licence companies would be entitled to re-examine it. This operator
  does not have that route, so for this book it is dead.

## Method note for the next run

**One. Search occupancy before writing the four parts, not after.** This run spent full specifications
on twelve candidates and eleven of them could have been killed by the first search. The order in Step 3
and Step 4 is backwards for a ledger this mature: the segment-plus-workflow search is cheap, decisive,
and it is what actually kills. Write the four parts only for candidates that survive one occupancy
query. The saving is roughly half the run's effort, which should be spent on more candidates rather
than better-written dead ones.

**Two. Go back to the barriered side, deliberately, and pick the barrier first.** Conclusion (c) says
the licensed, bonded, clinically staffed, publicly procured side is where openings persist. So invert
the search order: **start from a barrier** — a licence a software company cannot hold, a capital or
bonding requirement, a workforce that takes years to certify, a public procurement that takes a year to
win — and only then ask what job sits behind it. Three barriers not yet worked in this ledger and worth
opening on: **state-licensed insurance adjusting and appraisal**, **accredited laboratory
certification outside the killed testing-network frame**, and **court-appointed roles other than the
fiduciary and guardianship one already killed at ts-0405**.

**Three. Treat the YC Fall 2026 RFS as a thirteen-square exclusion zone, and say so in the kill log.**
Demo Day is 2 December, which means a funded cohort is aimed at every one of those squares right now
and will be visible by the January runs. Any candidate landing inside one of them — physical-world
operating systems, agent-native personal software, self-healing integrations, low-cost interceptors and
ground-combat logistics, ocean data centres — should be killed on arrival unless the barrier test in
note two is satisfied. That is not caution; it is the same arithmetic that killed ts-0421.
