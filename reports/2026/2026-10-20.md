# Trend Scout — Report 2026-10-20

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (fifty-fifth run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).
**Outcome:** **Zero surviving ideas. 6 new kills (ts-0526 to ts-0531).** No brief this run. No held-book entry
changes status.

This run followed the 2026-10-17 method note:

- **The skip list was respected.** Ramp, Stand, Voltus, Scarlet, Albert Invent and Suki repeated on this week's
  trackers and were not re-pointed. Blackwall (100M dollar Series C at 2.2bn dollars, web-traffic and bot
  management) was not re-pointed, because agent security is skipped.
- **Already-worked names were filtered before any flanking.** Healthleap (ts-0490, ts-0491 and the 2026-10-08
  run), Gallatin AI and Outmarket all appear in the ledger, so they were not worked again.
- **Industrial and energy Series A rounds with disclosed ARR (note three) found nothing new.** Searches turned up
  Novele (September), Almetra (June) and Arrakis (July), all outside the window and none with disclosed revenue.
- **Method note one from 2026-10-15 decided ts-0528.** The IV&V seat passed the wedge test. Sizing the mandate
  first showed it is shrinking.

**The finding worth carrying forward: this week's money went to horizontal products, and every new vertical name
was either seed-stage or already worked.** The large rounds with hard traction were Town (assistant, 1bn dollars
in four months), Greptile (code review, 1% of GitHub pull requests), Modal (passed 1bn dollars of run-rate),
Tenstorrent (chips) and Ramp. All of them are horizontal or infrastructure, and the leaders can follow into
nearly any segment with a config change. The vertical rounds that were new to the ledger were small: Chariot
Claims (10M dollar Series A), Pathways (6M dollar seed) and Solvera (6.3M dollar pre-seed). **"Independent AI
validator" (Greptile) is the one horizontal mechanism with a structural angle, the reviewer who is not the author.**
Its flanks died two ways. Where the code lives in a vendor's proprietary IDE, the IDE owner holds the data model
(ts-0527). Where the seat is mandated, the mandate turned out to be shrinking (ts-0528).

## Breakout startups scanned

1. **Greptile**: **50M dollar Series B led by Menlo Ventures at 750M dollars, 2026-10-19** (Benchmark, YC,
   Initialized, Jump Trading, Wing). It reviews and tests **more than 1% of all GitHub pull requests every day** for
   thousands of teams (Nvidia, Coinbase, Decagon, Vanta). Revenue is not disclosed. **Mechanism:** an independent
   AI reviewer, separate from whoever wrote the code, validates each change before merge. It matters now because
   agents write much of the code.
2. **Town**: **108M dollar Series B co-led by Forerunner and Menlo at 1bn dollars, 2026-10-19**, four months
   after launch. It reached **seven-figure ARR within weeks of its June launch** (company-reported). **Mechanism:**
   an assistant with memory over a person's email and calendar does follow-up and coordination work proactively,
   for individuals and teams.
3. **Chariot Claims**: **10M dollar Series A led by Silverton Partners**, October 2026, 15.7M dollars raised in
   total. It claims more than **200,000 pre-qualified consumer claims a month**, but only about 80 cases have
   been pursued since it changed model, so intake volume is not filed volume. **Mechanism:** AI matches consumers
   to misconduct patterns and assembles thousands of individual arbitration claims, so class-action waivers become
   leverage.
4. **Modal**: **750M dollar Series D led by Accel at 15.75bn dollars**. It passed **1bn dollars of annualised
   run-rate**, and Runpod passed 325M dollars of ARR the same week. **Not re-pointed:** GPU cloud is horizontal
   infrastructure with no segment block.
5. **Pathways**: **6M dollar seed led by Sorenson Capital** (week ending 2026-10-19). Its largest contract is
   **over 1M dollars with Heidelberg Materials**. **Mechanism:** models turn plant, supplier and energy data into
   the environmental product declarations that specifiers and procurement rules require.
6. **Solvera**: **6.3M dollar pre-seed led by Seedcamp, 2026-10-15** (Oslo). It began operating in July 2026 and
   reports **about 0.9M to 1M dollars of contracted revenue in three months with nine people**
   (company-reported). **Mechanism:** an engineering firm of record whose agents do the MEP design while licensed
   engineers sign, sold per outcome.

Also scanned and not re-pointed:
- **Tenstorrent**: 1.5bn dollar Series E at 6.2bn dollars. Chips, not a startup-idea pattern.
- **Abby Care**: 82M dollar Series C. It pays family caregivers through Medicaid, with thousands of families in
  eight states. It is not AI-native.
- **Probably Genetic**: 12M dollar Series A, with data from 130,000 patients. Rare-disease patient finding is
  already mined (ts-0336 area).
- **Readily**: 11M dollar Series A, with 10x revenue growth. Healthcare compliance is on the skip list.
- **RapidCanvas**: 29M dollar Series B, with ARR up eight-fold over six quarters. Horizontal enterprise AI.
- **Tolan**: 30M dollars (USV), an AI companion. No traction found.
- **Healthleap**, **Gallatin AI** and **Outmarket**: worked on earlier runs.

**Patterns worked and whether they are exhausted:**
- **Greptile (independent AI validator): two flanks tried, both closed.** Code locked in vendor IDEs belongs to the
  IDE owners, and the government IV&V mandate is shrinking. Safety-critical code was closed earlier (ts-0033,
  ts-0399). The pattern still supports only the defence SETA seat already held (ts-0470).
- **Chariot Claims: closed on both sides.** The claimant side is held by Chariot, FinLegal and the plaintiff field
  (ts-0230, ts-0341). The defence side is too few events a year, and its settlement stage is occupied (ts-0427).
- **Town: no durable flank.** A horizontal assistant reaches regulated buyers with connectors, and the advisor
  vertical is already held by Jump.
- **Pathways and Solvera: closed.** Both are seed-stage leaders whose flanks are config changes. In both, the
  incumbents bill per unit or per project, so AI helps them.

## Method note for the next run

**One. When the tape is mostly horizontal, rank flanks by whether the leader sells to the segment's adversary.**
Of this run's angles, only the channel-conflict ones (the defence side of Chariot, independent review against
integrators) produced a real structural block. The geography and compliance flanks of horizontal leaders were all
config changes. Next time the week's breakouts are horizontal, test channel conflict first.

**Two. Treat seed and pre-seed "leaders" as weak patterns, and flank them only if a category cluster exists.**
Pathways and Solvera have real contracted revenue, but their segments are their own roadmap. Kills against
seed-stage leaders are almost always config changes.

**Three. Before writing a mandate-seat idea, check whether the regulator has recently relaxed the mandate**, not
only how many mandates exist. One vendor sentence (PCG on CMS IV&V guidance) settled ts-0528.

**Four. Skip for now:** everything from the 2026-10-08 to 2026-10-17 lists, plus Greptile, Town, Chariot
Claims, Pathways, Solvera, Modal and Runpod, Tenstorrent, Blackwall, mass-claims defence, PLC and industrial
code review, government IV&V, advisor assistants, and EPD automation.
