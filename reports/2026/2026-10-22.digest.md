*Trend Scout — 2026-10-22*

*0 survivors, 9 kills.* No idea cleared all three gates this run.

*What we tried.* This week's winners were:
- ASI: AI that predicts and plans air traffic and logistics; raised 325M dollars at a 3bn dollar valuation, and holds an FAA contract worth up to 875M dollars.
- Arda: AI that turns a product design into a working factory line; valued at 1.5bn dollars, and already used by Anduril.
- Eka: robot hands that can feel what they grip; raised 150M dollars at 1.45bn dollars.
- Longeye: AI that reads every file in a police case to find missed evidence; raised 20M dollars, and is used by 50+ agencies.
- Qualitate: AI that interviews hundreds of experts at once; revenue up more than tenfold this year.
- Also Pigment (business planning, 100M dollars a year in revenue), Baba (Medicaid paperwork help), Daytona (safe computers for AI agents) and Otter (meeting notes).

*What died, in plain English.*
- AI that sorts police evidence for UK trials: the UK government is building this itself.
- AI tools for the bodies that investigate police: too few of them, with too little money.
- AI interviews of doctors for drug companies: THREAD, Koji and others already do it.
- AI that writes city budgets: OpenGov and ClearGov already do it.
- AI air-traffic prediction for other countries: ASI can sell abroad itself, and big incumbents win those contracts.
- Safe computers for AI agents on secret government networks: not really an AI product, and there are many alternatives.
- Robots that assemble aircraft wiring: Eka is already testing exactly this.
- An AI-run court-transcript firm: Steno already is one, and existing firms make more money with AI.
- AI that helps Medicaid patients appeal denials: the patients can't pay, and nobody else funds it.

*Lesson.* When a government is the buyer, check whether it is building the tool itself before looking for an opening. And when a startup can't serve a buyer because of a conflict of interest, first check that the buyer on the other side has money to spend.
