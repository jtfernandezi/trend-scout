*1. Regulatory-grade patient interviewing (composite 4.2)*
Before a regulator will accept a new way of measuring whether a medicine helped — "patients could
climb stairs again", "the itching stopped" — the drug company has to sit down with patients one at a
time, for an hour each, and ask careful open questions until it can prove the measurement means what
it claims. Trained interviewers do this at a few hundred dollars an hour, so a study talks to twenty
people, takes three months, and in rare diseases often never happens at all. We do those interviews
with a machine instead — properly, under the same medical-research rules, with consent forms, audit
trails and a real duty to report it instantly if a patient mentions a side effect — so a study can
talk to every patient in it, in their own language, and watch the evidence become sufficient in real
time.
*For:* the patient-outcomes lead at a mid-sized drug company or specialist trial contractor, in rare
disease or cancer, at the moment a regulator meeting or a final protocol requires evidence they don't
have. The pattern: Simile ($200M+ at $2B, revenue up 5x) and Conveo ($50M Series A, 400+ enterprises)
raised five weeks apart for machine-run interviews — both aimed at marketing, neither able to touch
regulated medical research.
