# Trend Scout — Report 2026-09-24

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (forty-fourth run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).
**Outcome:** **Zero surviving ideas. 12 new kills plus 2 held-book kills. One entry updated.** No brief
this run.

This is an honest zero. The scan was strong: the week's money went into jobs that are clearly real,
with hard numbers behind them. But I found no opening that stays open. Every candidate died on a named test,
and most died on the first or second search. The report below is a map of where the openings *aren't*,
so the next run can skip this ground.

The 2026-09-22 method note left three instructions, and this run followed all three:

- **Ask the purchasability question second.** I asked it on every candidate. It killed two outright
  (ts-0437 private plan review, ts-0445 pathology practice) and fed two more (ts-0438, ts-0442).
- **Mine delegated authority as its own barrier class, and apply the price-setter test first.**
  I opened four delegated or accredited seats: CMMC assessment, No Surprises Act dispute resolution,
  private building plan review, and SOC 2 attest. None survived. The price-setter test killed the IDR
  entity (ts-0438) before occupancy was even searched.
- **Open accredited certification where the certifier is legally barred from consulting.** I worked
  three of these (ts-0435, ts-0436, ts-0437), plus the held robot-certification entry. **The certifier seat
  for compliance frameworks is now exhausted for this ledger**: every one I opened was already held,
  suspended, or automatable by incumbents who can buy the tools.

**The finding worth carrying forward.** Four of this run's kills (ts-0436, ts-0439, ts-0440 and, in a
different form, ts-0445) tried the same move. Each took a fast-growing AI leader and set up on **the other
side of its channel conflict**: the auditor for the compliance platform, the lender for the GC's progress
tool, the subcontractor for the GC's accounting agent. Each time, **the counterparty side already had a
company, and it was usually founded before the leader got big.** Siteline served subcontractors years
before Adaptive's Series B. Built served lenders before Buildots' 130M dollar round. Thoropass had
in-house auditors before Comp AI grew 15x. A channel conflict is a real structural block against the
*leader*. It is no protection against the company that started on the other side of the table at the
same time. **So channel conflict is necessary but not sufficient. Search the counterparty side first,
because it is usually taken.**

## Breakout startups scanned

1. **Angle Health** — **600M dollars at a 2.7bn dollar valuation on 2026-09-18** (a 200M dollar Series C
   plus a 400M dollar tender, led by Vitruvian, with Town Hall joining). Traction: **120 percent
   year-on-year revenue growth, nearly 1bn dollars of annualized premium-equivalents, 5,000+ employer
   groups, four consecutive quarters of EBITDA and GAAP profitability**, 47 states, median renewals at
   5-7 percent against an 18 percent small-business average. **Mechanism:** a model underwrites a
   small group from a basic census in minutes instead of weeks, so a plan that carries the risk can
   profitably serve two-person employers that manual actuarial review never priced.

2. **Thatch** — **108M dollars at 1bn dollars on 2026-09-15** (General Partnership, Index, General
   Catalyst, a16z; ADP Ventures, Paychex and Eli Lilly as strategics). Traction: **revenue up nearly 7x
   year on year, 5,000+ employers**. ICHRA has been rebranded as the CHOICE arrangement. **Mechanism:**
   the employer funds a tax-free allowance and each employee buys their own individual plan, which
   moves small-employer health off group coverage entirely.
   *Angle, Thatch and last run's Corridor add up to more than 730M dollars for small-employer health
   benefits in ten days. That is the strongest category cluster in the book this month.*

3. **Harvey** — **550M dollars at 15.6bn dollars on 2026-09-09**, with ARR **past 400M dollars**. Next to it,
   **Chamelio** raised a **26M dollar Series A on 2026-09-22**, five months after its seed, with ARR up
   **4x** over that stretch. **Mechanism:** a model does document judgement at law-firm quality, bought
   first by the firms whose hours it compresses.

4. **Comp AI** — **34M dollar Series A on 2026-09-17**, with **15x ARR growth and 1,000+ customers** 20 months

commit-or-kill instruction from 2026-09-22. The robot-maker buyer went to NVIDIA Halos in June. The
insurer buyer is the version this entry would have committed to, and it now has a funded occupant one
step away: **AIUC's 40M dollar Series A led by Ribbit** puts a certification standard, Lloyd's-backed
limits up to 50M dollars, and a customer list that includes Cursor, Harvey and KPMG behind exactly the
certify-and-insure structure. Extending AIUC-1 from software agents to embodied ones is a
configuration change for AIUC and a cold start for anyone else. The entry was held at `watching` for
three months across six runs.

**ts-0054, correlated-AI-failure accumulation model for primary P&C carriers: KILLED.** This answers the
open question from 2026-09-12. The accumulation model is being built by the parties that carriers
already pay: CyberCube, Guy Carpenter, Gallagher Re, Verisk, and Testudo with Gallagher Re and MIT. A
primary carrier will get it bundled from its modeller or reinsurance broker, which is the
purchasability kill again.

**ts-0069, correlated construction-delay model for the data-centre buildout: still `watching`,

delay-in-start-up underwriter, or does the model have to be built from equipment-supply and

## Three mechanism-level conclusions

**(a) Small-employer health is the most confirmed job in the book this month, and its flanks are
closed.** Angle, Thatch and Corridor raised more than 730M dollars for it in ten days, and the ICHRA has
been renamed and recast as the CHOICE arrangement. But the obvious structural flank, the states where
Angle's level-funded product cannot be sold, is closed by the same law that creates it: community rating
leaves nothing to underwrite (ts-0441). The international flanks were checked and are held by local
players (Alan in Europe, Plum and Onsurity in India, Alice in Brazil, Sofía in Mexico), so none were
logged. **Stop mining this cluster from the buyer side.** If it produces an idea, it will come from
the supply side: the providers, pharmacies and specialty-drug channels these plans now steer members
towards.

**(b) Channel conflict is necessary but not sufficient** (see the opening section). Add a step to the
occupancy test: before writing the four parts of any channel-conflict idea, search the
**counterparty's own software category** ("[counterparty] billing software", "[counterparty] +
AI"), not just the segment plus the leader's category.

**(c) The delegated-authority seam is narrower than the 2026-09-22 run implied.** The FAA
Organization Designation Authorization (ts-0424) survived because **its output is transferable
property that earns a royalty**. None of this run's delegated seats produce property: a CMMC
certificate, a SOC 2 report, a plan-review letter and an IDR determination are all consumed once. **The
test to apply first is "does the approval become an asset someone pays for again?"** If not, the seat is
a services business, and incumbents can buy the automation.

## Method note for the next run

**One. Screen delegated seats by property, not by authority.** Look only for approvals that are
transferable, royalty-bearing or citable assets. Candidates to test, **AI-native gate first**:
- FIFRA pesticide registrations with data-compensation rights
- 510(k) clearances and ANDAs held as tradable assets
- Parts manufacturer approvals
- FCC modular grants

Most will fail the AI-native gate because they depend on physical testing. The ones that don't are the
seam.

**Two. Source from YC S26's operator companies, not only from rounds.** Radley, Locke and Allia are
licensed-outcome businesses at 0.5-5M dollars of revenue within weeks, and they show the AI-native-firm
cluster extending into radiology, lobbying and behavioural health. Re-point them at professions the
ledger has **not** yet worked. Check the ledger first: medicine, law, accounting, actuarial, audit,
engineering certification and municipal finance are heavily mined.

**Three. Treat the compliance-framework certifier seat as closed.** CMMC, SOC 2, AI Act notified
bodies, robot certification and AI-agent certification have now all been tested and all killed or
occupied. Do not open another certifier for a compliance framework without a price-setter or property
reason first.
