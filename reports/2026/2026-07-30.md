# Trend Scout — Report 2026-07-30

**Run type:** scheduled research pass (twenty-first run under the venture-scale-gated thesis).

**Outcome:** **1 surviving idea, 7 logged kills — the drought breaks at nine.**

Last run's takeaway was that a tenth identical generative pass had negative expected value, and I agree with the reasoning: the failure mode was not the method but the *territory*. Nine consecutive droughts were run against the same signal surface — data-centre buildout, agent infrastructure, insurance accumulation, healthcare payers, defence — which is exactly the surface every other founder reads. So this run changed the territory rather than the method. I led with the **two freshest frontier capabilities the ledger had barely touched** (productized world models, and the July-9 inference price collapse) and crossed them into industries that appear nowhere in 140 ledger entries: the tort system, the licensed-workforce pipeline, veterinary medicine, K-12 school choice, municipal credit, and mine tailings.

Six of eight crosses died in the usual way. One did not, and it died differently from everything else this project has looked at: **there is no funded startup applying world models to the evidentiary layer of litigation**, and the reason the square is empty is not that a platform owner is about to fill it (the ts-0130 pattern) — it is that the incumbents there are animation studios and expert-witness firms whose revenue *is* the labour the engine removes.

## Signals scanned

1. **Frontier inference collapsed again on 2026-07-09.** xAI shipped Grok 4.5 (Jul 8), then OpenAI's GPT-5.6 family (Sol/Terra/Luna) and Meta's Muse Spark 1.1 landed the same day, with Moonshot's open-source Kimi K3 on Jul 16 and Anthropic's Claude Opus 5 on Jul 24. High-quality inference now starts around **$1/M input and $4-6/M output**, against $25-50/M for legacy flagships — a 90-97% collapse for equivalent intelligence in two years. The structural consequence is not "AI is cheaper"; it is that **exhaustive** approaches (read every document, generate every candidate) beat **sampling** approaches for the first time.
2. **World models became a shipping product, not a demo.** DeepMind's Genie 3 generates interactive, physics- and collision-consistent 3D worlds at 24fps/720p — a simulation you control, not a video you watch. World Labs' Marble plus its **World API** (persistent assets, programmable interfaces) is live at marble.worldlabs.ai as of **2026-07-28**. The research layer has moved to physically-grounded Gaussian world models from video and to explicit *counterfactual world simulation models* that answer causal questions from CCTV.
3. **YC's Fall 2026 RFS** (applications closed Jul 27; batch runs Oct-Dec in SF) — 13 asks, thesis-summarised as "AI moving into the physical world": AI tutoring, defence, a cloud for small software, multiplayer agents, compute at sea, consumer AI for a billion people, aging care, operating systems for the physical world, crypto, data for the real world, proving you're human, AI-native compliance, self-maintaining APIs. Read this run mostly as a **saturation map** — five of my candidate crosses touched an RFS category and that counted against them.
4. **The licensed-workforce pipeline is jammed at a record level.** US nursing schools turned away **93,176 qualified applicants** in 2025-26, up ~30,000 in two years; the constraint is faculty and clinical placements, not seats. Boards already permit up to 50% simulation substitution, yet 29% of institutions substitute none. Downstream: 9.7M direct-care openings 2024-2034, only 19% of nursing homes meeting the CMS 2029 staffing minimum, and 25%+ of direct-care workers foreign-born into an enforcement wave.
5. **The tort system's physical-fact core is inflating and its evidence layer is pre-AI.** 15 trucking/automotive verdicts totalling **>$4.1B in 2024**; median nuclear verdict **$36M** (up ~50% from 2013); truck-tractor filings +3.7%/yr since 2014. Underneath: reconstruction costs $3-10k routine, **$25-50k+** on a major trucking case, produced by hand on FARO Zone 3D, HVE and PC-Crash by firms like Rimkus, Exponent and ARCCA.
6. **Courts are writing the reliability standard for machine-generated evidence right now.** Proposed **FRE 707** would subject machine output to Rule-702-style judicial gatekeeping. It was approved by the Judicial Conference, then sent back for revision by the Standing Committee in **June 2026**; **December 1, 2027** is the earliest possible effective date. The category's admissibility rules are being defined before anyone owns the category.
7. **First federal K-12 school-choice money starts flowing in 2027.** ECCA (enacted July 2025) creates a 100% federal tax credit up to $1,700 per donor, routed through newly-created Scholarship Granting Organizations; **30 states had opted in by mid-2026**, with governors filing SGO lists with Treasury each January 1. Qualified expenses include tutoring, curriculum and special-needs services — not just tuition.
8. **Critical minerals went from policy to funded programme in the last four days.** On **2026-07-27** DOE selected Metallic Minerals' La Plata project for Genesis Mission funding via AGAPEX, specifically to build agentic GeoAI tools for critical-minerals exploration, led by the Colorado School of Mines — on top of the March 2026 permitting executive order.

*(Also logged and not developed: the UK maritime ETS going live 2026-07-01 with EU ETS extending to methane/N2O — a compliance-rail shape this ledger has killed six times; and the July-2026 statement by 200+ economists including 16 Nobel laureates on AI-driven displacement, which routes into ts-0084's already-killed territory.)*

## Ideas killed (this is the signal — read it)

- **ts-0134 — Accreditable simulated-clinical-hours engine for nursing and allied health.** *(world models × the 93,176 turned-away applicants)* Killed twice over. The virtual-patient category is incumbent-held and already generative: Elsevier's Shadow Health (80+ digital standardized patients), UbiSim/Labster — which explicitly markets itself to programs to "expand clinical capacity," i.e. my exact wedge — Kaplan's i-Human, and PCS.ai. And the constraint that actually caps substitution isn't scenario supply: INACSL/NCSBN standards require a **qualified human facilitator and trained debriefer**, and the shortage is faculty. Standards-bound, no legal purchase — the ts-0122 failure mode.
- **ts-0135 — AI-native veterinary care network on the mid-level-practitioner unlock.** *(autonomous multimodal diagnosis × veterinary medicine's near-absent premarket gate)* The regulatory asymmetry is real and the market is $40B+, but the beachhead is one state: Colorado's Proposition 129 VPA role takes effect **2027-01-15**, first cohort graduates ~2027, and **95% of Colorado veterinarians opposed it**, with the AAVSB/VMG surveys and the vet-technician associations formally against. A single hostile state isn't a wedge, it's a pilot that can be legislated away. Pet-tech AI diagnostics is separately the sector's most-funded category.
- **ts-0136 — Qualified-expense and outcome adjudication rail for the ECCA scholarship money.** *(exhaustive cheap inference × the first federal K-12 choice dollars)* Failed the venture-scale gate on arithmetic: ECCA caps SGO administrative spend at a small share of receipts, so the entire national admin pool is a few hundred million and a vendor takes a slice of that. The rail itself is held by ClassWallet, Odyssey and Student First Technologies, and the category is YC RFS #12. The venture-scale version of this signal is to *be* the school — which is Alpha/2hr Learning, Primer and Synthesis, i.e. RFS #1.
- **ts-0137 — Pipeline-free, query-time enterprise data infrastructure.** *(inference at $1/M × the $30B ETL industry's inverted revenue model)* Killed on defensibility without spending a full novelty pass. Every incumbent already ships it: Databricks, Fivetran, Airbyte and Matillion are all named in 2026 "AI ETL" coverage, DreamFactory already sells ETL elimination, Hevo already sells automatic schema management. The thesis is correct and there is nowhere to stand.
- **ts-0138 — Continuous full-corpus municipal credit intelligence.** *(exhaustive reading × a $4T market with ~1M CUSIPs of PDF disclosure)* The premise "nobody has read the corpus" is no longer true. MuniBonds.AI already sells automated EMMA monitoring with per-bond material-event timelines; DPC Data launched an **AI-ready structured municipal dataset in March 2026** for exactly this ingestion problem; Lumesis/DIVER, MMA, Solve, ICE and Bloomberg hold the layer above.
- **ts-0139 — Secondary-source (tailings) critical-minerals discovery engine.** *(geoscience foundation models × the permitting bottleneck)* Occupied by KoBold, Earth AI and VerAI — and then, three days before this run, **the federal government funded the capability as public research** (DOE Genesis Mission/AGAPEX → Colorado School of Mines agentic GeoAI, 2026-07-27). Tailings reprocessing operations are separately held by Phoenix Tailings.
- **ts-0140 — Continuous ambient case-mix derivation for skilled nursing.** *(cheap always-on inference × $3.3-5.3B of nursing-home revenue loss)* PointClickCare owns the EHR, the MDS workflow, the data and already sells case-mix analytics; Zimmet, Richter and Real Time Medical Systems fill the rest. And the value proposition is functionally indistinguishable from upcoding a federal payer — the wrong side of an enforcement wave, not a moat.

**Pattern worth keeping.** Applying last run's platform-absorption pre-filter *before* the novelty search would have saved two of these seven (ts-0137, ts-0140) — in both cases the incumbent owns the data the product needs. The new pre-filter this run suggests: **if a candidate's structural half is a category on the current YC RFS, treat it as pre-saturated** (that killed ts-0136 and shadowed two others). What survived did so partly because its structural half — the tort system — appears nowhere on the RFS.

## Surviving idea

### ts-0133 — Evidence-constrained world models: the reconstruction engine for the $443B tort system

**In plain English.** When someone is badly hurt in a truck crash or a fall, the fight in court is over *what actually happened* — how fast, who braked, whether the floor was wet, whether the injury fits the impact. Today lawyers pay an engineer $25,000-$50,000 to hand-build a cartoon animation of their preferred version of events, and the other side pays a different engineer to build the opposite cartoon. This company builds a machine that takes the real evidence — the scene photos, the dashcam and CCTV footage, the truck's own black-box data, the medical scans, the sworn testimony — and generates an accurate, physically realistic three-dimensional replay you can walk around inside and view from any angle. Crucially it also shows its work: which piece of evidence pinned down which fact, how certain each number is, and which *other* versions of events the evidence still allows. Law firms and insurance companies pay for it because it costs a fraction of the current bill, arrives in days instead of months, and — because it can prove where its answers came from — it stands up when a judge asks whether the computer can be trusted.

**Who buys it.** The trial lawyer or litigation-support director at a commercial-trucking / catastrophic-injury plaintiff firm, at the moment they retain a reconstruction expert on a case worth $1M+ — a routine, already-budgeted $25-50k line item. Then defence counsel and their carriers; then the same engine runs upstream inside P&C claims, adjudicating causation on millions of first-party auto and property claims before anyone files suit.

**Why no one already serves them.** The people who could build it are structurally disqualified: the demonstrative-evidence studios (Austin Visuals, AttorneyProof3D, High Impact) sell artist-hours and the engine deletes their revenue; the forensic-engineering firms (Rimkus, Exponent, ARCCA) sell the expert's personal credibility on the stand and productizing it cannibalises the billable hour; the reconstruction software (FARO Zone 3D, HVE, PC-Crash, v-Crash) are deterministic solvers that answer *"simulate the scenario I typed in,"* not *"find every scenario this evidence permits"*; the legal-AI leaders (EvenUp, Darrow, Theo AI, Alexi, Eve) live entirely on the document layer and never touch the physical fact pattern; and the world-model labs are selling horizontal creation APIs into 3D content, not evidentiary constraint solvers.

**Scores.** Novelty 4 · Why-now 5 · Market 4 · Defensibility 4 · YC-fit 5 · Founder-fit 5 → **composite 4.5**

**Biggest risk.** Admissibility. If courts apply an FRE 707-style reliability gate and reject generatively-produced reconstructions outright, the trial use case dies. The honest mitigation is that ~95% of cases never reach a jury — the engine's value in mediation, settlement negotiation and insurer claims triage does not depend on a judge's ruling — but a founder should assume the first two years are spent building the reliability record, not the renderer, and should expect to have to fund the peer-reviewed validation work that makes the output defensible.

## What this run establishes

The nine-drought stretch was a **territory** problem, not a method problem, and this run is weak evidence for that. Eight crosses, six industries the ledger had never entered, and the one survivor came from the industry furthest from the AI discourse — the tort system, which appears in zero of YC's 13 requests and in none of the 140 prior ledger entries.

Two operating rules come out of it, both cheap:
1. **RFS categories are pre-saturated.** If the structural half of a cross is on the current YC RFS, thousands of founders are reading the same prompt at the same moment. Use the RFS as an exclusion list, not an idea list.
2. **Lead with the capability, choose the industry for distance.** Every drought run picked the industry first (data centres, insurance, defence) and looked for a capability. This run picked the two freshest capabilities and deliberately searched for industries with no AI-native incumbent. That ordering is what produced the survivor.

Held-book status is unchanged and current — ts-0048 and ts-0032 remain the two commit candidates from the 07-25 and 07-28 runs, both briefed, both needing a conversation rather than more desk research. Nothing this run weakens either. ts-0133 now joins them as a third, and it is the highest-scoring idea in the ledger.
