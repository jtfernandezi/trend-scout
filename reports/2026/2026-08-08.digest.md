**Trend Scout — 2026-08-08**

**0 survivors, 9 kills.** Third zero-survivor run in a row.

This run was the test the last report asked for: stop hunting new *topics*, change the *kind of company*. All nine ideas were consumer products, marketplaces, or businesses you own outright rather than software you sell — the one shape the project had never tried, chosen because "some vendor already sells this" shouldn't apply there.

It didn't apply. Something else did. Five of the nine were killed by a venture-funded startup already doing the exact same thing, two by the big platforms (Apple, Google, OpenAI) who get the needed data for free just by sitting in the middle, one by the fact that buying businesses is a contest between people with money rather than people with ideas, and one by economics that have already failed twice before. Changing the kind of company changed who beats you to it, not whether someone has.

The most useful kill: I reasoned from scratch — no wealth-management input at all — to "be the company that administers family trusts, using AI to do the patient monitoring a bank charges 1% a year to do badly." That is a genuinely huge market, over $100 trillion changing hands. A company called Axiom Trust came out of stealth five months ago with $11.8M from Lightspeed calling itself "the first AI-native trust company." Same words. That's the finding: independent reasoning now lands on the same squares as everyone else within months, whichever road you take to get there.

One piece of good news for an idea already on the shelf: the insurance-against-correlated-AI-failure concept got stronger evidence for free. Attacks that hijack AI agents are up 340% in a year and remain unsolved, and 88% of companies have had an agent security incident — the losses this idea is built to price are now measured, not predicted.

Recommendation stands for the fourth run running: stop scanning, and make one phone call to a real buyer about the two ideas already written up.
