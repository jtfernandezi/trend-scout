# Brief 0023 — Fix the chair first: the AI-native complex rehab and home mobility company

**Ledger id:** ts-0283 · **Date:** 2026-08-29 · **Composite:** 4.2 (rubric v3)
**Status:** new

---

## 1. The one-sentence version

Enter the complex rehab technology market through the repair backlog the private-equity-owned duopoly
is structurally unwilling to serve — remote multimodal fault diagnosis and part identification across
a mixed-vintage, multi-brand installed base — and use the earned patient relationship to take the
new-chair sale, reconstructing the user's seated posture and function from consumer video,
generatively designing the seating and mobility configuration, and additively manufacturing it instead
of assembling someone else's catalogue.

## 2. Why this, why now

**The pattern.** Three companies funded inside twelve months at the same job — collapse a
craft-labour-bound, insurance-reimbursed custom device category with AI-driven design plus additive
manufacture, and capture the value by owning the whole stack including the claim:

- **Hike Medical** — $22.5M across seed and Series A (reported 2026-08-25); an integrated orthotics
  and prosthetics system explicitly combining software, insurance and 3D printing, naming a $100B
  addressable market and "tens of millions of Americans."
- **CustoMED** — $6M to scale AI-powered 3D-printed solutions for orthopaedic surgery.
- **Kalogon** — $5.75M led by Enable Ventures in June 2026, shipping a **Medicare-coded** 3D-printed
  variable-density lattice seating line (the Verro Lattice) alongside a dual-density foam model.

Kalogon matters most, and not for the reason it looks. Kalogon distributes **through** National
Seating & Mobility, Numotion, Reliable Medical and Paragon Mobility. The best AI-native player in
custom seating took the *supplier* position under the incumbents rather than the operator position
against them. That is the market telling you which chair is still empty.

**The second pattern, for the money half.** The 2026 autonomous-RCM cluster proves that a model
holding a payer's entire rejection rulebook and repairing the submission before it is sent is a
compounding business: Candid Health's $120M Series D on 2026-07-22 (Sixth Street Growth, tripling its
2025 valuation) on **190% YoY contracted run-rate growth**, **180% net dollar retention**, 200+
provider organisations, 41,000 rules orchestrated hourly and customers reporting 96.7% touchless
claims — inside more than **$500M** raised for AI revenue-cycle management in 2026 (Commure $70M,
Adonis $40M, Anomaly $17M, Amperos $16M). In complex rehab this is not a side feature. The
medical-necessity letter, the face-to-face encounter note and the repair justification *are* the
revenue event.

**The wound.** Delivery of a custom power chair runs two to five months. Roughly a third of prescribed
mobility and participation devices are not delivered before discharge. Repairs are worse: routine
multi-month waits, and 7-21 days consumed by Medicare Advantage paperwork approval alone before any
part is ordered. This is politically live — Rep. Pressley introduced legislation specifically to
streamline wheelchair repair services in June 2026 — and it has been the subject of national reporting
since STAT's 2024 investigation into repair times after private equity consolidated the sector.

**What changed technically.** Every hard part of this failed at consumer capture quality until
recently and now does not: identifying a failed component and the exact replacement part on a
nine-year-old chair with no VIN system, no standard parts catalogue and six manufacturers, from a
user's phone photos plus a controller error log; reconstructing pelvic and trunk alignment,
anthropometry, pressure distribution and functional reach from ordinary video plus the clinical
record; generatively designing seating geometry that satisfies clinical, anatomical **and**
manufacturability constraints simultaneously; and writing payer documentation that survives audit.

## 3. The beachhead

**Buyer.** The power-wheelchair user or their caregiver — an adult with a spinal cord injury, multiple
sclerosis, ALS, muscular dystrophy or advanced cerebral palsy who depends on a Group 3 power chair.
The payer (Medicare, Medicare Advantage, Medicaid or commercial) is billed behind them, as it already
is today; this does not require inventing a new payment source.

**Trigger.** The chair breaks and the user is immobilised at home — dead drive motor, failed joystick
or controller, torn sling, a battery that will not hold — and the incumbent quotes weeks for an
assessment visit before the paperwork even begins. Secondary trigger: the five-year Medicare
replacement window opens, or a rehab hospital discharge planner needs a chair configured before a
patient leaves inpatient rehab.

**Why repair is the right wedge and not a distraction.** It is urgent (the buyer is stranded), it is
already funded (repairs are a covered benefit), it is unserved (no startup found doing it), it is the
cheapest possible customer acquisition for a $15,000-$45,000 device sale, and it is the half the
incumbent is *least* willing to defend, because defending it means defending a P&L line they would
rather not have.

## 4. Why the leader structurally cannot follow

**Numotion** operates 150 branches with 500+ ATPs and 1,000+ service technicians; **National Seating &
Mobility** runs a comparable nationwide field organisation. Both are private-equity-owned. To serve
this segment they would have to reverse three things at once:

1. **Abandon per-visit field labour revenue.** Their service P&L is billed against technician
   utilisation. Remote diagnosis and self-fit kits destroy the utilisation of the asset they own.
2. **Become a manufacturer against their own supplier margin.** Their gross margin on equipment is the
   distribution spread and the rebate on Permobil, Quantum and Sunrise product. Making their own
   additively-manufactured seating attacks the supplier relationships that are their P&L. This is
   textbook channel conflict, not inattention.
3. **Rebuild intake around consumer capture.** Their entire clinical process assumes an ATP in the
   room with a tape measure.

Leverage makes this strictly harder. A levered, PE-owned distributor cannot authorise deliberate
cannibalisation of two revenue lines simultaneously on a sponsor's hold-period clock.

**The manufacturers cannot follow either.** Permobil already ships connected-chair remote diagnostics
via MyPermobil — battery level, predicted range, seating data, service telemetry. But it reaches only
Permobil's own newer chairs, which is a fraction of the installed base, and Permobil sells through
Numotion and NSM. Going direct on service or on the sale would detonate its distribution.

**The AI-native players are suppliers, not rivals.** Kalogon sells its Medicare-coded 3D-printed
cushions through Numotion, NSM, Reliable Medical and Paragon. Matrix Seating USA and CREATech sit in
the same position. Hike Medical and CustoMED are one device class over, with different codes, a
different credentialed clinician (CPO rather than ATP) and a different fabrication process.

## 5. The $1B path

| Stage | Market | Why it opens |
|---|---|---|
| Repair on the CRT installed base | ~$0.5-1B US service spend | Urgent, covered, unserved; earns the patient |
| Custom chair and seating sale | US CRT $5.27B (2024) → $8.26B (2034) | The relationship earned in repair converts the replacement |
| All custom-fitted mobility and seating | Adjacent custom device categories | Same capture, design and fabrication stack |
| The home medical equipment base | ~$60B US; global medical equipment repair services $44B (2024) → $90B (2032) | Every incumbent is a low-margin distributor of someone else's catalogue |

The honest read: this is not an obviously massive market at the beachhead and the core CRT category
compounds at only ~4.6% a year. It scores 4 on venture scale, not 5. The $1B story rests on the
service layer of the wider home equipment base, which is large, fragmented and growing much faster
than CRT itself.

## 6. Reaching the first ten

These buyers congregate more visibly than almost any other patient population, because immobility is
a shared political grievance rather than a private one.

- **Spinal cord injury model systems and rehab hospitals** — Shepherd Center, Craig Hospital, Kessler,
  Shirley Ryan AbilityLab. Discharge planners have no provider they can promise a date to and will
  take a call.
- **Advocacy organisations** — United Spinal Association, the Christopher & Dana Reeve Foundation, the
  ALS Association, the Muscular Dystrophy Association. The repair-delay campaign is already organised
  and has congressional attention as of June 2026.
- **Seating clinics** — hospital-based seating and mobility clinics refer every CRT evaluation and
  currently watch their referrals disappear into a months-long queue.
- **The users themselves** — r/wheelchairs and disability communities discuss repair waits
  continuously and in public.

The first ten are repairs, not chairs. Ten fast repairs bought at a loss are worth more than any
pitch deck to a seating clinic.

## 7. What has to be true

1. **The multimodal diagnosis actually works on the installed base**, not on a curated set of newer
   chairs. This is the technical crux and it must be proven on the ugliest possible sample — old
   chairs, discontinued manufacturers, bad photos, no logs.
2. **A generalist technician plus a correctly identified part beats an ATP visit** for the majority of
   failures. If most repairs genuinely require a credentialed professional in the room, the wedge is
   much narrower.
3. **Medicare and Medicare Advantage will pay a new provider for repairs at a rate that supports
   same-week service.** Reimbursement is capped; the margin has to come from removing the truck roll
   and the paperwork cycle, not from a better rate.
4. **The repair relationship converts to the chair sale.** Untested. If it does not convert, this is a
   good service business inside a small market rather than a venture outcome.
5. **Regulatory footing.** Group 3 power chairs require in-person ATP involvement under Medicare.
   The company must employ ATPs and make each 5-10x more productive — this is a labour-multiplication
   story, not a labour-elimination story. Anyone pitching it as the latter is pitching something that
   is not allowed.

## 8. Biggest risks, stated plainly

- **It is an operations company with a software core, not the reverse.** Trucks, technicians,
  inventory, a fabrication supply chain and a payer-facing back office. Margins are earned by
  execution.
- **Hike Medical is one device class away with $22.5M raised.** If the underlying capability is
  general, complex rehab may be the second market Hike enters rather than a company started fresh.
  This is the single most important thing to check before committing.
- **The market is reimbursement-capped and slow-growing.** Every dollar of value created has to be
  taken out of the incumbent's cost structure, because the payer will not pay more.
- **The AI-native gate is the weakest of the three.** It passes — consumer-capture body reconstruction
  and part identification across an unstandardised installed base genuinely need frontier multimodal
  models — but it is a defensible pass rather than an overwhelming one, and it should be re-tested
  after the first fifty repairs.
